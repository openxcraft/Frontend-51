## Category overview

The stamp-card loyalty category is a crowded, low-priced, English-first tools market sitting on top of a behavioral machine (paper punch cards) that MENA local retail never stopped using. Three classes compete for the local retailer. **Standalone digital stamp-card platforms** (Stamp Me, Loopy Loyalty, CandyBar-legacy, LoyaltyPass, the freemium wave — FaveCard, Stampy, Passtastic, Stampeo, Brewstamp) sell wallet passes and a staff-side stamper app; pricing clusters at $25–$99/month with free-forever floors [1][3][6][31][32][33]. **POS-locked loyalty add-ons** (Square Loyalty class at $45–49/month) bundle loyalty to a register the independent bakery or laundry does not run [2][8]. **POS suites with built-in loyalty** (Loyverse-class free tier) give the register away and treat loyalty as a feature of it [1][7]. In the Gulf, a thin regional class (Sharaf Tona, Niqati) finally gets the language right — bilingual Arabic/English wallet cards for UAE/KSA merchants [10][13] — but is wallet-delivery-shaped, documents no staff-verified stamp governance, and no bilingual reward-tier authoring; and above everything towers the enterprise loyalty conversation (Shukran, Amber, MyClub — mall and chain scale) [27] that the corner store was never the buyer for. The wedge this product occupies: the counter itself. Staff-verified stamping as an enforced, audited, deterministic flow — plus bilingual reward tiers as authored data rather than a translated afterthought — is absent from every retrieved offering, while the fraud doctrine the category itself publishes ("close the opportunities inside the program construct with business rules", sources 19–23) is exactly a spec for this construct.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **The paper punch card itself** (the true incumbent): free, instant, everyone understands it — and fraud-prone (the borrowed marker, the friendly stamp), unmeasured, and lost weekly. Practitioner threads confirm staff tolerate it while distrusting its integrity (paraphrased sentiment, source 17). We formalize its one virtue — the counter moment — and nothing else.
- **Enterprise Gulf loyalty ecosystems** (Shukran/Amber/MyClub class, source 27): own the mall-and-chain conversation; the independent retailer is not their buyer, and their app-scale enrollment teaches nothing about a two-second counter interaction.
- **POS-first register stacks with loyalty features** (Rezku/Toki/SalesVu guidance class, sources 50–54): sell identification-at-register in under 3 seconds (source 51) — the right speed instinct welded to the wrong premise for a store that will not change registers. We adopt the sub-3-second identification budget as our performance bar and drop the register.
- **Cards-free habit: the owner's memory** ("the regulars, I know them"): zero-cost, zero-audit, zero-transfer — and it dies with the shift. The deterministic member record is the honest replacement.

## Market & demand evidence (with sources; claims marked)

- **The category pays monthly at SMB scale**: paid stamp-card tools cluster $25–$99/mo, POS-locked add-ons $45–49/mo, premium $199+/mo (official pricing pages and listings above) — a local retailer's existing budget line for exactly this job [2][3][6][31][32].
- **Regional demand is real and growing**: Middle East loyalty programs market projected +16.3% in 2025 to $3.27B (research-databook projection — market-sizing claim, marked, not audited) [42]; GCC loyalty environment characterized by high mobile usage, strong app adoption, growing digital payments [41]; 28% of consumers signed up for supermarket loyalty programs and 17% in coffee-shop/restaurant-class programs in MEA engagement data (panel claim, marked) [43].
- **Effectiveness claims circulate but are unaudited** (vendor/industry claims, marked; never used as product promises): structured programs → "30% higher repeat purchase rate" and "20–40% visit-frequency lift" [37]; "3x repeat visits" [39]; loyalty programs "play a role in 76%" of brand relationships [40]; frequency-reward mechanics accelerate habitual behavior [38]. The spec's own success signals are behavioral (03/07c), not percentages — the portfolio's zero-invention discipline.
- **Structure knowledge is practitioner-solid**: 8–10 stamps is the recommended range for a free-item reward [46][58]; reward should be reachable in 4–6 weeks, with cafe regulars visiting roughly every 4 days [47]; an 8-stamp card is claimed the "most effective" structure at an 11.4% claimed completion rate (vendor claim, marked, unverified) [45]. Multi-tier/mid-card rewards have documented psychology attention (endowed progress and the two-reward punch-card experiments, journalistic account [15]).
- **Fraud is a named, persistent category problem**: three actor classes — customers, internal staff, external abusers [19]; prevention belongs in the program construct as business rules [20]; "rising fast, costing billions" [21]; "acquiring or redeeming rewards not earned" as the canonical definition [22]; a three-type taxonomy [23]. None of the retrieved SMB tools documents an enforced staff-verification construct — the doctrine exists, the product doesn't.
- **Bilingual retail is the regional floor, not a feature**: Arabic-first retail forms including loyalty enrolment [25]; dual-script signage conventions with layout mirroring and translation QA [24][28]; the Saudi shop-sign linguistic landscape is documented academically [26]. The regional wallet tools confirm bilingual cards exist [10][13] — but none documents bilingual tier authoring (honest gap recorded in the digest).

## Pricing landscape

Free-forever floors with limits (FaveCard/Passtastic-class [1][12]) · standalone paid $25–$99/mo (Loopy $25–95 [32][33]; Stamp Me $49–199 [3]; LoyaltyPass $99 flat [6]; CandyBar $45 legacy [57]) · POS-locked $45–49/mo (Square class [2]) · premium $199+ [3]. Our posture (12 Q-3): **flat per-store monthly at the category's entry band, staff seats included, members and stamps never metered** — stamps and redemptions are the behavior being bought; rationing them is the category's worst instinct, and the deterministic console (no model spend, no message volume) has no cost basis for metering anyway. Export artifacts unmetered within policy.

## Positioning statement

For local retailers in MENA neighborhoods, loyalty-stamp-card is the loyalty console that keeps the stamp at the counter: members join by scanning a QR and carry a bilingual pass, but only authenticated staff can stamp — every stamp, undo, adjustment, and redemption is an immutable, attributable ledger entry, every correction is reason-coded behind a manager gate, and every reward tier is authored in Arabic and English side by side. Unlike wallet-pass platforms, it is governance-shaped rather than delivery-shaped; unlike POS add-ons, it presumes no register; unlike paper, it answers "who stamped this and when" in two clicks.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns, marked)

- Owner sentiment on paper (practitioner threads, paraphrased, source 17): stamp integrity is unenforceable — the marker is reachable by everyone, and "friendly" stamps are invisible losses.
- Staff sentiment (barista/reddit threads, paraphrased, sources 17 and q2): loyalty rituals that slow the line get skipped under rush — the counter flow must be one lookup and one tap, or it dies at peak.
- SMB-tool sentiment (comparison threads, paraphrased, sources 1/5/9): free tiers meter the things being bought (cards, stamps, members), and single-locale tooling makes Arabic reward names a workaround (hand-written translations on printed cards).
- Regional practitioner sentiment (LinkedIn critique, paraphrase-marked, source 44): loyalty measured at single-store granularity mislabels customers — context for why we hold the multi-store boundary explicitly rather than drifting into it.

## Market size (honest)

Local independent retailers across GCC/North-Arabic markets running (or tolerating) paper stamp cards — the long tail under the $3.27B regional loyalty projection (a projection, not an audited addressable figure; the local-retail slice of it has no published TAM — any number would be invented, so none is offered). Envelope: flat per-store SaaS at the category's proven $25–$99 entry band, one workspace per store, unlimited members — the same shape the region's coffee shops already pay for stamp tooling, minus the register lock-in.
