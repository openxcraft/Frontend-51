# 00 — Brief: loyalty-stamp-card

Family: commerce_integrations · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A bilingual (ar/en) loyalty console for local retailers — the corner cafe, the neighborhood bakery, the juice bar, the laundry, the minimarket — that replaces the paper stamp card without giving up the one thing paper does well: the stamp happens at the counter, in front of a human, after a real purchase. The product's spine is the **staff-verified stamp flow**: a member shows their pass (QR code on their phone, or a short code typed by staff, or a phone-number lookup); the counter surface resolves their card in seconds; the staff member taps once; the stamp lands on the card with an immutable ledger entry recording who stamped, when, and for which member. Members can join and view their own card freely, but they can never stamp it themselves — the anti-fraud posture the research corpus names explicitly (loyalty fraud is perpetrated by customers, staff, and outsiders alike; the program construct itself is the prevention surface, sources 19–23 in the research digest). Every correction is a reason-coded, manager-gated adjustment that survives in the audit ledger, so "my friend behind the counter stamped my card" stops being an invisible loss and becomes a visible, attributable event.

The second pillar is the **bilingual reward-tier system**. A loyalty card is authored once in the Card Studio: how many stamps fill the card, which redemption mode it runs (classic single-reward, or a tiered ladder where a mid-card reward and a top-card reward coexist), and — the part no retrieved competitor treats as a first-class construct — every reward tier carries both an Arabic and an English name, authored side by side, validated so neither locale can be left blank. The member's pass renders tiers in the member's own locale with the other script a glance away; the counter surface shows staff the reward line exactly as it will be spoken aloud at the register, in the language of the moment ("قهوة على حسابنا" or "any coffee on the house"). Card expiry, daily stamp caps, and undo windows are deterministic policy data, not vibes: the same card state always yields the same eligibility answer, which is exactly what a counter-speed surface requires.

The console around these two pillars is deliberately small and honest: a stamper surface built for two-second interactions, a members ledger with normalized bilingual search, a member timeline, deterministic insights (stamps issued, redemptions, tier mix, adjustment counts — counts with their arithmetic disclosed, never a scored judgment), a staff-and-audit surface where suspicious patterns surface as threshold flags rather than model verdicts, and an export path for the member list. This is a **declared non-AI product** (ai-native-patterns.md §1): every operation — stamping, redemption, adjustment, expiry, insights — is a deterministic rule over recorded state; no operation generates, scores, or transforms content by model, and the specs leak no AI affordances.

## Buyer

Local independent retailers in MENA neighborhoods: the owner-operator of a coffee shop or bakery with 2–8 staff across shifts, the laundry with one counter, the minimarket with two registers. The economic buyer is the owner who today runs paper punch cards (and knows the marker lives under the counter where anyone can reach it) or runs nothing at all (01 evidence: the incumbent is paper, rubber stamp, and memory). Daily users split three ways: the **counter staff** whose whole job on this surface is to not slow the line (one lookup, one tap, next customer); the **shift manager** who owns program edits, adjustments, and the audit ledger; and the **members** themselves — transient participants, like the visitors of the front-desk products, who enroll by scanning a counter QR and carry a pass on their phone but never hold an account on the merchant side. One workspace per store; staff seats are few; members are unlimited and never a billing surface. Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Responsive web console, desktop-first (≥1280px, keyboard-first stamper: search, Enter, one-key stamp), with a first-class **counter tablet view** (768–1024px touch, ≥56px one-thumb targets for the stamp and redeem actions) and a **member pass view** (phone-first ≥360px, token-keyed, read-only, renders the wallet-style card, stamp progress, tier ladder, and the member's own QR). Zones: console home (today at the counter), the stamper, card studio (with live dual-direction card preview), programs list, members ledger, member detail with timeline, insights, staff & audit, settings, command palette. No native app at v1 (the pass is a web view); no public API at v1; no POS hardware of any kind (LOOP-10 — the sale event arrives through an abstract capability or is entered by staff).

## Unique feature

Staff-verified stamp flows with bilingual reward tiers — one deterministic loop: enroll (self-scan or counter entry) → pass (token-keyed card view with QR) → stamp (staff-verified, capped, ledgered) → tier redemption (bilingual reward line, ladder or classic semantics) → audit (reason-coded adjustments, threshold flags, immutable entries). Defensible on three grounds (01 evidence): (1) the stamp-card category's fraud conversation is real and its published prevention doctrine is "business rules in the program construct" (sources 19–23) — yet no retrieved small-retail tool ships the construct: staff-only stamping, daily caps, reason-coded adjustments, and an attributable audit ledger as one enforced system; (2) the regional wallet-loyalty tools that are bilingual (source 10) market bilingual cards but not bilingual reward-tier authoring, and the POS-locked Western leaders (sources 2, 8) are single-locale-first with loyalty as an add-on to a register the local bakery does not run; (3) the entire loop is declared non-AI and deterministic — the same state always answers the same way, which is both the counter-speed requirement and the dispute answer ("who stamped this, when, at which register") that paper could never give.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No payments: no gateway, no stored cards, no points-to-cash conversion, no buying stamps, no paid tiers of membership. The product never touches money; it records visits and rewards.
- No marketing automation: no campaigns, no broadcast messaging, no sequences, no quiet-hours-aware persuasion ladders (the abandoned-cart family owns that artifact). Member notification is transactional only — reward-ready at the stamp moment and expiry warnings — and every notification is a recorded ledger event.
- No franchise hierarchies: one workspace per store, no parent-child org trees, no cross-store point pooling, no chain rollups (the store-performance family explicitly excludes loyalty data joins; the boundary is recorded in 12).
- No POS hardware or POS replacement: sale events arrive through an abstract POS-transaction capability or manual staff entry (LOOP-5); the console is never the register.
- No self-stamping by members, ever: the pass is read-only; stamps originate only from authenticated staff sessions.
- No gamification engine: no badges-for-badges, no streak mechanics, no points currency, no levels beyond the reward-tier ladder the merchant authors.
- No AI anywhere (declared non-AI): no fraud "detection models", no churn scores, no generated reward copy — deterministic rules and counts only.

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

An owner can, in one first session on the Mock Adapter with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI: open the Card Studio and author a ladder card (8 stamps, a 4-stamp mid tier and an 8-stamp final tier, each tier named in both Arabic and English — validation rejects a blank locale), preview the card face in both directions, and publish it; enroll a walk-in member at the counter with name, phone, and locale; resolve that member by QR code, by short code, and by phone lookup; stamp their card once and see the ledger entry with actor and timestamp; attempt a second same-day stamp and meet the daily-cap validation with the policy and the stamping staff named; redeem the mid tier on a seeded card and read the bilingual reward line; make a manager-gated adjustment with a reason code and find it in the audit ledger with the suspicious-pattern threshold flags computed; open the member pass view on the token and see the card, tiers, and QR render in the member's locale; and export the member list before artifact expiry — with every abstract capability unbound (no POS feed: staff stamp manually; no wallet delivery: the pass renders in-browser with a shareable link and printable QR; no notification channel: reward-ready renders on the counter surface and the pass itself). The whole loop replays deterministically twice with byte-identical state.
