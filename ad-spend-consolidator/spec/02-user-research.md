# 02 — User Research: ad-spend-consolidator

SME-B pass · Paraphrased sentiment only, marked (paraphrase) — zero verbatim quotes; personas synthesized from the research digest's vendor-page and calendar sources (research/batch10/ad-spend-consolidator-q1…q25) and portfolio buyer patterns. Personas use regional names; no real individuals are represented.

## Persona 1 — Huda, Head of Marketing Operations (primary buyer)

**Role**: runs a 4-person marketing-ops pod inside Nuqta Retail Group, a Riyadh-headquartered multi-brand beauty and home-goods retailer selling across KSA, UAE, and Egypt. Six buying channels, three currencies, one CFO conversation per month. She owns the consolidated spend number: nobody else can say what marketing cost in total last month without a spreadsheet evening.
**JTBD**: "When the month closes, I want one spend number per market and per moment — normalized, deduplicated, and versioned — so the CFO's reconciliation and my pacing story come from the same ledger I read all month."
**Triggers**: a finance reconciliation that found the agency's September number double-counting the marketplace's sponsored spend (the overlap trap); a Ramadan retrospective that compared a Feb–Mar window this year against a Mar–Apr window last year and called the campaign a failure — the windows were not the same retail moment (the lunar migration problem, digest Sources 7–8); a channel export that arrived with revised figures a week after she had already presented them (the upstream-revision reality, digest Source 12).
**Objections + responses**: "We already export everything to a dashboard tool" → the dashboard renders what the pipeline delivers; FX policy, dedupe, and calendar comparisons still live in her head and her workbook (01 indirect competitors). "Another seat-heavy platform?" → operator-seat sidecar pricing, viewer seats free for finance (12 Q-3); the $300–1,118/mo tier is what this replaces for her team's actual need (01 pricing). "Will it replace our channel consoles?" → read-only by design: the product never writes back to a channel (LOOP-10, no bid automation).
**Pains**: the consolidated number takes an analyst-evening per month to assemble and is stale the moment an upstream revision lands (paraphrase of the category's own "reporting that doesn't break" positioning — the vendors sell the pipeline, she needs the ledger); regional moments renamed per market ("White Friday here, Yellow Friday there" — the per-market naming reality, digest Source 9) make her monthly narrative a manual translation exercise.

## Persona 2 — Turki, Marketing Data Analyst (primary daily user)

**Role**: the person who actually pulls the exports. Five channel consoles, three currencies, weekly and monthly cadences that never align; today he lands them in one workbook, hand-applies FX multipliers someone emailed him last quarter, un-does the agency double-count by memory, and rebuilds the pivot every month.
**JTBD**: "When channel exports land, I want to import them once and have the normalization, currency conversion, and overlap flags computed for me — with the receipts — so my month-end is an hour of judgment, not an evening of arithmetic."
**Triggers**: an FX multiplier in his workbook that two people edited differently in the same quarter (no declared policy, no receipt); an agency report whose totals matched the marketplace console to within a rounding — and were counted twice (the overlap trap); a revised platform figure that made last month's already-presented total quietly wrong (he found out from finance, not the platform).
**Objections + responses**: "I can do all this in the workbook" → the workbook cannot version a snapshot, show a revision delta, or compute a Ramadan occurrence window by lunar reckoning — and it silently forgets every dedupe decision (07a state obligations). "Keyboard-first or I won't use it daily" → full keyboard traversal of timeline, breakdown, and reconciliation surfaces (09). "Auto-dedupe will eat real spend" → dedupe only ever *flags*; every resolution is a recorded human decision with a reason (03 CP-3).
**Pains**: hand-maintained FX tables with no provenance; re-doing the same reconciliation every cycle because the workbook has no memory; explaining the number to finance with no receipts (paraphrase of the reconciliation pain the category's monitoring features gesture at — 01 q3).

## Persona 3 — Nouf, Regional Marketing Manager — KSA / UAE / Egypt (Arabic-first reader)

**Role**: owns the regional plan and the moment calendar. Reads the dashboard weekly in Arabic-first chrome; author of the Ramadan and White/Yellow Friday plans; asks the comparative questions ("did Yellow Friday beat last year's Yellow Friday? did Ramadan spend pace the way we planned?") and turns the answers into next quarter's plan.
**JTBD**: "When a retail moment ends, I want its like-for-like read — this occurrence against the same moment's prior occurrence and against a normal-period baseline — in both languages, so my plan review works in the language my leadership reads."
**Triggers**: a year-over-year chart whose "Ramadan this year" and "Ramadan last year" bars were 30 days apart in Gregorian terms and told her nothing about the moment itself (the lunar migration, digest Source 7); the same shopping weekend appearing under three different names across her three markets (digest Source 9); planning docs that say "late November" while the moment library in the product says exactly which four days, per market.
**Objections + responses**: "Every tool is English-first and I adapt" → bilingual-en with full Arabic chrome as a first-class citizen: channel data stays Latin (campaign names, currency codes), chrome and moments render per locale (05 direction model; 09 verification matrix). "Is the Hijri conversion right?" → the method is declared in-product per moment (Umm al-Qura presentation for KSA contexts, the portfolio default), and the disclosure is one glance away (P24 discipline). "I don't want to become a data engineer" → overlay authoring is policy data with bilingual names and anchor rules — no query builder (03 CP-4).
**Pains**: fixed-window comparisons that misrepresent migrating moments; moment names that change per market and per language; overlay logic living in her slide notes instead of the product (paraphrase of the calendar-structure evidence — 01 evidence blocks 3–4).

## Persona 4 — Adel, Finance Controller / FP&A Partner (viewer seat)

**Role**: receives the marketing number, reconciles it against invoices and the media agency's statement, and challenges anything without evidence. He will never author an overlay; he will absolutely open a conversion receipt.
**JTBD**: "When marketing hands me their spend number, I want the reconciliation ledger behind it — every conversion receipt, every overlap resolution with its reason, every revision delta — so my challenge process is reading, not re-doing."
**Triggers**: an invoice in AED reconciled against a report in SAR with no stated rate; a month where marketing's total included the same spend twice; a revised figure that made an already-locked month total move with no explanation (the upstream-revision reality, digest Source 12).
**Objections + responses**: "I only trust what I can recompute" → every receipt shows native amount, rate, basis, and policy version (07c rules 2–3); snapshots are immutable, so the number he reconciled last month is still the number (07a). "Do I need a paid seat to audit?" → viewer seat: read the ledger, the receipts, and the deltas; export the pack (07b Session role 'viewer'). "Where do the FX rates come from?" → the table declares itself workspace policy data with declared basis — never presented as a market feed (08 N3, 11 copy).
**Pains**: re-deriving marketing's arithmetic every close; month totals that mutate after reconciliation; no audit trail for dedupe decisions (paraphrase of the governance posture the enterprise vendors sell as "governed layer" — 01 q19).

## Persona 5 — Lina, Channel Manager (connection owner)

**Role**: owns the buying-channel relationships and the connection health: cadences, credentials, and the metric-shape knowledge ("this console reports spend by spend-date, that one by report-date"). She is the person who notices a connection has gone stale.
**JTBD**: "When a channel's reports change or stall, I want the connection's health, cadence, and policy visible in one place — so the ledger never silently goes stale and I never discover a three-week gap at month-end."
**Triggers**: an agency report that arrived twelve days late and left the month partial (cadence reality); a credential that failed silently while the workbook kept looking normal (credential-contract §3 posture); a metric-shape change at a channel that shifted a week's spend into the wrong days.
**Objections + responses**: "Connections are IT's job" → kind-not-vendor language, consent-gated capability, and a paste path that always works (08 N1) — the product never requires IT to be useful. "What happens when I disconnect one?" → revoked connection degrades explicitly: its line items and snapshots stay, freshness flags name it (08 degradation map). "Will it touch the channel?" → read-only capability class, grant display says exactly what it reads (credential-contract §4).
**Pains**: freshness discovered too late; health living in nobody's surface; policy knowledge (cadence, metric shape) living in her head (paraphrase of the "nothing slips through" promise the category sells as alerts — 01 q3 — here it is the connection's own state surface).

## Voice derivation (SME-J)

Operator-desk voice: **plain, calendar-aware, receipt-honest** — states what was spent, in which currency, at which rate, over which window, and what the human decided; never editorializes about campaign quality; never forecasts beyond declared budget lines; never presents declared policy data as market truth. Arabic register فصحى بيضاء (utility precision surface); English professional-neutral, contractions off (governance-adjacent analytics family).
