## Category overview

The category the vendors call marketing-data consolidation / marketing intelligence exists, prices high, and names its own founding pain on its own pages: "Automate reporting — Fast, accurate reporting that doesn't break" (Funnel homepage, q17); "Reporting operations — Never sent broken" and "Monitoring & alerting — Nothing slips through" (Whatagraph pricing, q3); "brings all your cross-channel marketing data on one governed layer" (Whatagraph homepage, q19); "Connect all your marketing data", "Ensure data quality and accuracy" (Adverity pricing, q22). The product class these vendors sell is broad: connectors-in, destinations-out, pipelines, storage, AI layers — Supermetrics titles itself "AI-first Marketing Intelligence Platform" (q16); Improvado prices against "Ad spend per year ($0–$300M)" and sells MMM/incrementality add-ons (q4); NinjaCat repositioned as "The AI Agent Platform Built for Multi-Account Marketing" (q24). None of the retrieved pages carries a MENA retail-calendar feature, a regional-holiday overlay, or a currency-normalization posture: Funnel's page lists "Context: budgets, targets and business goals" (q2) but nothing Hijri; Supermetrics' page lists destinations and AI credits (q16) but no FX policy; the one pricing page that mentions currency at all is AgencyAnalytics' billing-currency selector ("USD, CAD, AUD, NZD, GBP and EUR… Pricing and available plans may vary by region", q23) — billing currency, not spend normalization. The gap this product occupies is therefore not "another consolidation platform": it is the narrow, regional, honesty-first spend ledger — one declared reporting currency, receipted conversions, inspectable dedupe, versioned snapshots, and a MENA overlay engine — sold to a marketing-ops buyer the breadth platforms price out of their top tiers (Supermetrics Growth at $199/mo covers 7 data sources for 2 users, q16; Whatagraph starts at $1,118/mo, q3).

Demand is structural rather than speculative: the buyer's market runs at effectively total digital adoption — Saudi Arabia at 99.0% internet penetration, 99.6% social-media identity penetration, 140% mobile connections (DataReportal Digital 2025: Saudi Arabia, q13); globally 73.8% of the world's population uses the internet and 69.9% holds social identities (DataReportal global overview, q14). Ad-driven retail in that market runs on the lunar calendar's retail moments — Ramadan "lasts 29 to 30 days, from one sighting of the crescent moon to the next" and "migrates throughout the seasons" (Wikipedia, q9); the Black-Friday-aligned shopping weekend carries different names per MENA market — "Black Friday started as White Friday campaign in 2014. In 2018 local e-commerce platform noon.com created Yellow Friday in the U.A.E." and "noon.com created Yellow Friday Sale which is now an annual event" in Saudi Arabia (Wikipedia, q8) — and platform-reported figures themselves get revised after the fact ("these platforms may revise or 'correct' the audience reach figures that they report in their advertising tools", DataReportal's own caveat, q13). Every one of those facts is a design requirement, not a style preference (→ 07a/07c).

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **The shared spreadsheet** — the true status quo every direct competitor implicitly fights: channel exports pasted into one workbook, a pivot per channel, FX applied by hand-edited multipliers, the Ramadan comparison done by eyeballing two screenshots. Free, universally understood, and structurally unable to carry a conversion receipt, an overlap resolution, or a versioned snapshot; the "which number is real" argument happens every month between marketing and finance (the consolidation trap the category's own marketing describes — q3/q17).
- **Free dashboard layers (Looker Studio class)** — named on Supermetrics' own pricing page as a destination (q16): free visualization over connectors someone else pays for. The dashboard renders whatever the pipeline delivers — FX policy, dedupe, and calendar logic all live unwritten between the export and the chart; the regional overlay work still happens in the analyst's head.
- **Native in-platform reporting consoles** — staying inside each channel's own console: numbers are correct per channel by construction, but the consolidated view does not exist; currencies, cadences, and metric shapes differ per console, and the month-end total is manual assembly. The posture costs zero and delivers exactly nothing this product's buyer is accountable for.
- **In-house warehouse + BI builds** — the engineering-heavy alternative the ETL vendors position against (Adverity ships a "Build vs Buy Calculator", q22): flexible and eventually powerful, but the marketing-ops buyer of a 5–20 person team has no data engineering bench; the calendar and FX policy still have to be authored by someone — and maintained when the lunar year drifts.

## Market and demand evidence (with sources)

1. **The category is priced and proven**: seven vendors with retrieved pricing pages spanning $20/client/month to $1,118/month with quote-based tiers above (Sources: q16 Supermetrics; q2 Funnel; q4 Improvado; q3 Whatagraph; q22 Adverity; q23 AgencyAnalytics; q25 ReportGarden). Vendor scale claims as printed: "200,000+ companies in 120 countries" (q16); "1,000+ marketing agencies and brands" (q3); "7,000+ agencies" (q23) — claims, marked as such.
2. **Digital adoption in the primary market is total**: KSA 99.0% internet / 99.6% social identities / 140% mobile connections (https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-saudi-arabia, q13); global 73.8% / 69.9% (https://datareportal.com/global-digital-overview, q14). The audience for ad-driven retail marketing exists at population scale.
3. **The MENA retail calendar is structural**: Ramadan is lunar, "10 to 11 days shorter" per solar year, and migrates through seasons (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan, q9; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_al-Qura_calendar, q21); the Islamic calendar renders dual-date in real-world convention (the retrieved page's own widget, q21). Fixed Gregorian year-over-year comparisons of Ramadan windows are arithmetically wrong — the like-for-like problem this product's overlay engine exists to solve.
4. **Regional shopping moments carry market-specific names**: White Friday in Egypt/Libya "due to religious, traditional and cultural concepts"; UAE White Friday since 2014 and Yellow Friday since 2018; Saudi Yellow Friday as an annual event (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Friday_(shopping), q8). An overlay library with per-market bilingual names is the honest data model, not a translation table.
5. **Upstream revision is a documented behavior of the source systems**: platform tools revising reported figures after the fact (DataReportal's caveat, q13) — grounds the immutable, versioned-snapshot design (07a state obligations).
6. **Currency conventions to lean on**: ISO 4217 canonical alphabetic codes with minor-unit fractions (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_4217, q10); the functional-currency concept — "the monetary unit of account of the principal economic environment in which an economic entity operates", with IAS/GAAP translation rules (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_currency, q20) — grounds one declared reporting currency per workspace with declared translation policy.
7. **Dual-calendar rendering is a real-world convention**: the retrieved Islamic-calendar page itself renders a live dual date (q21); the portfolio's own dual-calendar pattern (ux-patterns P24) and the seasonal-campaign-board / bilingual-content-calendar specs carry the same discipline — the overlay engine is the analytics-side member of that family of surfaces.

## Positioning statement

For MENA marketing operations teams accountable for a consolidated spend number across markets, currencies, and retail calendars, ad-spend-consolidator is the receipt-honest spend ledger: every channel's money normalized into one declared reporting currency with a conversion receipt on every figure, overlaps reconciled in the open with recorded reasons, snapshots versioned so upstream revisions never rewrite history finance already reconciled, and MENA retail moments — Ramadan by lunar occurrence, White Friday and Yellow Friday by market — overlaid on the daily timeline with like-for-like comparisons a fixed Gregorian tool cannot make. Unlike the pipeline-and-connector platforms (Supermetrics, Funnel, Improvado, Adverity) it does not sell breadth, storage, or an AI layer — it sells the one number and its evidence; unlike agency-reporting tools (Whatagraph, AgencyAnalytics, ReportGarden) its artifact is not a client-facing dashboard but the operator's reconciliation ledger; and unlike all of them, the regional calendar is the product's center of gravity, not a locale setting.
