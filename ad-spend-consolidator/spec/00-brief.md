## What it is

A spend consolidation dashboard for MENA marketing operations teams whose money is scattered across every channel they buy from. Marketing spend arrives here from **channel connections** — abstract, kind-not-vendor intake points ("search-ads-class", "social-ads-class", "marketplace-ads-class", "offline-media-class", "agency-report-class") each declaring its native currency, its reporting cadence, and its metric shape — or from the always-available manual path: pasted or uploaded spend line items, zero credentials required. Every line item lands raw and verbatim (native amount, native currency, spend date, report date, external key, campaign reference); the product's engine then runs a **consolidation**: deterministic normalization that converts every amount into the workspace's single declared reporting currency through a declared FX policy (per-day spend-date rate or monthly average — a policy, printed on every converted figure as a **conversion receipt**), resolves **overlaps** (the agency report and the marketplace console both claiming the same spend — detected by external key + day + amount-within-epsilon, never silently deduplicated), and produces a **versioned snapshot**. Snapshots are immutable: when an upstream channel revises its figures after the fact — a documented reality of platform-reported numbers (research digest, DataReportal caveat) — the revision lands as a new snapshot version with a named delta, and history never mutates.

On top of the normalized ledger sits the feature that has no equivalent in the retrieved category (01): **regional holiday overlays**. The workspace carries a library of MENA retail moments — Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha, White Friday, Yellow Friday, national days — authored bilingually (English and Arabic names per market: the same shopping weekend is White Friday in one market and Yellow Friday in another, per the retrieved record), each anchored either to a fixed Gregorian date or to the Hijri calendar with its conversion method declared in-product (Umm al-Qura presentation for KSA contexts, the portfolio's recorded default). Because Ramadan is lunar and migrates 10–11 days per solar year, fixed Gregorian windows cannot compare like-for-like across years; the overlay engine computes each moment's occurrence windows, then compares spend inside a window against the **same moment's prior occurrence** (lunar reckoning) and against an equal-length **control window** immediately before it — mean daily normalized spend, lift ratio, and the year-over-year like-for-like delta, all as deterministic arithmetic whose inputs and formulas are published in this spec (07c).

The product is deliberately **declared non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1): normalization, dedupe detection, overlay computation, and pacing are deterministic rules; there is no AI operation on the adapter, no model binding, and no AI affordance anywhere in the surface. It is equally deliberately narrow (LOOP-10): it consolidates and explains spend; it does not bid, it does not judge creatives, and it does not attribute — the "did spend cause the sale" question is explicitly out of scope, and the boundary with funnel-drop-off-explorer (which owns user-behavior exploration on the other side of that question) is recorded in both products' 12-open-questions (LOOP-3). The deliverable is a **report pack**: a consolidated statement, a reconciliation ledger (every conversion receipt, every overlap resolution with its reason, every revision delta), and overlay analyses — exported as an artifact with a declared expiry, never a silent channel write.

## Surface

Responsive web spend dashboard, desktop-first (≥1280px primary; the timeline and reconciliation tables are the speed surfaces) with a tablet review posture (≥820px) and a mobile check-in view (≥360px: totals, pacing status, job progress — no reconciliation editing on mobile). Zones: spend dashboard (period total, daily timeline with overlay bands, channel breakdown), channel connections, reconciliation console (overlap findings beside the raw line items that produced them), normalization & FX (policy + rate table with receipts), overlay library + overlay comparison, budgets & pacing, snapshots & revisions, report packs, jobs center, settings. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). Direction default **bilingual-en** (proposed, LOOP-6): English-first chrome — channel-native campaign names and currency codes are Latin-first data — with full Arabic chrome as a first-class citizen, because the regional manager and leadership layer read Arabic (02 persona split).

## Unique feature

**Cross-channel normalized spend with regional holiday overlays** — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the founding pain is the category's own marketing — "reporting that doesn't break" (Funnel), "Never sent broken" (Whatagraph) — but every retrieved vendor sells pipeline-and-connectors breadth; none ships a MENA retail-calendar overlay, and Ramadan's lunar migration makes every fixed-window year-over-year comparison structurally wrong (research digest, Sources 7–9); (2) currency normalization as a first-class, receipted surface — a single declared reporting currency (the functional-currency convention, Source 11) with the FX basis declared per workspace and every converted figure carrying its receipt — none of the retrieved pricing pages mentions FX policy at all; (3) overlap reconciliation as inspectable policy, not silent dedupe — agency-vs-platform double counting is the consolidation trap every practitioner knows, and here every overlap is a finding with evidence and a recorded human resolution; (4) snapshot versioning against upstream revision — platform-reported figures get revised after the fact (Source 12's caveat), so the honest ledger keeps its versions and shows its deltas instead of quietly rewriting the number finance already reconciled.

## Exclusions (narrow scope, enforced by LOOP-10)

- **No bid automation** — the product never writes back to any channel: no budget pacing rules that move money, no bid changes, no campaign state management. Spend is read, normalized, explained; never steered. (Roster exclusion verbatim.)
- **No creative analytics** — no ad-creative performance analysis, no asset-level reporting, no creative fatigue detection. The ledger's grain is the spend line item, not the asset.
- **No attribution modeling** — no last-click, multi-touch, or incrementality claims; no revenue-side joins; no "did spend cause the sale" analysis. Conversion data belongs to other tools; the boundary with funnel-drop-off-explorer is recorded in both 12s (LOOP-3, their Q-22 → this side's Q-4).
- **No warehouse/pipeline product** — the product stores its own specs (connections, policies, moments, budgets, snapshots, findings) and the line items it imported; it is not a data pipeline, storage tier, or connector marketplace (that is the retrieved category's center of gravity, 01 — deliberately not ours).
- **No forecasting or anomaly ML** — pacing is linear arithmetic against declared budget lines with declared variance thresholds; trend lines are computed series, not predictions. No AI anywhere (declared non-AI, 07a/07b/08).
- **No channel campaign management** — campaign references arrive as line-item metadata (external keys, names); the product never edits, creates, or syncs campaigns upstream.

## Success signals (spec-level definition of done)

Against the Mock Adapter, with zero provider knowledge anywhere in the UI:

1. **A first one-number dashboard in under 30 minutes**: a new analyst pastes a month of spend lines from two channels (zero credentials, LOOP-5), declares the reporting currency, runs the first consolidation, and reads the period total, the channel breakdown with conversion receipts, and the daily timeline — no spreadsheet pivot, no data-team request (P22 onboarding milestones).
2. **The full honesty journey on the Mock**: five channels connected (two non-native currencies), 26 months of line items, snapshot v2 computed; two overlap findings reconciled with recorded reasons (keep-first by connection priority, split); the upstream revision re-imported and the v3→v4 delta read as a named table (−SAR 1,560 on one day, one channel); Ramadan 1447 vs 1446 like-for-like read with lift ratio and control-window baseline; the report pack exported and retrieved before expiry — replayed deterministically twice with byte-identical state (07c determinism oracle).
3. **The receipt tests**: every non-native-currency figure expands to its conversion receipt (native amount, rate, basis, policy version); the AED daily-basis and USD monthly-average connections produce visibly different receipt shapes on the same screen; the FX table declares itself workspace policy data, not a market feed.
4. **The overlay tests**: White Friday (KSA scope) and Yellow Friday (UAE scope) render as separate moments over the same weekend; a Hijri-anchored moment shows its conversion method disclosure; the like-for-like comparison refuses to compute against a Gregorian-lookalike window and explains why (occurrence matching is lunar, 07c rule).
5. **The keyboard-speed test**: an analyst moves day-to-day on the timeline, expands breakdown rows into raw line items, resolves an overlap, and switches overlay windows entirely from the keyboard (09) with every state change announced per the live-region schedule and zero color-only carriers anywhere in the timeline (09, a11y-baseline §4).
