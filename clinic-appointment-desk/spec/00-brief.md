# 00 — Brief: clinic-appointment-desk

Family: specialized · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A bilingual (ar/en) booking console for clinic staff: the front desk runs the clinic's appointment day on a **day board** — providers as columns, time as rows, appointment blocks in the cells — and the product keeps that day honest and full. Staff book appointments for patients who call, message, or walk in (there is no patient self-booking portal at v1 — the buyer is the staff, not the patient); every appointment walks an explicit, inspectable lifecycle — `booked → confirmed → checked-in → in-consult → completed | no-showed | cancelled` — with every transition validated, timestamped, and attributed in an append-only activity log. The lifecycle's rules are deterministic arithmetic, and the product is **declared non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1 declaration rule): no predictive scoring, no smart anything. The machine counts and guards; the staff decides.

The unique feature is **waitlist auto-fill with no-show pattern flags**, and both halves are deterministic by construction. When a cancellation or a declared no-show opens a slot, the waitlist engine computes a ranked list of eligible waitlist entries using a fixed, inspectable formula — priority tier, days waited, and a no-show penalty — and presents the fill proposal to the staff with every ranking input visible. The top candidate gets an offer (sent through the abstract patient-messaging capability when bound, or called by the receptionist from a prepared bilingual script — the manual path is the tested default); offers carry honest expiry (15 minutes for same-day slots), decline or expiry advances to the next candidate, and at most three candidates are offered per slot so the waitlist never becomes a broadcast. The second half is the **no-show pattern flag**: a per-patient count of no-showed appointments in a trailing window (default: ≥2 in 180 days flags the pattern), surfaced to staff as a chip naming the count and the window, feeding the waitlist ranking penalty, and never blocking a booking — care access beats punitive gates. The flag rules, thresholds, and ranking weights are clinic-configurable data rendered in an inspectable policy panel, the same inspectability grammar as the portfolio's labor-rule guardrails (attendance-shift-planner kinship — judged honestly in 12, no artifact adjacency claimed).

The evidence that this is a real product, not a feature list: no-shows run ~23% of outpatient appointments on average (systematic review, 01), reminder ladders measurably reduce them (01 evidence blocks), and automated waitlists compress the cancellation-to-refill gap from hours of front-desk calling to minutes (01). The category, however, sells this either as patient-side marketplaces (Zocdoc class), practice-management suites bundling charting/billing/telehealth (Jane/Cliniko class — LOOP-10 exclusions here), or AI-receptionist prediction products (the counter-position this product refuses). A staff-side, bilingual-ar, waitlist-first booking console with inspectable deterministic rules — priced for the clinic, not the enterprise roll-out — is unoccupied in the MENA set (01 positioning gaps).

## Buyer

Front-desk staff and clinic managers of small-to-mid private clinics in MENA — family practice, dental, dermatology, pediatric, and specialty clinics running 1–6 providers with 1–3 receptionists. The daily user is the lead receptionist who answers the phone, books, reschedules, chases confirmations, and scrambles to fill cancellations (02 Reem); the economic buyer is the clinic manager or owner-director who answers for fill rate and no-show losses (02 Maha). Providers are deliberately thin participants at v1: they read their own day and mark consult states, review-only on the board (02 Dr. Haddad). The patient is an actor, not a user: they receive reminders and waitlist offers in their own locale and their replies land on the appointment — patient-facing surfaces are outbound only. Two to six staff seats; providers participate without front-desk seats. Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary console; responsive to 360px in day-review and patient-lookup modes — booking authoring is desktop/tablet only). Zones: **day board** (provider columns × time ruler, virtualized appointment blocks, day summary bar with dual Hijri/Gregorian date per P24); **appointment detail drawer** (lifecycle timeline, reminder ladder, minimal notes); **booking flow** (patient find-or-create → visit type → slot picker → confirm, with the no-show pattern flag surface); **patient view** (contact, locale, history, flag stats, waitlist entries); **waitlist console** (entries + fill proposals + offer states); **messages outbox** (reminder/waitlist-offer/confirmation records with states and record-reply actions); **providers & schedule** (session hours, working days); **policies** (inspectable deterministic rules: reminder ladder, no-show policy, waitlist weights — plus workspace locale); **day sheet export**; **jobs/activity console** (T3 consolidation per async-contract §7). ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05 — list surfaces exceed three). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1; no patient-facing booking page (LOOP-10).

## Unique feature

**Waitlist auto-fill with no-show pattern flags**, defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the ranking is **knowable arithmetic** — priority tier (staff-set, never auto), days waited, and a bounded no-show penalty, with fixed tiebreaks; every candidate row shows its inputs, so the front desk can defend every offer to a patient who asks why someone else was called first; (2) the no-show flag is **counting, not prediction** — a trailing-window count the staff can verify appointment-by-appointment, against a category trend toward opaque probability scores (01's AI-receptionist counter-position, Topuz 2024's "explainability" gap); (3) offers carry **honest expiry and a bounded offer count** — the waitlist never broadcasts, and a declined offer is recorded data, not a lost lead; (4) the whole loop runs **bilingual end to end** — outbound templates render in each patient's locale with Arabic-script-safe bidi handling, and the manual phone path is first-class with prepared scripts, which is where MENA clinics actually operate (01 channel evidence).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No telehealth — no video consults, no remote-visit links, no virtual-waiting-room surfaces (roster row exclusion; the appointment lifecycle is an in-person chair-side lifecycle).
- No billing — no invoices, payments, deposits, insurance eligibility, or no-show fees. The cancellation-policy *text* a clinic writes into its reminder template is copy, not a charge; money belongs to billing tools.
- No EMR — no clinical notes, diagnoses, prescriptions, or documents. The appointment carries a visit type and an optional free-text staff note; the patient record carries contact facts only. patient-intake-forms (batch 9) owns the clinical-intake artifact class (LOOP-3, mirrored in 12).
- No patient self-booking portal or booking page at v1 — staff-side booking only; patient surfaces are outbound messages.
- No predictive no-show scoring, no AI overbooking, no demand forecasting, no "smart" scheduling suggestions — declared non-AI (ai-native-patterns §1); the literature's overbooking line is the explicit counter-position (01).
- No queue-management displays or waiting-room TV pagers — check-in is a lifecycle state on the appointment, not a called-number system.
- No provider-pays marketplace, no patient acquisition, no reviews — the clinic's own book, not a listing surface.

## Mock-Adapter Definition of Done (success signals)

A receptionist can: find a calling patient by phone (or create one with a bilingual name); book a consultation into a free slot and watch the reminder ladder schedule itself; see a no-show pattern flag on a known patient's booking and read its count and window without the flag blocking anything; walk the day — check a patient in, mark a consult complete; declare a no-show and watch the slot open; run the waitlist fill on that slot and read the ranked candidates with their inputs; send the top offer (channel in the mock, or copy the call script); accept the reply and watch the slot refill; run a cancellation honestly to superseded state; record a phone confirmation on a pending reminder; edit the reminder cadence in policies and read every rule; export the day sheet and retrieve it before expiry — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full flow completing with the patient-messaging capability unbound (the phone path is the tested default).
