## Curated references

1. **Zocdoc — waitlist practice** — https://www.zocdoc.com/resources/blog/article/how-to-improve-patient-waitlist-management-and-fill-cancellations-faster and https://www.zocdoc.com/resources/blog/article/how-to-handle-patient-cancellations-without-losing-revenue-or-trust
   Take: the cancellation→offer idiom the whole category now teaches — the offer goes to the next eligible patient immediately, and the front desk stops calling down a list (the "73 minutes" framing is the workload shape our S4 fill panel + 15-minute offer clock render as product). Not taken: the marketplace posture — their waitlist exists to feed Zocdoc's patient demand into the clinic's slots; ours fills the clinic's own book, ranked by the clinic's own disclosed rules; no patient-acquisition surfaces, no listing economics (LOOP-10).

2. **Jane App — the practitioner-column day view** — https://jane.app and https://jane.app/pricing
   Take: the day-view grammar buyers already know — practitioners as columns, the day as rows, appointment blocks legible at a glance; our S1 board inherits the idiom and hardens it (bilingual blocks, lifecycle state chips, the flag glyph, virtualization). Not taken: the practice-suite bundle (charting, billing, telehealth — all LOOP-10 exclusions here), per-practitioner seat pricing that taxes the front desk, English-first template posture; their waitlist is a tab, not a ranking engine with disclosed inputs.

3. **NexHealth Waitlist — the latency bar** — https://www.nexhealth.com/features/waitlist
   Take: the seconds-to-notify latency expectation ("notify waitlisted patients in seconds… confirm with a single click") — our synchronous <150ms fill computation + instant offer send is designed against exactly this bar (10 performance budget). Not taken: the platform breadth (EHR integrators, forms funnel, reviews — the patient-experience-suite scope we deliberately refuse); one-click confirm without an expiry story — our offers carry honest expiry and a bounded offer count, which their marketing does not model.

## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T3-appropriate)

The Leaf Ledger: a receptionist's desk in calm green over cool paper — one sheet, three columns, everything the day knows written on it. The day board reads as a ruled ledger page: the time ruler down the inline-start edge in 700-weight Mada, provider columns separated by hairline leaf rules, appointment blocks as soft-cornered paper cards whose state is written in ink (a checkmark-seal for confirmed, a slate chip for checked-in, a quiet red flag glyph where the pattern counts). Nothing floats except the two surfaces that matter: the appointment drawer, and — the product's one object-quality moment — the waitlist offer card, lifted one soft step above the page with its ranking inputs printed like a receipt line: tier, days waited, penalty, total, in that order, in Western numerals. The signature moment is the **Slot Refill**: a cancelled slot renders as a dashed outline in the ledger (an honest absence), and when the offer is accepted the outline fills — 280ms, no translation, no scale, just paper becoming appointment — while the day's counts update in the same frame and one calm line of arithmetic announces itself ("تمت تعبئة الموعد من قائمة الانتظار — تبقى 4 طلبات"). The policies panel is the desk's rulebook drawer: every number the machine uses (48 hours, 08:00, 15 minutes, 100/2/10/30) printed with its plain-language explanation in both scripts, because a front desk trusts arithmetic it can read. Calm motion throughout: everything settles, nothing bounces, and urgency is always a number and a clock, never an animation.

## Mood anchors

A green-ruled ledger over cool paper · a dashed outline that becomes an appointment · ranking inputs printed like a receipt · a quiet red flag that counts, never punishes · the phone script as a first-class citizen · one soft-lifted card for the moment the day refills itself.
