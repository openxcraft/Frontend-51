## Category overview

Three established categories collide at this product, and none of them serves the buyer. **Product-analytics retention grids** (Mixpanel, Amplitude, PostHog class) own the cohort-grid shape — acquisition-window rows, subsequent-period columns, cell-to-member drill, period-0 semantics, incomplete-period honesty (S2) — but they are event-space instruments for growth teams: cohorts form around product events, meter by event volume, and carry no notion of order revenue or a retail season calendar (S1/S2/S3). **Commerce analytics platforms** (Glew class) own the revenue data model — customer segments, LTV, top-customer reporting at $250/mo team pricing (S5) — but their surface is dashboards and pre-built segments, not an acquisition-cohort grid, and MENA seasonality is absent from their feature vocabulary. **Storefront-native analytics** (WooCommerce-class reporting, the free incumbent every merchant already has) ship mature metric definitions and period comparison — Net Sales = Gross − Returns − Coupons, AOV = Net Sales / Orders, compare-to-previous-period-or-year, CSV exports as background jobs (S12) — with no cohort construct and no seasonality concept at all. The result is a practitioner gap the corpus documents directly: the revenue-ops analyst hand-builds the cohort grid in SQL and a spreadsheet, re-solves the Ramadan alignment by eye every year (the lunar drift is calendar mathematics — S7), and the honest fix official statistics would suggest (YoY comparison, S11) still compares misaligned Gregorian windows for drifting seasons. The wedge: a revenue cohort grid whose season calendar is a first-class, inspectable object — season-anchored cohorts, composition disclosure, drift disclosure — over the buyer's own commerce source, ending at an exportable evidence artifact. No captured competitor does any part of this.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **Storefront-native analytics** (WooCommerce-class built-in reporting, S12): free, already installed, owns the metric definitions — but period comparison is its ceiling; it cannot express "the customers who first bought in Ramadan 1447" at all. The definitional baseline we adopt and the cohort gap we fill.
- **Hand-built spreadsheet + SQL workflows**: the true incumbent for cohort grids — every captured practitioner convention (S1/S2) describes grids analysts already rebuild by hand. No season calendar, no composition disclosure, no reproducible snapshot; the manual alternative we formalize (and the LOOP-5 manual path we keep first-class).
- **Enterprise BI suites** (warehouse + dashboard class): could build cohort grids with analyst effort and calendar tables; the cost is a data project, a dashboard-authoring skill, and no season semantics baked in. Heavy, generic, and unowned by revenue ops.
- **Retention-marketing / CRM automation platforms**: their "cohort" features target campaign audiences (who to email), not revenue analysis (which acquisition windows produce durable customers); the CRM is our bound source and our roster exclusion, not our competitor feature-set — the boundary is recorded in 12.

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- **The season problem is mathematics, not taste**: the Islamic calendar is lunar, 10–11 days shorter than the solar year, with no intercalation — Ramadan migrates through the seasons (S7, Wikipedia Ramadan); Umm al-Qura dates are the standard Saudi reference and local moon sighting shifts dates by about a day across countries (S7) — the ±1-day drift disclosure this product renders is documented reality.
- **MENA season commerce is a named, dated economy**: Ramadan's advertising economics are Super-Bowl-class — a 30-second peak commercial usually costs more than double the normal rate (S7); White Friday was introduced as such in Egypt in 2014 and in the UAE in 2014, in Libya since 2019, and in Saudi Arabia by the local platform noon (S13, Wikipedia Black Friday); Eid al-Fitr is the fixed-in-the-lunar-calendar gifting moment immediately following Ramadan (S16). Two clocks — drifting lunar and fixed Gregorian — that a Gregorian grid cannot separate.
- **The platform consolidation that makes source binding real**: Souq.com, the largest e-commerce platform in the Arab world, was acquired by Amazon (2017, ~$580M confirmed) and became Amazon.ae/.sa/.eg (2019–2021) (S9) — commerce data lives in a small number of mature platforms, which is why an adapter-bound analysis layer is the right shape.
- **The cohort-grid craft is standardized** (S1/S2/S3/S14): dynamic computation, acquisition uniqueness, period-0 semantics, cell drill, incomplete-period marking, weighted means, definition governance — none of it retail-revenue-native, all of it the pattern a focused tool can implement exactly.
- **The YoY instinct is documented and insufficient**: official statistics use year-on-year changes to avoid seasonality contamination when adjustment isn't performed (S11) — the instinct exists, the tooling for drifting-lunar-season alignment does not.
- **Team budgets for commerce analytics are proven**: Glew Pro at $250/mo with pre-built customer segments (S5); cohort analytics as paid category in subscriptions (S4). No auditable MENA-specific TAM figure exists in the corpus; none is offered.

## Positioning statement

For revenue operations teams at MENA retailers, sales-cohort-view is the cohort grid that stops lying about seasons: acquisition cohorts form over order revenue from the buyer's own commerce source, the season calendar is a first-class inspectable object with dual Hijri/Gregorian dates and drift disclosure, cohorts anchor to named seasons (Ramadan 1446 vs Ramadan 1447, not March vs March), and every comparison carries its season-composition disclosure. Unlike product-analytics retention grids, it works in revenue, not events; unlike commerce dashboards, its unit of work is the acquisition cohort, not the segment or the widget; and unlike spreadsheet workflows, its arithmetic is published, versioned, and reproducible in an exportable evidence pack.
