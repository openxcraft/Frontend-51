# 00 — Brief: meeting-notes-action-desk

Family: business_ops · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A notes-to-actions desk for team leads in MENA who run bilingual meetings. The product begins where a meeting ends: the lead brings in what was captured — typed notes, a transcript pasted from whatever recorded the call, or an audio file run through the abstract transcription capability when one is bound — and the desk turns that capture into the three records a meeting actually owes its team: a bilingual meeting record (title, date, participants, the notes/transcript itself), the decisions that were agreed, and the action items that were born in the room. Actions are not a byproduct of a summary; they are the product's spine. Every action carries a single named owner, a due date, a bilingual text pair (each side authored, never auto-translated), and a link back to the evidence span in the source capture that produced it — so "who said what" stays auditable weeks later.

The unique feature is the **owner confirmation loop**. In the incumbent workflow — notes in a document, actions shouted across a room, follow-through left to memory — an "assigned" task is a hope, not a commitment. This product makes assignment a handshake with a state machine: a lead proposes an action to an owner; the owner explicitly **confirms** it (the action becomes a commitment, with the confirm timestamp and the owner's own session recorded), **questions** it (asks for clarification; the action returns to the lead's desk with the question attached), or **declines** it (the action lands in a reassignment-needed state instead of silently dying). Unconfirmed actions escalate honestly — they sit in the loop desk, past their respond-by window, visible to the lead, never auto-reassigned and never silently dropped. Follow-through continues on the same rail: confirmed actions move in-progress → done by their owners; overdue is a derived, computed zone, not a judgment. The loop is the accountability grammar Gulf office culture already runs on paper — the attestation instinct behind عهدة custody logs and signed hand-offs (asset-register-tracker's grammar) — applied to commitments instead of equipment.

The AI stance is **conditionally-AI** (ai-native-patterns.md §1): a bilingual summarization assist proposes the meeting summary, decision candidates, and action candidates from the captured notes or transcript when the capability is bound and consented, with every proposal draft-by-default until a human accepts, edits, or dismisses it (P15). When the assist is unbound, exhausted, or refused, the product degrades to **manual note-taking**: the lead types notes and authors actions by hand on the same surfaces, with deterministic cue-phrase highlighting (bilingual cue tables, 07c) still available to flag candidate actions inside whatever text was captured. Manual mode is the floor, not a stub — a complete meeting record, decisions, confirmed actions, and exported minutes must all be producible with the assist off (tested against the Mock Adapter's assist-off configuration). Arabic-English meetings are the design center, not a locale setting: captures arrive mixed-script and code-switched (Gulf meetings run Khaleeji/Najdi/MSA interleaved with English — research digest source 42), segments render at their own direction with bidi isolation, and every identity-bearing string (meeting title, action text, decision text) is authored in both languages by humans — the product never fabricates a translation the way it never fabricates a commitment.

## Buyer

Team leads at MENA businesses running recurring bilingual team meetings: ops leads at F&B and retail groups, delivery/logistics hub supervisors, agency account leads, school and clinic administrators, PMO coordinators — the person who runs the weekly huddle, watches follow-through dissolve between meetings, and answers when the quarter's commitments surface unfinished. The economic buyer and the daily user are usually the same seat at single-team scale; at multi-team groups the buyer is the ops or PMO lead and daily users include the team leads they support. Action owners — the people whose confirmations the loop depends on — are deliberately first-class but thin: they confirm, question, decline, and complete their own actions without ever needing the full desk. No mobile app at v1; the owner confirmation surfaces are responsive web reachable from a notification link. Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary: desk home, meeting detail, loop desk, actions register), responsive to 360px in owner modes (confirm/question/decline/complete an action, read one's own actions and the meeting record). Zones: **desk home** (the loop digest — pending confirmations, confirmation-overdue, overdue and due-soon actions, plus the meetings list); **meeting detail** (capture panel with the notes/transcript, summary panel, decisions, actions); **loop desk** (the confirmation queue with respond surfaces); **actions register** (every action across meetings, filtered by owner/status/zone); members directory; jobs & activity console (T3-consolidated); settings. ⌘K command palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three). No conferencing of any kind (LOOP-10): the product never hosts, joins, records, or bots into calls — capture arrives by typing, paste/import, or the abstract transcription capability. No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

Bilingual transcripts to assigned actions with an owner confirmation loop. Defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the category's action items are pushed, not handshake-confirmed — every major AI notetaker extracts action items and pushes them to a task list or CRM (research digest sources 1, 7, 32), and none documents an acceptance state machine before an action counts as assigned; the accountability-practice literature (sources 21–28) converges on owner/deliverable/deadline discipline but no product enforces the owner's own yes; (2) bilingual Arabic-English meeting records with per-side direction and human-authored pairs are structurally absent from the category leaders (source 42's tool table: Otter carries no Arabic at all; Tactiq/Fireflies handle MSA via third-party engines; dialect and code-switching honesty is the regional tools' differentiator, not the global ones'); (3) the loop produces a governance artifact — an action register with confirmation provenance (who committed, when, from which meeting, against which evidence span) — that a transcript-and-summary tool structurally cannot emit, and that survives as the auditable record of the team's commitments.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No conferencing** — the product never hosts, joins, records, or injects a bot into a call or meeting. Audio arrives as a file the lead already possesses; transcripts arrive pasted or imported; the transcription capability (08) is an abstract, consent-gated channel, not a meeting surface.
- **No wiki** — meeting records are not a knowledge base: no free page authoring, no cross-page linking, no backlinks, no spaces hierarchy. The meeting record and its three artifacts (capture, decisions, actions) are the entire content model.
- **No task management beyond meeting actions** — no projects, no kanban boards, no swimlanes, no priority matrices, no subtasks, no recurring task definitions, no task lists born outside a meeting. Every action in the register must trace to a meeting; that trace is the product's honesty and its scope fence. (An action created from the desk home still carries a meeting reference — it is authored into a meeting record.)
- No scheduling or calendar management (meetings have dates; the product never books rooms, sends invites, or owns availability).
- No messaging/chat channel, no comment threads beyond the action clarification loop, no @-mentions.
- No performance analytics on people (no action-completion leaderboards, no productivity scoring — the register reports state, not people-ranking).
- No AI generation beyond the declared summarization assist (summary/decision/action proposals); no auto-translation of authored bilingual pairs (either side empty is an honest, visible state, never a machine-filled one).

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

A team lead can, in one first session on the Mock Adapter with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI: create a meeting with a bilingual title and participants; paste a mixed-script capture (the seeded code-switched transcript) and watch ingestion land with per-segment direction; run the summarization assist and review its proposals — accept one action candidate, edit one, dismiss the planted low-confidence false positive (P15 gate exercised); author an action by hand with a bilingual pair, a single owner, and a due date; send it for confirmation and watch the owner-side confirmation arrive in the loop desk; as the owner, confirm it and see the commitment state land with a timestamp; question a second one and return it to the lead's desk; watch the seeded overdue action and the confirmation-overdue fixture render as derived zones with named values; mark a confirmed action done; record a decision; export bilingual minutes with the action register appendix before artifact expiry — and the entire loop must complete with the assist unbound and every abstract capability off: typed notes, hand-authored actions, deterministic cue highlighting, owner confirmations, and direct-download exports (LOOP-5 bar).
