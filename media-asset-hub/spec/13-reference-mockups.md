## Original direction sketch (prose — identity-driven, T3-appropriate)

**The bilingual card catalog.** The library renders as a card-catalog wall: spruce-tinted tiles on cool paper, each a catalog card — thumbnail (or duotone kind glyph in link-catalog mode), the filename exactly as the team wrote it (Arabic flowing right-to-left beside Latin, never transliterated), and small stamps: rights state, in-asset-text mark, pending-review count. The metadata drawer slides from the inline-end like a catalog drawer pulled out: paired ar/en fields as parallel columns with parity ticks between them — the archivist's eye reads both languages at a glance, and the missing side is a visible blank slot, an invitation, never a machine guess. Search is one bar that listens in both scripts: type "مؤتمر" and the grid answers with conference photos whose banners carry the word burned into the pixels — each hit wearing a stamp reading "found inside the asset, 92% confident" — while a quiet chip beneath the bar admits "matched with hamza and taa-marbuta variants normalized," the library whispering its matching rules instead of hiding them. Motion is nearly still — cards settle in a two-step fade, drawers slide without bounce — because a catalog's virtue is that things stay where you filed them. The rights console is the room's amber lamp: a short wall of expiring licenses, each a card with its days counted in Western numerals, so nothing lapses quietly.

## Mood anchors

A spruce catalog wall in a working newsroom archive · filenames in the hand that made them · stamps and confidence marks like ledger annotations · one amber shelf for expiring rights · stillness — find, don't perform.
