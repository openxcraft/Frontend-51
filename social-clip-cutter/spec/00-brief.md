# 00 — Brief: social-clip-cutter

Family: content_tools · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A transcript-driven clip editor for podcast and video teams: the workspace that sits between "episode finished" and "short-form clips shipped". The team registers an episode source (a media reference that stays wherever the team already keeps it, plus an imported transcript — pasted, or pulled from an abstract "transcript source"), and the transcript becomes the cutting surface. Highlights are found by reading and searching text, not by scrubbing waveforms: a deterministic heuristic pass (`findHighlights`) ranks candidate moments with inspectable scores (keyword density, speech-pause boundaries, hook patterns — rules, not a model), and the human selects, adjusts, or ignores them. Every clip boundary anchors to transcript segment indices, so timecodes derive from the segments and cannot drift; boundary refinement moves anchors in segment steps with sub-second offsets, never hand-typed timecodes.

Captions are the second half of the job, and Arabic is where category tools fail: mainstream editors and players break RTL subtitle text (reversed runs, clipped glyphs, migrating punctuation — documented across NLE and player communities, 01), while AI clip tools emit machine captions tuned for Latin scripts. social-clip-cutter ships **Arabic-safe caption burn-in specs**: caption styles drawn from an Arabic-safe font catalog (real weight sets, no synthetic bold), RTL line-breaking that never splits joined words, bidi isolation for mixed-direction runs (a Latin product name inside an Arabic caption renders correctly, never reordered), punctuation mirroring, tashkeel-aware line-height, reading-speed gates, and per-platform safe-zone placement (9:16 TikTok/Shorts/Reels-class, with platform UI overlays kept clear). The export runs as an asynchronous render job with per-clip progress and partial-results delivery, and every export also emits sidecar caption files (SRT/VTT) alongside the burned-in master — because burn-in works everywhere but closed captions serve accessibility better where players support them (01 evidence).

The product is deliberately **non-AI** (ai-native-patterns.md §1 declaration): no generation, no model calls, no sparkles. The "smart" part is a set of deterministic, inspectable selection heuristics specified as rules in the adapter contract — keyword density over declared keyword sets, speech-pause detection over segment timing gaps, hook patterns (questions, imperative openers, numbered lists) — each candidate carries its rationale string, so the operator always sees *why* a moment was proposed. This is the honest answer to the category's documented pain: reviewers report discarding 20–40% of machine-picked clips and paying credit-metered prices for the privilege (01); text-first human selection with rule-based assistance keeps the human in the cutting decision while removing the scrubbing.

## Buyer

Podcast and video teams in MENA producing episode-length content and shipping short-form clips from it: video-podcast studios (Arabic-first and bilingual), newsroom/social-video desks, agencies cutting client episodes into TikTok/Reels/Shorts-class clips, and solo hosts with a producer. Team size 1–5 seats; the buyer already records and edits elsewhere (recording suites and NLEs) and currently loses hours per episode to clip selection and caption rework — especially when Arabic captions break in their editor.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary). The workbench is a three-zone surface: transcript pane (virtualized segment list with selection ranges and normalized bilingual search), clip rail (the episode's clips with boundary state and lint), and preview stage (local media reference playback + burn-in preview composited client-side — media never uploads). A persistent export bar anchors the workbench foot; a jobs center and an artifacts library sit one navigation hop away. Responsive down to 360px: the episodes board, artifacts library, and jobs center remain usable; the workbench stacks to a single column with zone switching; the preview stage degrades to an audio-less storyboard of segment cards. Zones: left rail (Episodes · Clips · Exports · Jobs · Settings), workbench (transcript · clip rail · preview stage), burn-in studio (a workbench mode), export composer.

## Unique feature

Transcript-driven clip selection with Arabic-safe caption burn-in. Selection is text-first: the operator reads and searches the transcript (with Arabic normalization — alef variants, taa marbuta, yaa forms), the heuristic pass proposes moments with visible scores and rationale, and boundaries anchor to segments. The burn-in spec is Arabic-safe by construction: bidi policy, no mid-word line breaks, mirrored punctuation, tashkeel-aware line boxes, safe-zone placement, and reading-speed gates — all validated by a deterministic lint (`setClipBurnInSpec` returns warnings, never silently accepts an unreadable spec). Defensible because (a) AI clip tools' auto-selection is the category's documented complaint (discard rates, review time exceeding manual editing — 01), and text-first precision is the direct counter-position; (b) RTL subtitle breakage in mainstream editors is documented (01 evidence: reversed text, clipped runs, SRT→ASS workaround communities) and nobody in the clip category treats Arabic caption typography as a first-class discipline; (c) captions here are burn-in *convenience* on cut clips — the deep refinement suite belongs to caption-lab (boundary in both 12s), and refined outputs can round-trip via standard caption files.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10, binding)

- No caption refinement suite — caption-lab owns tashkeel-aware refinement, reading-speed craft, and burn-in style authoring at depth; our caption specs are convenience-grade presets + lint on cut clips (adjacency flagged in 12, mirrored in caption-lab's Q-4).
- No scheduling, no publishing, no auto-posting to social platforms (calendar family; several category competitors bundle one-click publishing — we hand off files).
- No library management — no asset collections, folders, tagging taxonomies, or rights workflows (media-asset-hub owns the library family); episode sources and clips are flat, per-episode lists.
- No video timeline editing beyond clip boundaries — no trims inside a clip, no effects, no multi-track surgery (NLEs own that; LOOP-10).
- No transcription of its own at v1 — transcripts are imported (paste or abstract transcript source); the product never claims to transcribe.
- No analytics on clip performance.

## Success signals (definition of done, Mock Adapter)

A producer can: register an episode source and paste an hour-long bilingual transcript, landing ~900 timed segments; search the transcript in Arabic and English and get normalized hits; run the highlight pass and receive ranked candidates each carrying a rationale and score; accept 4 candidates and hand-draw 2 selections; refine every boundary by segment steps with keyboard only; spec Arabic-safe burn-in on each clip, see reading-speed and safe-zone warnings resolve in the preview (both directions), and watch mixed-direction caption runs render correctly inside the RTL preview; queue a 6-clip export with burn-in + sidecar caption files; receive per-clip progress, one simulated partial failure with a scoped retry; and download artifacts with expiry + renewal — all in one session on the Mock Adapter, zero provider names anywhere in the UI, and the export recipe (caption files + boundary timecodes + burn-in spec sheet) available as the fully-capable manual path for external tools.
