## Category overview

Three classes of product currently answer "tell me when my business number is off", and none of them answers it for a MENA team lead. The **KPI platform class** (Databox) bundles dashboards, goals, AI summaries, and anomaly detection into one analyst-shaped platform — anomaly detection is commodity-inclusive there (included at the free tier, q2), metered by AI credits, and priced for analysts and agencies. The **dashboard class** (Geckoboard, Klipfolio) sells TV dashboards and team visibility, with threshold alerts as a tier-gated feature delivered to chat channels (q12: alerts gated to the $319/mo tier; "success or warning thresholds" vocabulary). The **engineering alerting class** (Grafana Alerting; the SRE tradition) owns the mature discipline — alert rules from queries, notification pipelines, and the ticket/page severity split — but its buyer is an infrastructure team, its vocabulary is queries and expressions (q3), and its cultural home is on-call rotations we explicitly exclude. The **anomaly-detection pure-plays that once sold business digests are gone or pivoted**: the Anodot domain now lands on a 404 redirect inside an experience-management vendor's site (q1), and the Outlier domain hosts an AI-training freelancer platform instead of the weekly business-anomaly digest product (q6) — consolidation evidence that the standalone anomaly vendor did not survive, leaving the digest behavior to live inside platforms shaped for other buyers. The open ground: a focused, team-lead-priced console whose differentiator is not detection math (seasonal decomposition is published, orthodox craft — q14, S-H-ESD; q16, additive seasonality with holiday effects) but **explanation quality in two languages** — the one axis no retrieved competitor competes on.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **The SRE paging/ticket culture** (Google SRE Book ch. 6 and Workbook "Alerting on SLOs", q8/q9): the discipline our digest-first cadence borrows — pages are expensive, tickets/digests are the default — sold as a book and practiced in engineering tools with on-call rotations, not as a product for business team leads. Our cadence tiers are this discipline, retargeted.
- **Observability platforms** (Datadog-class "Monitoring 101" conventions, q10): "alert liberally; page judiciously", "page on symptoms rather than causes", and the plain-language alert ideal ("90% of all web requests are taking more than 0.5s") — infrastructure-first tools whose buyer is not our team lead, and whose alerts speak English to engineers.
- **Spreadsheets and manual checks** (the true incumbent): the team lead's morning ritual of opening four dashboards and one spreadsheet before 9am, with conditional formatting as the closest thing to a threshold rule. Our manual-paste path (LOOP-5) is this workflow, given a watch engine and a digest.
- **BI-suite alert footnotes** (global full-stack suites): carry data alerts inside much heavier, analyst-owned platforms; the team lead is not their buyer, and their alerting is a settings surface, not a console.

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- **Alert fatigue is documented, cross-domain, and behavioral**: "busy workers… become desensitized to safety alerts, and as a result ignore or fail to respond appropriately" — healthcare/industrial evidence (Wikipedia "Alarm fatigue", q11); "real problems are often lost in a sea of noisy alarms" (Datadog Monitoring 101, q10); "Paging a human is a quite expensive use of an employee's time… you should never trigger an alert simply because 'something seems a bit weird'" (SRE Book ch. 6, q8). No business-monitoring desensitization percentages were retrievable from this environment — the argument stands on qualitative and cross-domain evidence (honest gap, digest).
- **The severity-split numbers are public and citable**: paging defaults of 2% budget consumption in 1 hour and 5% in 6 hours; ticket alerts at 10% budget consumption in 3 days (SRE Workbook, q9) — the published defaults our digest-first tiering adapts (07c policy provenance).
- **Anomaly detection is already a commodity checkbox**: included at Databox's free tier with AI-credit metering (q2); tier-gated at Geckoboard (q12). Detection math is not differentiable; explanation quality is.
- **The standalone anomaly-digest category consolidated**: Anodot domain → 404 redirect inside an experience-management vendor's site (q1); Outlier domain → AI-training freelancer platform (q6); the archived reference implementation of seasonal anomaly detection (S-H-ESD, archived Nov 2021, q14) sits frozen while platforms absorbed the behavior. Open ground, not contested ground.
- **Seasonality-with-holidays is orthodox method, not invention**: additive models with yearly/weekly/daily seasonality plus holiday effects, needing several seasons of history (q16); decomposition robust "in the presence of seasonality and an underlying trend" (q14). For MENA retail metrics, Ramadan's ~11-day annual drift makes holiday-effect handling mandatory — without it, every seasonal peak is a false alert.
- **The team-lead digest instinct exists in the wild**: "Catch what's changing across every client while there's still time to act" (Databox agency positioning, q2) — the buyer's sentence, in an analyst platform's voice.

## Positioning statement

For team leads in MENA who own numbers living in other people's tools, kpi-alert-watch is the alert console that explains itself in both languages: every detection carries a bilingual threshold explanation — the baseline, the observed value, and the band arithmetic, in English and in Arabic — and every watch defaults to the digest instead of the interrupt. Unlike KPI platforms, it is not an analyst suite with anomaly detection as a checkbox; unlike dashboard tools, it does not gate alerts behind a $319 tier and a chat channel; and unlike engineering alerting, it speaks to the team lead, not to a rotation. The console earns attention by explaining the arithmetic behind every alarm it raises.
