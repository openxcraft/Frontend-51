## What it is

A cross-metric alert console for team leads in MENA who own numbers that live in other people's tools. The buyer is not a data engineer and does not want to become one: she runs a delivery operation, a marketing budget, or a support queue, she can name six numbers she is accountable for (average delivery time, order completion rate, blended acquisition cost, CSAT, COD rejection rate, daily sales), and today she learns that one of them broke three days late — from a customer, from her manager, or from a monthly report. The product takes that job. The lead declares **metric feeds** (a named metric with its unit, its direction — higher-is-better or lower-is-better — and its data arriving either through the abstract metric-source capability when one is bound or by pasted/imported snapshots, which work from the first minute with zero credentials), puts a **watch** on each feed (a detection policy: threshold bands against a declared limit, seasonal anomaly detection against a computed baseline, or both), and chooses a **cadence tier** per watch: page-class (instant console interrupt) or digest-class (the daily or weekly digest). Everything the product says afterwards passes through one disciplined surface: the console's detection feed and the digest.

The signature artifact is the **anomaly digest with bilingual threshold explanations**. Every detection the engines produce carries an **explanation sentence pair** — one English, one Arabic, both authored by the product as fixed bilingual templates with number slots, never machine-translated — that states the rule that fired in plain language: what was watched, what the baseline is and why (same-weekday seasonal mean over a trailing window, excluding declared season windows), what was observed, how far outside the expected band it sits, and for how long. "Average delivery time rose to 38 minutes — 2.4 standard deviations above its Thursday baseline of 29 minutes; the warning band starts at 34." / "ارتفع متوسط زمن التوصيل إلى 38 دقيقة — بانحراف 2.4 انحراف معياري عن خط أساس الخميس البالغ 29 دقيقة؛ وتبدأ نطاقات التحذير عند 34." The explanation is not a courtesy; it is the product's answer to alert fatigue, which the research corpus documents across domains: people stop responding to alarms that do not explain themselves (01 §4 — desensitization evidence; "real problems are often lost in a sea of noisy alarms"). An alert that names its own arithmetic earns the attention it asks for.

The cadence philosophy is digest-first, and it is borrowed deliberately from the paging/ticket discipline of the SRE tradition (01 §3): interrupting a human is expensive, so the default tier for anomaly detections is the digest, and only a threshold rule the lead explicitly marks page-class interrupts. The counter-fatigue stack continues: **season windows** (declared calendar moments — Ramadan, White Friday, Eid, national days — with expected direction) suspend seasonal anomaly watching during the window and say so in the digest, so the Ramadan sales surge is a season note, not forty false alerts; **backtest preview** shows, before a watch is saved, how many times the policy would have fired over the trailing window — the precision check the lead runs before committing her team's attention; and **dismissal with reason + snooze with expiry** record why an alert was noise, into a queryable journal, forever capped — never a silent mute. The AI stance is **conditionally-AI** (ai-native-patterns.md §1): exactly one surface — anomaly **narration assist** for a detection's context — may be enriched by an abstract model capability when bound and consented, appending a narrative section with visible provenance; when unbound, rate-limited, or failed, the narration degrades totally to the templated bilingual rule text, which is the shipped default and is complete on its own. The detection engines themselves — baseline computation, threshold evaluation, seasonal decomposition, digest assembly — are deterministic and published (07c), and the product ends at the monitoring boundary (LOOP-10): no incident management (no incident objects, responders, postmortems, or escalation chains), no on-call scheduling (no rotations, no calendars of who is paged — the console tells the team lead, not a rota), no log analytics (metrics only, never raw logs).

## Buyer

Team leads at MENA businesses who own operational or commercial numbers across a handful of tools: quick-commerce and F&B ops leads watching delivery SLA and completion rate, marketing leads watching spend pacing and acquisition cost across channels, growth leads watching conversion and repeat rate, customer-experience leads watching CSAT and contact volume. The economic buyer and daily user are the same seat at single-team scale — the person who answers in the Monday meeting for why last week's number moved; at multi-team groups the buyer is the ops or analytics lead and daily users are the team leads they support, plus one deliberately thin seat class: the **digest reader** (an Arabic-first regional director who reads the weekly digest and never opens the console — a viewer with digest delivery, which the bilingual explanations exist for). Per-seat team tool; the digest reader seat is priced to be included, not to be taxed (12 Q-3). The adjacent buyers we do not serve: SRE and infrastructure teams (their alerting culture is symptom-based paging with on-call rotations — excluded scope, different buyer), and BI teams (they own dashboards; this product owns the space between dashboard glances).

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary; responsive to 360px in read/triage modes — the morning digest scan and the detection detail are one-hand surfaces). Zones: **console home** (the detection feed — open items by severity, page-class banner, feed health); **feed workspace** (metric series with baseline band rendering, ingestion actions); **watch editor** (policy authoring with backtest preview); **detections triage** (filterable list with bulk acknowledge); **detection detail** (the bilingual explanation card, series context, narrate assist, ack/dismiss/snooze); **digest view** (the bilingual daily/weekly document); **season calendar** (windows list, active states); jobs & activity console (T3-consolidated); settings (digest tiers per watch class, capability consent state, baseline disclosure). ⌘K command palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three) with bilingual normalized search (rtl-rules §4). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1; no widget walls — the console is a queue with explanations, not a dashboard of gauges (the family's recorded posture, 12 Q-4).

## Unique feature

Anomaly digests with bilingual threshold explanations. Defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **nobody explains thresholds in the retrieved category set** — the KPI platform class ships anomaly detection as an inclusive commodity checkbox (Databox includes it at the free tier) and the dashboard class gates threshold alerts behind a tier (Geckoboard at $319/mo), but in every retrieved case the alert arrives as a number and a threshold crossed, with the baseline logic invisible; explanation quality is not the competition's axis, and desensitization research says it should be; (2) **the bilingual pair is structurally absent** — no retrieved competitor renders threshold reasoning in Arabic, and the MENA buyer's reality is a lead who works in English reporting to a director who reads Arabic; the explanation pair (authored templates, never machine translation) is the only artifact in the category that both seats can read and trust; (3) **the pure-play anomaly-digest vendors consolidated away** (01 §2 — the standalone anomaly platforms are dead or pivoted; the behavior survives inside platforms priced and shaped for analysts), which leaves the focused, team-lead-priced, bilingual console as open ground rather than contested ground.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No incident management** — no incident objects, no responder assignment, no status pages, no postmortems, no escalation chains. A detection is acknowledged or dismissed; what the team does about it is their operational reality, not our data model (12 Q-4).
- **No on-call scheduling** — no rotations, no shift calendars, no who-is-paged logic. The console addresses the team lead's workspace and the digest reader's delivery; it never schedules humans (adjacent product class owns that culture).
- **No log analytics** — metrics only (numeric series with declared units and directions); no raw log ingestion, search, or parsing. The engines evaluate numbers, never text streams.
- **No dashboards/widget walls** — no KPI card galleries, no gauge walls, no free-form chart boards. The series chart exists to explain a detection or a feed, not to be arranged into a wall (family posture, mirrored from the flagship's 00).
- **No predictive forecasting** — baselines describe the observed past (trailing seasonal means with declared windows); the product does not forecast, does not project, and never claims a prediction. Anomaly narration describes computed evidence only.
- **No auto-tuning** — dismissal reasons and snooze records build a queryable journal, but the product never silently rewrites a policy from feedback; policy changes are explicit, versioned, and previewed via backtest (P15-adjacent honesty; 07b rule).
- **No machine translation of explanations** — the Arabic explanation is an authored template with number slots, co-equal to the English one; there is no "translate this alert" affordance anywhere (the bilingual pair is the product, not a feature of it).

## Success signals (spec-level definition of done)

Against the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI:

1. **A first explained alert in the first session**: the lead creates a feed, pastes 8 weeks of daily snapshots (the seeded manual path), puts a threshold watch on it, runs a backtest preview that says "this policy would have fired 4 times in the trailing window — 1 inside a season window, 1 matching a previously dismissed reason", saves the watch, runs one evaluation, and reads a detection whose bilingual explanation names the baseline, the observed value, and the band arithmetic — all without binding any capability (LOOP-5 bar).
2. **The digest moment**: the daily digest renders bilingually with 4 detections across 3 feeds (1 page-class item held at top); the lead acknowledges two, dismisses one with a reason, snoozes one for 3 days; the digest records all three decisions with timestamps and re-renders honestly.
3. **The degradation test**: with the narration capability unbound (and with every abstract capability off — metric-source, narration, delivery), the full loop above completes with template explanations, in-console digests, and pasted data; the explanation card renders identical template text with provenance "template" where the bound path would append an assist narrative — never a blank, never a spinner lie.
4. **The fatigue counters, exercised**: backtest preview shows a detection count before save; a snooze expires and the watch resumes; a dismissal reason is recorded and queryable; a season window suspends anomaly watching, a threshold rule still fires inside it, and the digest says so in both languages.
5. **The bilingual read**: every explanation string exists in both languages by construction (no machine translation anywhere in the loop); numerals stay Western in both directions (ledger row); the digest reader persona can consume the weekly digest end-to-end in Arabic alone.

The 07c mock implements `KpiAlertWatchAdapterV1` (07b) exactly: a seeded bilingual workspace (Sidra Market — a Riyadh quick-commerce group) with 5 named members (one Arabic-only digest reader), 6 bilingual metric feeds carrying 12 weeks of deterministic daily series (fixed weekday multipliers, trend terms, and a fixed noise table, with engineered deviation fixtures at pinned dates), 6 seeded watches spanning every policy class (threshold-only, anomaly-only, combined, page-class, paused), a season calendar containing the Saudi National Day window inside the series range plus queued Ramadan/White Friday windows, 10+ seeded detections across every status (open, acknowledged, dismissed-with-reason, snoozed), a rendered daily digest and a delivered weekly digest with delivery history — with the baseline engine, the threshold engine, the seasonal-suspension rule, the backtest counter, the explanation template tables, and the digest assembler specified as EXACT deterministic rules (07c §2) that the seed data cannot contradict: every seeded detection, baseline number, and explanation sentence is reproducible by the engines from the seeded series, or it does not ship. Fixed seeded clock 2026-10-17T08:00+03:00; reset/snapshot/replay with byte-identical state; zero external dependencies, no network, no timers beyond declared cadences.
