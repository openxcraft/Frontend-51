# 00 — Brief: patient-intake-forms

Family: specialized · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A bilingual (ar/en) patient-intake product for clinics, in three connected surfaces. The **builder** is where clinic staff author intake forms: sections, fields, and — the product's signature field type — the symptom field, with every label, option, and help text written in both locales before the form can publish. The **patient fill surface** is what patients see: a one-question-per-screen, Arabic-first RTL flow (English a full citizen, one tap away) that runs on a phone via a link, on a waiting-room tablet in kiosk mode, or from a printed QR — resumable by design (P10 autosave, P11 recall within the submission), with honest timeouts and no PHI in URLs. The **review console** is where submitted intakes land: answers rendered bilingual-side-by-side where the patient wrote free text, the symptom vocabulary normalization panel showing every match with its mode, and the clinic's escalation flag for red-flag terms — counting and surfacing, never diagnosing.

The unique mechanic is **Arabic symptom vocabulary normalization**. Patients describe symptoms in whatever Arabic they actually speak — MSA, Gulf, Levantine, Egyptian colloquial, mixed with English drug names — and the clinic needs that free text to land on canonical symptom vocabulary a provider can act on. The deterministic core does orthographic normalization (rtl-rules §4: tashkeel and tatweel stripping, alef/hamza unification, taa marbuta, alif maqsura) plus dictionary lookup against the workspace's own symptom vocabulary — every entry carrying canonical ar/en terms, colloquial variants, and a clinic-set urgent-attention flag. That core is **conditionally-AI**: an abstract vocabulary-normalization capability, when a workspace binds one, additionally resolves dialectal paraphrases the dictionary misses — and when unbound, rate-limited, or failed, the product degrades to the dictionary lookup, total and instant, with unmatched free text kept verbatim and flagged for staff. Assist matches are drafts until a human confirms them (P15); the normalization panel is the confirm surface. The stance is honest about why: the dialectal-Arabic symptom vocabulary literature is nearly empty (research digest S31 — five hits), so the dictionary plus a degradable assist is the defensible v1 shape, not a claimed understanding engine.

The boundaries are hard. No EHR integration at v1 — the handoff is an export package (bilingual intake record, PDF/CSV/print) through an abstract delivery channel with download as the always-available manual path. No diagnosis support — no triage advice, no severity scoring, no "smart" anything: the urgent flag is a count of clinic-configured red-flag terms present, the same counting-not-prediction grammar as the portfolio's clinic-appointment-desk. No insurance claim submission, no eligibility, no payments. The product's artifact is the completed, normalized, reviewed intake record with a full audit chain (P17) — evidence a clinic can hand a provider, and the front-desk pair to clinic-appointment-desk's booking console (LOOP-3 mirrors in 12).

## Buyer

Clinics in MENA — family practice, dental, dermatology, pediatric, and specialty clinics running 1–6 providers — and the staff who run their front of house. The economic buyer is the clinic manager or owner-director who answers for waiting-room throughput and for the quality of what reaches the provider (a missed allergy line is a clinical risk event, not a UX bug). The daily users are two: the **practice manager or lead nurse** who authors and maintains the forms and the symptom vocabulary (builder role), and the **front-desk / clinical staff** who review submitted intakes, confirm normalization matches, and escalate red flags (reviewer role). The **patient** is an actor, not an account: they receive a link or a tablet, fill in their own locale, and their answers — not their identity — are the product's payload. 2–6 staff seats; patients unbounded and unmetered. Personas in 02.

## Surface

Web application in two shells over one IA (04). The **staff console** (desktop-first, ≥1024px; responsive to 360px in read modes) carries: forms library; the form builder (section canvas + field palette + bilingual copy editor + live preview); the submissions console (filters, flags, saved views); the submission review workspace (answers + normalization panel + escalation + audit); the symptom vocabulary manager; exports & print; jobs center; settings. The **patient fill surface** is a separate mobile-first shell (≥360px, touch, RTL-first, one question per screen — the form-craft evidence base in NN/g's top-10 recommendations, research S9) with: entry (link/kiosk/QR), the progressive flow with autosave and resume, the symptom field with live normalization suggestions, the review-and-attest step, and a calm confirmation. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate) on the staff console only. No mobile staff app; no browser extension; no public API at v1; no patient self-scheduling (clinic-appointment-desk's family).

## Unique feature

**Bilingual medical intake with Arabic symptom vocabulary normalization**, defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **the vocabulary is clinic-owned, not machine-translated** — Arabic questionnaire translation is a formal COSMIN-grade discipline (research S30) and Arabic medical terminology needed a multi-country consensus for one disease family (S4), so the workspace dictionary with human-reviewed variants is the honest instrument, not an auto-translation claim; (2) **the normalization is inspectable** — the fixed orthographic pipeline is disclosed in-product, every match carries its mode (exact / normalized / assist / unmatched) and the matched text, and the deterministic core always produces the same result from the same input; (3) **the assist degrades gracefully** — conditionally-AI with a dictionary-lookup floor, per the portfolio's AI declaration discipline (ai-native-patterns §1/§8), in the exact thin-literature zone (S31) where overclaiming would be easy and wrong; (4) **the escalation is counting, not judgment** — clinic-configured red-flag terms surface as counts and links to the exact answers, never as a scored risk (the symptom-checker counter-position the literature actively criticizes, S10/S17). No retrieved competitor ships an Arabic-first intake surface with dialect-aware symptom normalization at clinic price points (01 positioning gaps).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No EHR integration at v1** — records move out as export packages (PDF/CSV/print) through an abstract intake-delivery channel (08 N2); download is the default path. A live record write is a cross-product owner decision (LOOP-4), never a silent default.
- **No diagnosis support** — no triage, no severity scoring, no symptom-checker logic, no differential anything. Normalization maps vocabulary; the urgent flag counts clinic-configured red-flag terms; humans assess. Enforced in 07b (no operation returns an assessment).
- **No insurance claim submission** — no claims, eligibility, copay collection, or payments of any kind; fees belong to billing tools.
- **No patient records management** — the artifact is the intake submission, not a longitudinal patient chart; no cross-visit clinical history, no medication timeline beyond what one form captures; recall (P11) is within-submission only.
- **No telehealth, no scheduling, no reminders ladder** — booking and the appointment day belong to clinic-appointment-desk (LOOP-3 mirror in 12); our only outbound surface is the fill link invitation.
- **No charting, no clinical notes authoring, no document generation beyond the intake export.**
- **No OCR of paper forms** — the paper path is manual entry by staff into the same form (recorded as the honest manual alternative), not a scanning promise.
- **No AI-written questions, AI-translated forms, or AI-made clinical judgments** — the single conditionally-AI surface is the vocabulary-normalization assist, labeled, degradable, and confirm-gated (P15).

## Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done (success signals)

A clinic team can, in the first session on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and every abstract capability unbound: create a form with a symptom field; author its ar+en labels and options (the publish gate refuses an English-only form, naming the exact gaps); add symptom vocabulary entries with colloquial variants and one urgent flag; publish; open the patient fill surface from a link in Arabic RTL on a 360px viewport; type a colloquial symptom phrase and watch the deterministic dictionary resolve it (suggestion chip, mode "normalized"); leave the form mid-way and resume it with prior answers marked as recalled (P11); submit with an attestation; watch the review console surface the submission with an urgent flag counting the red-flag term; confirm one assist-suggested match through the P15 panel (mock simulates the capability deterministically); mark the submission reviewed; export the intake package and retrieve the artifact before expiry; and audit the whole chain in the activity log. With the normalization capability unbound, the same colloquial phrase falls back to dictionary mode or lands verbatim as unmatched-with-flag — the degradation test (08) — and every step above still completes.
