# 02 — User Research: patient-intake-forms

SME-B pass · Paraphrased sentiment only, marked (paraphrase) — zero verbatim quotes; personas synthesized from the research digest (q1–q31: Qatar language-barrier study, NN/g form-craft corpus, COSMIN/terminology literature, vendor pricing pages) and portfolio-adjacent clinic-buyer patterns. Personas use regional names; no real individuals are represented.

## Persona 1 — Huda, Clinic Manager / Owner-Director (economic buyer)

**Role**: runs a 4-provider family practice in Riyadh — two receptionists, one nurse-practitioner-led intake desk, mixed expatriate and Saudi patient population. Answers for waiting-room throughput, for what reaches the provider before the consult, and for anything that looks like a clinical near-miss from bad intake data.
**Context**: currently prints bilingual intake packets (an English form plus a stapled Arabic translation that drifted out of sync months ago); the receptionist hand-keys highlights into the practice system; the clipboard queue at 4pm is the day's bottleneck.
**Goals**: every patient completes intake before the consult; the provider sees structured, canonical symptom data in both locales; zero double-keying; an audit trail she can show a physician partner or an inspector.
**JTBD**: "When a patient walks in, I want their intake completed on their own phone or our tablet in their own language before the consult starts, so the provider reads a structured record instead of a scanned PDF and my receptionist stops re-typing." (paraphrase posture — persona voice, not a real quote)
**Triggers**: an allergy line a provider only caught verbally mid-consult; a packet that ran out of Arabic pages; a no-show-length wait driven by clipboard intake; a physician partner asking why symptom text is unsearchable.
**Frustrations (pains)**: English-first tools that treat Arabic as a template problem (research S4/S30: bilingual medical instruments need deliberate authoring, not auto-translation); per-submission quotas (the Jotform ladder meters the exact thing she wants more of — 01 pricing); health-system pricing she cannot justify (Phreesia demo sales, 01).
**Objections + responses**: "We already have forms in our practice system." → the suite's forms are English templates; the intake instrument — bilingual publish gate, symptom vocabulary, escalation flags — is what's missing, and it exports back without re-platforming (08 N2 manual default). "Is this an EHR?" → no: no chart, no longitudinal record, no claims (LOOP-10); the artifact is the intake package. "What about patient privacy?" → PHI discipline is structural: opaque tokens in URLs, no answer-echo in errors or announcements, retention as clinic policy with expiry sweeps (09 §privacy; 12 Q-11).

## Persona 2 — Nour, Lead Nurse / Practice Manager (builder, primary daily user)

**Role**: owns the forms and the symptom vocabulary at Huda's clinic; 11 years in primary care; the person physicians complain to when an intake question is ambiguous. Maintains the Arabic copy herself and knows which colloquial symptom phrasings the patient population actually uses.
**Context**: splits her week between clinical duties and "form admin"; edits happen in stolen minutes between patients, often interrupted — exactly the pattern NN/g documents for interrupted form users (research S8).
**Goals**: author one form that works in both locales from a single structure; keep the symptom vocabulary current with the phrases she hears at the desk; see, in writing, what the normalization matched and why; never again maintain two drifting documents for one form.
**JTBD**: "When I maintain an intake form, I want to write each question once and have the product refuse to publish until both languages are genuinely complete, so bilingual quality is enforced by the tool instead of my memory." (paraphrase)
**Triggers**: the Arabic stapled translation drifting from a live English edit; a provider asking why patients write "وجع راس" when the chart vocabulary says "صداع"; a new colloquial phrase cluster from a new expatriate cohort.
**Frustrations**: placeholder-as-label forms patients abandon (research S8's seven harms); template libraries that translate labels but not options; vocabularies she cannot inspect — "smart" matching with no receipts; publish gates that accept half-translated forms silently.
**Objections + responses**: "Will the AI translate my form?" → no: AI never writes form copy (LOOP-10); the bilingual publish gate enforces human-authored copy in both locales, and the only assist surface (vocabulary matching) is confirm-gated (P15). "Keyboard-first or I won't maintain it." → full keyboard map for the builder and vocabulary manager (09), no raw-letter shortcuts (Arabic-layout conflicts, a11y-baseline §3). "What if the normalization is wrong?" → every match carries its mode and matched text; assist matches are drafts until she confirms; unmatched text is kept verbatim, never silently coerced (07c rules).

## Persona 3 — Tariq, Front-Desk / Intake Reviewer (reviewer, daily user)

**Role**: runs the review console at the same clinic: every submitted intake lands in his queue between phone calls; he confirms normalization matches, escalates red flags to the nurse line, chases incomplete submissions, and hands the provider the printed or on-screen package.
**Context**: works under arrival-time pressure; reads Arabic and English; the console is his second screen all day. The Qatar study's failure mode — "reliance on ad hoc interpreters" compromising "assessment accuracy and patient safety" (research S7) — is his Tuesday.
**Goals**: process a submission in under two minutes; never miss a red-flag term; know exactly what still needs his confirmation; get the provider's package without copy-paste.
**JTBD**: "When submissions land, I want the queue to show me flags, completion, and what needs confirmation before I open anything, so the two minutes I spend per intake goes to judgment, not triage of the queue itself." (paraphrase)
**Triggers**: an urgent flag on a chest-pain colloquialism he would have skimmed past in free text; an assist match pending confirmation blocking his "reviewed" state; a patient who abandoned mid-form (needs a resend decision); an export needed before the consult.
**Frustrations**: severity scores he cannot defend (the symptom-checker posture he distrusts — research S10/S17); PHI echoed in toast messages visible across the desk; queues that hide pending-confirmations; exports that arrive after the consult.
**Objections + responses**: "I don't want an AI telling me the patient is urgent." → the flag counts clinic-configured red-flag vocabulary terms and links the exact answers — counting, not prediction (00; clinic-appointment-desk's no-show-flag grammar). "My screen faces the waiting room." → PHI never appears in page titles or URLs; toasts carry counts and states, never answers; a shoulder-surfing mask mode is a recorded owner decision, default full-fidelity work surface (09 §privacy; 12 Q-12). "I review in Arabic, the physician reads English." → answers render bilingual with the patient's free text verbatim and the canonical term in both locales side by side (P23 columns).

## Persona 4 — Amira, the Patient (actor, not an account)

**Role**: 58, Arabic-first, phone-only, arrives at Huda's clinic with her daughter's phone number as her only "account"; describes her symptoms the way her family speaks — colloquial, mixed with a Latin drug name or two.
**Context**: at the clinic's door or at home with a link the receptionist sent; interrupted constantly (the NN/g interrupted-form-user reality, research S8); anxious in medical settings; reading glasses, large text preference.
**Goals**: finish intake in her own language without help; understand each question (health-literacy evidence — Arabic health-literacy studies are an active literature, research S20); stop and resume without losing work; know it actually submitted.
**JTBD**: "When I'm asked to fill an intake form before my appointment, I want to answer in Arabic at my own pace on my own phone and pick up where I stopped if I'm interrupted, so I don't perform my medical history at a counter under pressure." (paraphrase)
**Triggers**: a link that expires mid-completion; an English-only form she photographs and sends to her son; a symptom box where her phrase "doesn't fit"; a wall of 40 questions on one screen.
**Frustrations**: placeholder hints that vanish (research S8); forms that re-ask what she already answered (WCAG 3.3.7); losing everything on timeout; uncertainty about whether the clinic received it.
**Objections + responses (carried by the staff personas, since Amira is never sold to):** "Will it judge my symptoms?" → the form asks; it never assesses (LOOP-10). "My Arabic is dialect, not formal." → the normalization exists precisely for that: colloquial variants are in the clinic's vocabulary, the deterministic pipeline normalizes orthography, and unmatched phrasing is kept verbatim for a human (07c).

## Voice derivation (SME-J)

Clinic-floor voice: **plain, unhurried, concrete** — the register NN/g's evidence argues for on forms (plain language, no blame, next action always present — research S22) and the health-literacy literature supports for Arabic patients (research S20). English: professional-neutral, contractions off (specialized family, copy-voice §4). Arabic: فصحى بيضاء (plain MSA) — a utility surface for anxious readers, never وسطى flourish in chrome, and questions written for a general reader (short sentences, no compound clinical clauses). Warmth lives in success and empty states only; errors state the field, the reason, and the fix (copy-voice §1.4/§6). Fixed terminology (locked across 07a/11; one concept, one term — copy-voice §3): استمارة القبول=intake form (استمارة alone never used for a form kind) · قسم=section · سؤال=question · حقل=field · اختيار واحد=select-one · اختيار متعدد=select-many · حقل الأعراض=symptom field · مسرد الأعراض=symptom vocabulary · مصطلح=term · صيغة عامية=colloquial variant · مطابقة=match · تطبيع=normalization · مطابقة قيد التأكيد=pending confirmation · غير مطابق=unmatched · إشارة عاجلة=urgent flag · تصعيد=escalation · مراجعة=review · تسليم=submit · محفوظة تلقائياً=autosaved · استئناف=resume · تصدير=export · رمز QR مطبوع=printed QR. Patient-side copy avoids staff nouns entirely (she never sees "review console"). Numerals Western per ledger row (dates, counts, pain-scale values — a data surface, design-language-map §7); Arabic strings run ~125% of English length — layout budgets for it (10) and the 11 matrix lists both strings verbatim (copy-voice §5). Health-data copy rule (a11y-baseline §7 healthcare overlay): no answer text is ever echoed in toasts, announcements, titles, or URLs — success copy carries counts and states only.
