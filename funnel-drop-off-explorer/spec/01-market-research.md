## 1. Category frame

Product analytics is a large, consolidating category whose funnel reports are commodity infrastructure. The evidence is structural, not rhetorical: Mixpanel's docs state "The Funnels report is available on all free and paid subscription plans" (q4); Heap's plan comparison lists Funnels as included in every tier (q6); Amplitude gates only the *advanced* funnel features ("A/B Test Funnel View and Conversion Drivers are available on the Growth and Enterprise plans. User funnels is available on all plans" — q8). Every vendor answers *where* users drop off; the craft of answering *which users and why* is, in the retrieved corpus, a thin layer of chart readouts ("Largest drop-off step", "Slowest conversion step" — q20) plus a manual hand-off ("Save the dropped-off users as a cohort to target, or dig deeper into where they went using the Journeys chart" — q11). The wedge this product owns is that hand-off: the seam between the funnel chart and the segment explanation, today bridged by a data analyst with a notebook. The category's own consolidation proves the demand for approachability — June onboarded "more than 1,000 startups" to a "warm and welcoming" analytics product before selling to Amplitude (q12) — and equally proves that approachability alone, without a defensible analytic surface, is a fragile position at the bottom of the market ("it's hard to start from the bottom end of the market and grow into an enterprise product without extensive funding" — q12). The counter-position: not a simpler funnel chart, but a deeper drop-off instrument.

## 3. Indirect competitors (4)

- **The SQL analyst + notebook against the raw event store** (the incumbent behind every "let me check with data"): full flexibility, zero governance, answers arrive in days. This product must beat the notebook on the analyst's OWN ergonomics — the anneal must be faster than writing the group-by, and the iteration trace must be more trustworthy than a pasted query (00 success signal 1; 02 Omar's JTBD).
- **The generic BI dashboard stack** (chart-first tools where a funnel is one widget among many): they answer "what does the funnel look like," not "which segment explains the leak"; the widget-wall posture is precisely what our canvas IA rejects (04).
- **Avo-class taxonomy governance tooling** (q13): solves the dictionary problem in a standalone product with review workflows and drift inspection; the exploration gap remains — governance without an analysis surface leaves the funnel chart exactly where it was.
- **The spreadsheet + raw export** (status quo of small teams): counts pasted into a sheet, pivot tables by hand, screenshots into chat. Zero reproducibility, zero evidence chain; our saved analyses with parameters and provenance are the direct counter (07a Analysis entity).

## 4. Demand evidence (defensible, cited)

- **Market size and growth**: "The product analytics market size is expected to grow from USD 11.39 billion in 2025 to USD 13.04 billion in 2026 and is forecast to reach USD 25.73 billion by 2031 at 14.55% CAGR over 2026-2031" (Mordor Intelligence, q18 — the research firm's proprietary estimate, marked as such). Segment shape from the same page: solutions 71.60% of 2025 share; cloud 87.60%; large enterprises 60.18%; "retail and e-commerce held 29.40% revenue in 2025"; North America 42.35%; Asia-Pacific fastest at 20.40% CAGR.
- **Funnels are commodity**: q4 ("available on all free and paid subscription plans"), q6 (Funnels included in every Heap tier), q3/q8 (user funnels on every Amplitude plan). Implication: the purchase decision cannot be "has funnels" — it must be "explains drop-off."
- **The explanation gap is documented vendor behavior**: Amplitude's own quick guide routes the why-question through a human/agent hand-off ("Read the steepest step, then ask Global Agent a follow-up to understand why. Suggested prompt: 'Why did conversion drop?'" — q11); the interpret docs define the ceiling of shipped craft as two readouts ("Largest drop-off step… the absolute decrease, and not the percentage decrease"; "Slowest conversion step… the longest median time to transition" — q20).
- **Path exploration exists as a separate instrument**: "User paths are a type of insight that enable you to follow users along their journey through your product and determine where the biggest drop-offs are… Paths can be overwhelming at first, especially if they have many steps" (PostHog, q19) — the overwhelm admission is our seam-level integration argument.
- **Taxonomy discipline is real and monetized**: naming-convention rules published by Mixpanel ("Object-Action convention", q9) and Amplitude ("[Noun] + [Past-Tense Verb]… consistent capitalization… consistent actor", q10; "A solid taxonomy keeps analyses consistent, prevents data gaps, and avoids costly schema rework later" — q10); a governance vendor with delivery-speed case studies (q13, marked vendor claim).
- **Data-quality anxiety is documented**: "Frontend event tracking (especially on the web) loses between 15% and 30% of events" (Mixpanel docs, q9) — the honesty posture (source freshness chips, coverage notes) is a demand response, not decoration.
- **Approachability demand is proven; its fragility is proven**: June's 1,000+ onboarded startups and Amplitude absorption (q12).

## 5. Positioning gaps (attack map)

1. **Nobody ships the drop-off explanation engine.** The retrieved craft ceiling is chart readouts (q20) + cohort save + a separate paths chart (q11/q19). → The anneal with published separation math, support floors, and full iteration traces is the headline surface.
2. **Taxonomy governance is a separate purchase (Avo) or a docs page (Mixpanel/Amplitude).** → The bilingual governed dictionary inside the exploration product closes the loop: lint before analysis, usage counts on every event, deprecation with funnel-impact checks.
3. **Exploration posture is buried under query builders and chart switchers.** Funnel build flows are documented as module-by-module chart configuration (q8: Events Module + Segmentation Module; q2: sidebar → + New Insights → Funnel dropdown). → The seam is the unit of work: compose once, select a gap, drill — canvas IA, not dashboard IA (04).
4. **Bilingual (EN/AR) analytics surfaces are absent from the retrieved set.** Every console in the corpus is English-only; regional platform ecosystems market analytics pain in Arabic (portfolio-level evidence; this corpus's honest gap, §9). → bilingual-en direction with Arabic full-citizen taxonomy labels and normalized Arabic search (LOOP-6 proposal; 05 direction model).
5. **Event-metered pricing taxes the wrong resource for an exploration tool.** Every anchor meters events (q1/q3/q5) or MAU (q7) or sessions (q6). → this product meters neither: it binds existing sources and prices the exploration desk (Q-3 posture, 12).
6. **Determinism and evidence-grade reproducibility are unsold.** Saved charts re-run against drifting data with no parameter provenance. → analyses are reproducible evidence: parameters + engine versions + iteration traces, exportable as bilingual artifacts (07a/07c).
