## What it is

An exploration canvas for conversion funnels, built for growth teams who already have event data and do not have answers. Every product analytics vendor on the market ships a funnel report (01 §2 — funnels are table stakes, included on every plan tier of every major competitor), and every one of them buries the interesting part behind a query builder: pick events, get a bar chart, read the steepest bar, save a cohort, go ask a data analyst why. This product owns the part after the bar chart. The analyst composes a funnel from a **bilingual event taxonomy** (a governed dictionary of canonical event names with English and Arabic display labels, declared properties, owner, status, and a lint report that enforces the naming conventions the vendors themselves publish — 01 §3, q9/q10/q13), the funnel renders as **The Cascade** (06/13 — the product's signature visualization: each step a stratum, conversions flowing down in chartreuse, drop-offs shearing off as visible lost flow), and then the real work begins: selecting a **seam** — the gap between two steps where users leak — and exploring it. Seam exploration is three instruments with one grammar: **breakdowns** (any taxonomy property axis sliced against the seam, sorted by separation), **displaced paths** (what the lost users did instead — the path-exploration precedent, 01 §3, q19), and time-to-convert distributions (median and p90 per transition, the "slowest conversion step" craft, 01 §3, q20).

The flagship mechanic is **segment annealing**: a deterministic, inspectable greedy search that explains a drop-off. Given a seam and its baseline drop-off rate, the anneal tests candidate predicates from the taxonomy's segmentable property axes, admits the predicate whose segment maximizes a published separation score (excess drop-off times the square root of coverage, with a minimum-support floor, an improvement epsilon, and a depth cap — exact formulas, weights, and the full iteration trace in 07c §2.2), and iterates until no candidate improves the chain. The result is a **segment chain** — an ordered conjunction like `tenure = new ∧ platform = web` — rendered as the **Anneal Spine** (06/13) with every iteration visible: what was tested, what was admitted, what was rejected, and how many excess drop-offs the final segment explains. The anneal is not a black box and not a guess: it is the same inspectable-math posture shift-swap-marketplace brought to fairness scores and cod-order-confirmation brought to RTO risk (pattern kinship recorded in 12), applied to the question every growth team actually asks — *which users, exactly, are we losing here, and how do we know*. Chains convert to saved segments, cohorts with user-level drill (pseudonymous keys only — 09 privacy posture), side-by-side comparisons, and narrated analyses.

The AI stance is **conditionally-AI** (declared in 07a/07b/08): exactly one surface — drop-off narration assist — may be enriched by an abstract model capability when a workspace binds one; when unbound, rate-limited, or failed, the narration degrades totally and instantly to raw segment metrics composed from deterministic bilingual templates, with engine provenance (`template-metrics` vs `model-assisted`) displayed on every narrative (ai-native-patterns §8). No other surface reads a model: the anneal engine, the funnel computation, the taxonomy lint, and every breakdown are deterministic functions whose inputs and outputs are published in this spec. The product deliberately ends at the analysis boundary (LOOP-10): it stores no raw events (the adapter binds an existing event source, or a paste-imported bounded sample — 08 N1), it has no session replay (roster exclusion — the displaced-paths ribbon answers "what did they do instead" at the aggregate level, without replaying anyone's session), and it does not manage experiments (no A/B test authoring, allocation, or significance math — an analysis may be *compared* across date ranges, which is not an experiment).

## Unique feature

**Segment-annealed drop-off exploration with bilingual event taxonomy.** Three interlocking mechanisms, all inspectable:

1. **The anneal as published math, not a magic score.** Competitor evidence (01 §3): the closest public craft is Amplitude's "Largest drop-off step" chart readout (absolute decrease, not percentage — q20) and Mixpanel's save-dropped-users-as-cohort (q11); nobody ships an engine that searches the property space for the segment that explains the leak, shows the full iteration trace, and states its support floors. The anneal's separation score, support floor, epsilon, and depth cap are versioned policy data (07a), rendered as the same evidence the analyst sees (07c §2.2 exact trace).
2. **The taxonomy as a governed, bilingual first-class entity.** Taxonomy discipline is a published discipline with its own governance vendors (Avo — 01 §2, q13) and documented naming conventions (Mixpanel q9, Amplitude q10 — `[Noun] + [Past-Tense Verb]`, Title Case, consistent actor, consistent capitalization because `Song Played` and `song played` are two events). Here the dictionary lives inside the exploration product: canonical names, EN+AR display labels, declared properties with types, owner, status lifecycle (proposed → active → deprecated), usage counts, and a lint report that catches convention violations and bilingual label gaps before they become analysis garbage. Bilingual-en direction (LOOP-6 proposal): English-first console, Arabic a full citizen — the taxonomy serves growth teams where the analyst works in English and the growth lead reads Arabic (02 persona split), and where event display labels must render correctly in both.
3. **Exploration, not reporting.** The displaced-paths ribbon ("where they went instead") integrates the path-exploration precedent (PostHog user paths, q19) directly into the drop-off seam; the seam is the unit of work, not the funnel; and every exploration artifact (segment chain, comparison, breakdown) is savable as evidence with its parameters — an analysis is reproducible by construction, because the deterministic engine recomputes it from the same inputs (07c §5).

## Exclusions (narrow scope, enforced by LOOP-10)

- **No warehouse storage** — the product stores its own specs (funnels, segments, cohorts, analyses, taxonomy) and computes over bound event sources (08 N1); it never becomes an event store. Raw event retention, ingestion pipelines, and event volume pricing belong to the bound source (adapter binds — roster exclusion verbatim).
- **No session replay** — no player, no recording, no per-session video. Displaced paths answer the aggregate "what did they do instead" (q19 precedent); individual session replay is a roster exclusion and an adjacent vendors' product.
- **No experiment management** — no A/B authoring, allocation, exposure assignment, or significance testing. Time-range comparison of the same funnel is analysis, not experimentation; experiment tooling is excluded (adjacent vendors own it — 01 §2).
- **No identity resolution** — user keys arrive pseudonymous from the bound source; the product never merges identities, never stores emails/phones/names, and never claims a cross-device identity graph (09 privacy posture; the adapter's identity model is upstream).
- **No predictive modeling** — no churn prediction, no forecasting, no ML scoring of users. The anneal explains observed drop-off; it does not predict it. The single conditionally-AI surface (narration) describes what the deterministic engines already computed.
- **No dashboards/alerting** — no scheduled report emails, no threshold alerts, no widget walls (kpi-alert-watch owns the alert console — LOOP-3, 12 Q-8). The product's ambient surface is a digest inbox for collaboration events, not a monitoring system.

## Success signals (spec-level definition of done)

Against the Mock Adapter, with zero provider knowledge anywhere in the UI:

1. **A first insight in under 10 minutes**: a new analyst pastes a bounded event sample (LOOP-5 manual path, 08 N1), composes a 5-step funnel from taxonomy search, sees the Cascade render, selects the largest seam, runs the anneal, and reads the segment chain with its full iteration trace — no notebook, no data-team request, no query builder beyond step selection (P22 onboarding milestones).
2. **The full evidence journey on the Mock**: funnel composed → cascade (400 → 348 → 271 → 185 → 121; overall conversion 30.25%) → largest seam selected (First Search → Add to Cart, 86 lost of 271, 31.7%) → anneal run → chain `[tenure = new ∧ platform = web]` (60 users, 60.0% drop-off, +28.3 points above baseline, ~17 excess drop-offs, two admitted iterations, one rejected for no-improvement) → narration (template-metrics provenance, unbound model) → segment saved → cohort drilled to pseudonymous user keys → analysis saved and exported as the bilingual evidence artifact — replayed deterministically twice with byte-identical state (07c §5).
3. **The taxonomy governance loop in one sitting**: lint report read (naming violations + bilingual label gaps + a deprecated event still referenced by a saved funnel), the missing Arabic label fixed, the violating event renamed with the diff previewed against its usage counts, the deprecated reference resolved — every funnel that touches a taxonomy change sees the impact before it commits (CP-4).
4. **The honesty tests**: with the narration capability unbound, the analysis surface renders the same evidence with template-metrics narration and provenance named; with every abstract capability unbound (source binding, narration, artifact destination), the product remains a complete exploration desk on the paste-imported sample — full anneal, full breakdowns, full taxonomy (LOOP-5 bar, 08).
5. **The keyboard-speed test**: a working analyst moves seam-to-seam, opens breakdowns, runs the anneal, and compares segments entirely from the keyboard (j/k seams, B breakdown, A anneal, C compare — 09) with every state change announced per the live-region schedule and zero color-only carriers anywhere in the Cascade (09, a11y-baseline §4).
