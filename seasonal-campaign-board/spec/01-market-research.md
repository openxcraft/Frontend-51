# 01 — Market Research: seasonal-campaign-board

SME-A pass · URLs retrieved 2026-10-16 via live web search (raw results in research/batch3/scb-q1…q9.json). Figures appearing only in search snippets are marked "unverified estimate"; nothing below is invented.

## Category overview

The category is a collision of three tool families, none of which serves the buyer's actual job. **Marketing calendar software** (CoSchedule, Loomly, Opal, Planable, Uptempo, monday-marketing) plans channel content against weeks and months — Latin-first, campaign-as-container, no concept of a *retail moment* with Hijri dates, drift, or phase windows. **Task boards** (Quire-class, template checklists from Aha!/Manifestly/Jodoo/Puntt) hold launch checklists with owners and due dates — but as static documents detached from any calendar, any countdown, and any season memory. **Regional storefront platforms** (Salla/Zid-class merchant tooling) power Saudi e-commerce — merchandising and payments, not marketing team readiness. The buyer's real workflow — "Ramadan is 8 weeks out; here is the checklist; who owns what; are we ready" — runs on spreadsheets, shared docs, and group chat (the Airtable seven-step manual method [12] is the honest status quo).

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **Airtable/Notion DIY seasonal calendars**: the default competitor — teams build moment tables and checklist bases by hand [12]; free but effortful, dies with its author, no drift awareness.
- **General workspaces + spreadsheets**: the true status quo for most MENA retail marketing teams; zero season semantics, zero coupling.
- **Agency account managers**: outsourced seasonal planning — high cost, the ceiling above us; the board is the in-house alternative for teams below agency retainers.
- **Platform-native campaign schedulers** (storefront/marketing automation add-ons inside commerce platforms): bundled but shallow — schedule a discount, not a readiness process [13].

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- Ramadan is a documented region-wide retail surge: Grand View Research positions the month as consistently triggering significant region-wide consumer spending increases [14]; byyd.me aggregates category spend lifts (groceries, gifts, food, clothing, entertainment) [15].
- Ramadan economics have a *shape*: nighttime economy with promotions peaking in the last 10 days before Eid (Infobip [16]; Visa insight [17]); app downloads surge 36.5% during Ramadan with F&B growth starting *weeks before* (GoDaddy AE [18]) — evidence for build-up phase windows, not just a start date.
- Eid al-Fitr behaves like a second peak: 39% of surveyed consumers planned increased Eid-gift spending (Adgully, snippet figure, unverified estimate [19]); GoDaddy AE documents the gift/clothing/food/decoration shift [20]; Eid al-Adha 2026 GCC e-commerce expected +10% (Arabian Business, snippet figure, unverified estimate [21]).
- White Friday is the KSA commerce event: Saudi shoppers spend up to 3x more during White Friday week (Netcore, snippet claim, unverified estimate [22]); KSA e-commerce market scale and growth documented by BCG (~60% average annual online sales growth, earlier period [23]), trade.gov (34.5M users expected by end-2025 [24]), and MarkNtel (USD 19.29B 2025 → 21.29B 2026, snippet figures, unverified estimate [25]).
- National days are brand-moment obligations: 91% of Snapchatters expect brands to inspire them during celebration periods (Snapchat for Business, snippet figure [26]); Almarai's Saudi National Day promoted video and Eyewa's "Saudi National Day 95" campaign are exemplar activations [27][28]; UAE Eid Al Etihad campaign guidance exists as a genre [29].
- Timing discipline is the pain: businesses start planning months ahead (GoDaddy AE [18]); Muslim consumer behavior shifts weeks before Ramadan (Muslim Ad Network [30]); the pre-Ramadan build-up window is explicitly named in GCC planning guides (The Grower: Feb 5–18 build-up for a ~Feb 19 Ramadan 2026 [31]); expected start dates drift by a day (Right Media AE [32]).
- Readiness checklists are established practice: 48-point and 7-point launch checklists circulate as templates [8][9][10][33] — none countdown-linked, none moment-aware.

## Pricing landscape

Category norm is per-seat: CoSchedule free ≤3 members then $15/user/mo up; ~$19 Social Calendar and ~$59 Agency (annual) [1][2][3]. Enterprise suites are custom-quote [34]. DIY is free-with-effort. Recommended posture (12 Q-3): per-seat planner pricing in the $12–18 band with the moment library included (it is the differentiator, not an upsell) and a light export quota — see 12.

## Positioning statement

For MENA retail marketing teams, seasonal-campaign-board is the readiness layer that turns the moment calendar into countdown-linked checklists — unlike marketing calendar tools (which plan channel content, not seasonal readiness) and unlike static launch checklists (which float free of any calendar), the board couples a curated dual-Hijri/Gregorian moment library with phase windows and drift disclosure to per-item T-minus due dates, and closes the season loop by carrying closure notes into next season's templates.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns, no verbatim quotes)

- Category reviews and comparison threads (q3/q8 raw results): teams report calendar tools becoming content-scheduling sprawl — the *season* disappears into channel rows (paraphrase pattern).
- Ramadan planning guides repeatedly open with "start early / avoid the last-minute rush" framing [16][18][30] — evidence the timing pain is chronic, not niche.
- Hijri date drift is a named hazard in regional guidance [32] — spreadsheets hard-code a date and break; drift disclosure + adjustable go-live is the answer.
- Checklist template content circulates as static docs [8][9][10][33] — teams rebuild ownership and due dates by hand every season; closure lessons are not captured anywhere.

## Market size (honest)

Bottom-up: MENA retail marketing teams above DIY and below enterprise suites. No public sizing exists for "seasonal readiness boards" as a category; per-seat anchors ($15–59/user/mo, 3–12 seats [1][2][3]) imply a $540–8,500/yr per-team envelope — unverified estimate from public list prices, not a market study. The demand engine (Ramadan/Eid/White Friday/national-day spend evidence above) is documented; the tool category capturing it is not, which is the opportunity and the risk both.
