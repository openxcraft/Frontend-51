# 00 — Brief: customs-tariff-lookup

Family: specialized · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

An HS-code navigator for import/export desks working GCC-bound trade. The product's object is the **tariff nomenclature itself**: the hierarchical tree of sections, chapters, headings, subheadings, and national tariff lines, rendered bilingually — the international 6-digit core the World Customs Organization maintains (research digest: over 5,600 commodity groups, 6-digit codes, English and French the official languages) plus the national extensions a desk actually clears against (the GCC integrated tariff runs to 12 digits; national editions amend descriptions on their own annual cadence). A desk officer searches "دجاج مجمد" or "frozen chicken", or walks the tree chapter by chapter, lands on a tariff line, and reads the line as a complete reference card: the code in tabular mono digits, the English description, the Arabic national rendering (with its absence honestly flagged where the schedule publishes no Arabic side — never auto-translated), the published customs-duty reference for the active schedule edition, and the ruling-notes library attached to that heading.

That library is the product's second object and its defensibility. Mature customs regimes publish classification rulings and interpretation notes — advance rulings, committee opinions, explanatory-note passages — and practitioners defend their code choices by citing them (research digest: the US CROSS database, Canadian and Japanese advance-ruling systems; the WCO's own Explanatory Notes and Classification Opinions). No surveyed tool attaches that evidence layer to the browsed code, and none of it is bilingual. customs-tariff-lookup curates ruling-notes as first-class records — reference number, issuing authority kind, issue date, goods description in both languages where the source carries both, the outcome code, a summary, a supersession chain — linked to the headings they interpret, searchable on their own, and linkable to the desk's own saved codes as classification rationale. The deliverable that leaves the product is an **evidence pack**: the desk's saved codes with product descriptions, chosen lines, duty references, and the linked ruling-notes, exported as a shareable artifact for the licensed broker, the finance team, or the audit file.

The classification decision itself is the desk's, never the product's. AI stance (ai-native-patterns §1): **conditionally-AI** — a bilingual search **assist** maps a free-text product description to candidate headings with provenance and confidence, and it degrades to the deterministic spine — hierarchical browse plus normalized bilingual keyword search over the indexed nomenclature — which is always available, always primary, and complete on its own (degradation map in 08). Every assist suggestion is draft-by-default and never auto-saves (P15). Duty figures are **published reference data, never guarantees**: the product shows the schedule's statutory rate and its structure (ad valorem percent, or specific duty per unit), and explicitly refuses to compute or promise what a customs authority will assess — the exclusion is written into the adapter contract as a rule, not just a disclaimer (07b rule 2). The product ends at the reference and its evidence; it files nothing, brokers nothing, and guarantees nothing.

## Buyer

Import/export desks at GCC-trading companies and distributors: the trade-compliance specialist who defends code choices when a customs query lands; the import coordinator who needs the right code and its Arabic rendering for clearance paperwork; the procurement lead evaluating a new product line's duty treatment before finance runs landed-cost numbers. Multi-seat team tool — the value compounds when the desk shares saved classification lists and a common evidence base across an office, so pricing posture is per-seat (12 Q-3). Not for licensed customs brokers' filing operations (excluded), and not a consumer "find my code" widget — the desk buyer lives in the nomenclature daily, not once per parcel.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px navigator with persistent tree rail + reading pane; responsive to 360px in read/review modes). Zones: nomenclature home (search-first entry); hierarchy navigator (tree browse, the deterministic spine); tariff-line reading pane (code card, bilingual descriptions, duty reference, linked notes); ruling-notes library (filterable list + note detail); saved classification lists (the desk's working memory with rationale and status); schedule/version surfaces (active edition switch, edition change log); activity/jobs console (T3-consolidated per async-contract §7); settings (locale, assist default, normalization disclosure); ⌘K command palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three). No mobile app; no public API at v1; no offline nomenclature claim at v1 (offline queueing applies to saved-list writes only, P20).

## Unique feature

Bilingual HS-code navigation with a ruling-notes library. Defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the official interpretive stack is English/French (WCO official languages — research digest), and every free or commercial lookup surveyed is Latin-first with Arabic as an afterthought at best — while the desk's clearance reality is bilingual by regulation (UAE import invoices must be presented in Arabic and English; Arabic national schedules exist); (2) the ruling/interpretation-note layer exists as scattered public databases and expensive enterprise content (research digest), not as a navigable library attached to the codes a desk browses; (3) national tariff editions change on their own cadence (Saudi schedule amended December 2025, platform tariff-code update January 2026 — research digest), and no surveyed tool reconciles a desk's saved codes across edition changes — this product makes version reconciliation a first-class flow (03 CP-5).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No customs filing, declaration submission, clearance, or any government-portal integration: the product is a reference and evidence workspace; the licensed broker or the declarant files.
- No brokerage network, broker marketplace, or broker communication: the evidence pack is exported as an artifact; what the desk does with their broker is their business.
- No duty calculation guarantees, no landed-cost calculators, no total-duty computation, no "you will pay" figures: duty surfaces show the published schedule rate as reference with its structure and source edition, plus an explicit no-guarantee contract (07b rule 2); landed-cost assembly belongs to other tools (and freight-rate comparison is a sibling product's lane — 12 Q-4).
- No automatic classification: the assist proposes candidates with provenance; a human desk officer selects and takes responsibility for every saved code (P15 trust checkpoint; status model candidate → desk-verified).
- No product-catalog management, no purchase-order or invoice documents, no HS-code-on-invoice fields: saved lists are classification working memory, not commercial documents (invoice adjacency judged in 12 Q-5).
- No consumer parcel lookup surface, no shipment tracking, no restricted-party screening, no origin-management or FTA qualification, no customs duty refunds/objections workflow.
- No AI generation of legal text: the assist is confined to bilingual search/candidate suggestion; ruling-note summaries in the library are curated reference records, not generated prose.

## Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done (success signals)

A first-session desk officer can: pick the KSA 2026 schedule edition as active; search "frozen chicken" and the Arabic "دجاج مجمد" and reach the poultry heading through both queries; walk Section I → Chapter 02 → the poultry heading via the tree when search is too noisy; open the tariff line and read code, English description, Arabic rendering, and the 5%-fixture duty reference with its no-guarantee marker; open a linked ruling note and read its outcome code; save the line to a classification list with a product description; link a second ruling note as rationale; run checkCodeChanges against the prior edition and see the drone heading flagged as newly added; export the evidence pack (manual download, zero credentials); flip the workspace locale to Arabic and read every surface in RTL chrome — all on the Mock Adapter, with the assist capability switched off, zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI, and the full flow completing on the deterministic spine alone.
