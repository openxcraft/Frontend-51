## What it is

An analysis workspace for survey open-ends, built for research teams whose fieldwork is already done. Every survey in the world ends the same way: a column of numbers the team can tabulate in minutes, and a wall of open-ended answers nobody has time to read. Practitioner sources put manual coding at 2–5 minutes per response [01 q13] — 500 responses is a week of analyst time; one large study is "150+ hours" [01 q13] to "200–300 hours" [01 q13]; whole vendors exist to sell the acceleration (Displayr, Caplena, Veridata-class, 01). This product is the analysis-side instrument for that wall, and it deliberately starts *after* collection: responses arrive by paste or file upload from whatever survey tool the team already ran (LOOP-10 — no distribution, no panel management), as rows of verbatim text with optional metadata columns (city, channel, plan, satisfaction score — the segments the team already thinks in).

The engine posture is the portfolio's inspectable-math discipline applied to qualitative work, and it is honest about the hardest fact in the domain: **Arabic**. Arabic NLP evidence is unambiguous that morphology and dialect variety make off-the-shelf text analytics unreliable for Arabic survey language — MSA-trained tools "may not work efficiently with dialectal Arabic," dialects differ country to country, dialect data is scarce, and dialect identification is a research task in its own right (01 q8/q10/q14). So the product is **conditionally-AI** with a declared deterministic floor: language-class detection (MSA / dialect-leaning / mixed-script, by a disclosed marker rule — never silently normalized), tone signal (a disclosed three-class lexicon heuristic, labeled as such), and **keyword-frequency grouping** — normalized per rtl-rules §4, stopword-aware, threshold-based — which carries the whole workday when the abstract response-clustering capability is unbound, rate-limited, or fails. When a clustering capability IS bound and consented, it proposes richer themes — and every proposal lands as `proposed` with a confidence band, confirmed only through a human gate (P15); nothing auto-applies. Every theme carries engine provenance (`keyword-grouping` | `model-proposed` | `human-confirmed`) on its face, because a research deliverable that cannot say how its themes were made is not a deliverable.

The artifact the product exists to produce is the **theme set with quotes**: clusters of open-ended responses, each named bilingually (Arabic and English labels — the report audience is MENA), each carrying its member count and share, each grounded in **theme quotes** — verbatim representative responses selected by a published deterministic rule (theme-keyword coverage, then length, then stable tie-break — exact formula in 07c), which the researcher can override by pinning a better quote with her name on the change (activity log, P17). The methodology spine is Braun & Clarke's six-phase thematic analysis (01 q7) — the product operationalizes phases 3–6 at survey scale (searching/generating themes, reviewing, defining/naming bilingually, reporting with evidence), while the 2022 good-practice caution [01 q7] is designed in, not bolted on: the tool never claims to replace the analyst's judgment, it makes that judgment faster and auditable. Coverage honesty is structural: responses that match no theme stay visibly unassigned; responses carrying several stay in several — theme shares may sum past 100% and the product says so in writing rather than flattening the truth for a tidy chart.

## Buyer

Research teams (2–20 seats) who run bilingual fieldwork and own the reporting: **market-research agencies** delivering Arabic open-end coding to brand clients; **brand/CX insights teams** at MENA consumer apps, banks, telecoms, and retailers fielding CSAT/NPS/UX surveys; **public-sector and university research units** surveying citizens and students; **BPO/fieldwork shops** that code open-ends on behalf of clients. The economic buyer is the research or insights lead who has quoted a client a coding line-item measured in analyst-days and wants that line-item to shrink without the methodology defense weakening (02 Mariam). Daily users: the **research analyst** who codes and curates themes (today in a spreadsheet or a CAQDAS license built for interview transcripts, 02 Khalid); the **bilingual research associate** who reads Arabic verbatims and crafts theme names (02 Dana); the **stakeholder viewer** who reads the theme report (02 Faisal). Pricing recommendation (12 Q-3): analyst/lead seats priced, associate seats included cheaply, viewers free, model-assisted clustering runs metered within a generous window — the keyword floor is unlimited because the floor is the product's honesty, not an upsell.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px analysis surfaces; responsive to 360px in **reading** modes — theme reports, quote walls, coverage reads; theme curation and bulk coding are desktop/tablet). Zones: projects home (surveys with response counts, language mix, onboarding checklist); project overview (questions, import, response stats); the **theme board** — the core surface: theme cards with share bars and provenance chips beside a quote wall of representative verbatims; the **response reader** (virtualized verbatim list with detection chips — language class, tone, script mix — and full-text reading pane at each response's own direction); theme detail/editor (bilingual labels, definition, members, quotes, the confirm gate, merge composer); coverage & crosstab console (assigned/unassigned/overlap; theme × segment counts); intake (paste/upload with per-row detection preview); jobs center (import/clustering/export jobs); activity log (P17); settings (capability consent in capability language, usage window). ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1; no survey distribution anywhere in the product (LOOP-10).

## Unique feature

**Open-ended Arabic response clustering with theme quotes** — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **the Arabic floor is the category's blind side** — enterprise feedback analytics (Kapiche, Chattermill, Thematic) are English-first VoC platforms volume-gated for CX teams (Chattermill's own "not a fit" list starts at 5,000 feedback pieces/month, 01 q2), survey-platform text modules are plan-gated afterthoughts (SurveyMonkey, 01 q9), and CAQDAS workbenches are built for interview-transcript projects, not thousand-row survey columns (01 q6); nothing serves a research pod whose open-ends are Arabic, mixed-script, and dialect-laden; (2) **inspectable clustering** — the deterministic keyword-frequency floor with disclosed normalization, stopwords, and thresholds (the G2 review of Thematic names the exact differentiator buyers feel — theming "without us having to set up key word models" [01 q5] — our posture is the inverse and we say so); (3) **theme quotes as first-class evidence** — verbatim representative selection by published rule (07c) with human pinning, so every theme in the exported report carries its own proof; (4) **bilingual theme naming** as a structural act — every theme carries Arabic and English labels from creation, because the report's audience reads both (02 persona split), which no monolingual tool does by construction.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No survey distribution** — no sending, no links, no response collection, no quotas, no completion tracking. Fieldwork happens in whatever tool the team already runs; this product starts at the export (roster exclusion verbatim). Intake is paste/file-upload plus an abstract intake capability (08 N1).
- **No panel management** — no respondent sourcing, panel books, incentive handling, or sample balancing; respondents arrive as pseudonymous references in the import (09 privacy posture).
- **No conjoint analysis** — no choice-modeling, no utility estimation, no simulator; closed-question data arrives only as metadata columns for segmenting themes, and closed-question statistics (topline scores, NPS arithmetic) belong to the survey platform that collected them.
- **No topline/cross-tab statistics engine for closed questions** — the crosstab surface crosses *themes × metadata columns* only; significance testing, weighting, and sampling-error math are explicitly out (they are a different product's center).
- **No dashboards/alerting** — no scheduled digests of metric thresholds, no widget walls (kpi-alert-watch owns the alert console — LOOP-3, 12); the product's ambient surface is a jobs/activity feed for its own work, not a monitoring system.
- **No automatic theme application** — model-proposed themes are confirm-gated one theme at a time (P15); no bulk-accept, no silent re-clustering on live confirmed themes without a recorded run.
- **No respondent identity resolution** — respondent references stay pseudonymous verbatim from the import; no email/phone enrichment, no cross-survey identity merging (09).
- **No transcript/audio analysis at v1** — text open-ends only; interview and focus-group media belong to the CAQDAS/research-repository class (01).

## Success signals (spec-level definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

1. **A first theme set in under 10 minutes**: a new analyst pastes a response block (zero credentials, LOOP-5), watches per-row detection split MSA/dialect/mixed, runs the keyword-floor clustering, and reads a theme board with counts, shares, provenance, and quotes — no spreadsheet, no CAQDAS license, no coding manual.
2. **The evidence journey on the Mock**: 300-response seed question → detection mix (MSA/dialect/mixed with named markers) → keyword floor run → board renders (e.g., "الشحن والتوصيل" 31% + "التسعير" 22% + …) → representative quotes per published rule → one model-proposed theme confirmed through the gate → two duplicate themes merged with member counts named → one stray response manually assigned → one quote pinned with actor recorded → coverage read (assigned/unassigned/overlap stated) → crosstab by city → bilingual report exported with quotes and the coded dataset retrieved before artifact expiry — replayed deterministically twice with byte-identical state (07c).
3. **The honesty tests**: with the clustering capability unbound (the tested default state), the same board renders with `keyword-grouping` provenance and the floor's disclosure panel (stopword list, thresholds, normalization ruleset) one click away; a dialect-leaning response is never silently normalized — its chips name the markers that classified it; overlapping theme shares sum past 100% and the coverage panel says so.
4. **The craft loop**: Dana renames a theme bilingually, writes its definition, pins a quote, and the exported report carries theme labels in both scripts with the definition and quote beneath — the analyst's judgment, made faster and auditable (P17 activity trail on every mutation).
5. **The keyboard-speed test**: Khalid moves theme-to-theme, opens members, assigns a response, and pins a quote entirely from the keyboard (T next theme, ↓ members, A assign, P pin — 09) with every state change announced per the live-region schedule and zero color-only carriers anywhere on the board (09).
