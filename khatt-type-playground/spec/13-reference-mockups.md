## Original-direction sketch (prose)

**The proof-sheet bench.** The screen is a foundry proof sheet pinned to a drafting bench: warm paper (or night-shift ink), hairline rules instead of shadows, brass instrument accents. The left rail is the type drawer — families as self-demonstrating cards whose weight strips render in the family itself. The canvas is oversized and quiet: specimen blocks pinned like clippings, each a small typographic argument (headline pair, reading paragraph, numeral run, mixed line) with its settings printed in graphite microtext at the block's foot — provenance as typographic ornament. The kashida panel slides in from the inline-start edge like a brass rule sliding under the proof: a segmented mode control (Off · تطويل · jalt) and an intensity slider whose value ticks appear as small stretch marks on the justified block itself; beneath, a per-line readout table names the lines that stretched and warns where word shapes broke — the moment the product earns its keep is a designer seeing, in real time, what a font does to a justified line. The comparator pins a second session as a ghosted column behind the first — two proof sheets held to the light. Exports land as clipped sheets with a settings stamp in the corner: weights, scale, kashida mode, numerals — the artifact a committee can argue with by name. Nothing on screen moves except opacity; the drama is typographic, and it belongs to the fonts.

## Mood anchors

Graphite on proof-paper with brass instruments · hairline rules, cut corners · specimens pinned like evidence · settings printed at the block's foot · the quiet of a bench where the type is the loudest thing in the room.
