# 00 — Brief: price-rule-engine

Family: commerce_integrations · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A rules console for pricing leads at MENA commerce businesses — the person who owns the price file. The product's spine is a **catalog with a cost basis**: every priced item carries its landed cost as effective-dated components (supplier cost + freight + duty + fees + packaging), its current net price, its market (Saudi Arabia 15% VAT, UAE 5% VAT — rates are workspace configuration, not hardcode), and its channel. On top of that catalog runs a **deterministic rule set**: scoped conditions (category, channel, market, price band, margin band) and priced actions (set markup, set target margin, adjust by a percentage, clamp at a cost floor, round to an ending convention), evaluated in declared priority order. Nothing here watches competitors and nothing here reprices on its own — both exclusions are LOOP-10 records with grounding: competitor scraping sits on genuinely unsettled legal ground ("not per se illegal … has risks", Quinn Emanuel, 01), and algorithmic repricing execution carries live competition-law exposure (CMA/Skadden/MacKay, 01). The product's whole value is computed from the merchant's **own** numbers, which is exactly why its answers are defensible in an internal review.

The signature mechanism is the **cost-floor-aware rule simulation with margin preview**. A simulation runs the rule set against the catalog at a chosen cost snapshot and returns, per item and in aggregate: current net price → raw rule price → floor clamp (when the rule would price below the landed-cost floor, the clamp fires and the row says so — never silently) → ending rounding → proposed net, with margin-before/margin-after on every row, the VAT-inclusive display price per market, and a formula trace the pricing lead can read line by line. Simulation is read-only against the catalog — the same rule set, cost snapshot, and catalog always produce the same rows (the determinism contract; declared **non-AI**, ai-native-patterns §1: markup/margin arithmetic, clamping, and rounding are rules, and there is no demand-sensing, no elasticity guess, and no AI operation anywhere). From a simulation the pricing lead curates a **price proposal**: a reviewed subset of rows (floor-clamped rows excluded with reasons), which passes an approval gate that refuses while any row is unreviewed, and lands as an **exportable price-update package** — a price sheet plus an audit ledger — with an abstract publish channel declared for a binding-time future. At v1 the product never writes a price to any channel: the human approves, the artifact ships, the merchant's own systems apply it. That is the same deliberate stopping point the portfolio's other commerce tools take (cod-order-confirmation stops at the courier's door; this one stops at the approved price sheet).

The gap this occupies is between two categories that both refuse it. Competitor-monitoring/repricing tools (Prisync, Price2Spy class, 01) sell the loop we exclude: scrape the market, reprice against it, execute automatically — and their pricing pages say so. B2B price-management platforms (Zilliant, Intuilize class, 01) sell the pain we serve — margin erosion from delayed cost response, "silent margin leakage" in spreadsheets — at enterprise weight and integration depth. What no retrieved tool ships is the middle: a bilingual, VAT-aware, cost-floor-honest rules console where a two-to-eight-person commerce team can simulate a price decision against landed costs and read the margin consequence *before* committing — with the arithmetic shown, not summarized. The regional wedge is sharper: VAT-inclusive display is a consumer-protection expectation in KSA/UAE (the "599 sign, 628.95 till" complaint class, 01), so a MENA pricing tool must compute and preview the VAT-inclusive figure per market as a first-class column, and the Ramadan promotion season makes date-bounded markdown rules a structural need, not a nicety (01).

## Buyer

Pricing leads at MENA commerce businesses — D2C brands, multichannel sellers, and small retail chains running 100–5,000 SKUs across two to five channels and two markets (KSA/UAE at minimum): the person who answers for blended margin when supplier costs move, who today reworks a price spreadsheet by formula-fragments and defends it in a meeting with screenshots. The economic buyer is the e-commerce or commercial manager; daily users are the pricing lead (owns rules and simulations), the finance reviewer (owns the approval gate), and a viewer seat for the founder who reads margin previews without editing. Secondary: category managers who scope rules to their categories. This is a team tool with per-seat economics and a generous simulation allowance (12 Q-3) — not an enterprise pricing platform, and not a repricing automation (LOOP-10).

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px — the simulation table and rule editor are dense, keyboard-heavy work surfaces), responsive to 360px for read/monitor modes (simulation summaries, proposal status, job progress; row-level review is desktop/tablet). Zones: rules console home (rule list with status, priority, and validity windows); rule editor (conditions, actions, priority, window, with the deterministic formula rendered beside the form); catalog (items with cost, net price, margin, floor status); item detail (cost history timeline, price history, applied-rule trace); cost desk (bulk cost refresh by paste and per-item cost records); simulation workspace — the core surface: summary header (margin before/after, clamps, direction counts) over a virtualized before/after row table with per-row formula drawer; proposal review (row include/exclude with reasons, the gate); exports & jobs; settings (markets/VAT, floor defaults, ending conventions); activity log. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

**Cost-floor-aware rule simulation with margin preview** — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the cost floor is a clamp, not a warning — every action in the pipeline is evaluated against an effective-dated landed cost, and a rule that would price below floor is clamped with the row carrying the trace (raw price → floor → clamped), so a margin accident cannot slip through as a rounding artifact; (2) margin preview is arithmetic you can read — per-row margin-before/after computed from the same landed costs, blended margin as catalog-weighted aggregate explicitly labeled for what it is (no sales-velocity weighting at v1, no elasticity claims — honesty over theater); (3) the VAT-inclusive display column per market — KSA 15% / UAE 5% computed and previewed beside net, because that is the number the shopper actually sees and the number consumer-protection complaint classes are made of; (4) the simulation → proposal → gate → export chain: nothing touches a live price without a human-approved, auditable artifact, which is both a LOOP-10 boundary (no dynamic repricing execution) and the governance answer a pricing lead's boss actually wants.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No competitor scraping** — excluded on legal risk (unsettled legality, CFAA exposure, ToS conflicts — 01 §legal-risk band). No competitor price ingestion, no market-price feeds, no "match lowest" logic anywhere; rules compute from the merchant's own costs and prices only. The category exists and is honestly noted in 01; this product is not in it.
- **No dynamic repricing execution at v1** — no continuous automated price changes, no channel writes, no event-triggered repricing loops. The product produces simulations, human-approved proposals, and export artifacts; an abstract "price publish channel" is declared contract-compatible for a binding-time decision (08) with the export/download path as the permanent manual alternative (LOOP-5). Competition-law exposure of algorithmic repricing is the recorded rationale (01), not just scope caution.
- **No price A/B tests** — no split-price experiments, no variant assignment, no conversion measurement. Excluded per roster; price-discrimination optics and experimentation cost are noted in 12, not re-litigated here.
- **No sales/analytics ingestion** — no order history, no demand signals, no velocity weighting; margins are catalog-weighted and labeled so. (analytics family owns analysis; exports are the interop.)
- **No payment processing, no ledger/accounting** — the product prices; it does not charge, post, or reconcile. invoice-generator-desk's family owns the money document (LOOP-3 mirror, 12).
- **No listing content management** — titles, descriptions, images, and channel field mapping belong to marketplace-listing-sync (LOOP-3 mirror, 12); this product owns price and cost data only, and hands off via export.
- **No AI anywhere** — declared non-AI: rule evaluation, clamping, rounding, VAT arithmetic, and margin computation are deterministic (07a/07b/07c); no generation, no suggestions, no demand prediction, no sparkle affordances.

## Success signals (definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

A pricing lead can: import a bilingual catalog by paste (zero credentials, LOOP-5); record a landed cost with components and watch the margin column fill; author one scoped rule (target margin + ending convention) and read the deterministic formula rendered beside it; run a simulation and read the aggregate margin preview plus per-row before/after with the VAT-inclusive display price; watch a floor clamp fire on a row that would price below cost and read the trace (raw 84.50 → floor 96.14 → clamped); refresh costs by paste (the vendor-increase moment), re-simulate, and see the margin erosion quantified; curate a proposal excluding clamped rows with reasons; hit the approval gate and be refused with a named unreviewed row, then pass it; export the price-update package and retrieve the artifact before expiry — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full loop completing with every abstract capability unbound.
