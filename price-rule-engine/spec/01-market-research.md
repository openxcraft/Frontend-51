## Category overview — and the category we refuse

Three product classes surround this product. **Competitor price monitoring + dynamic repricing tools** (Prisync, Price2Spy, Pricefy, Dealavo class) watch competitor prices and stock and reprice automatically — Prisync's own pitch: "Track competitor prices and stock 24/7, then reprice automatically with rules you control" [58]. **Amazon repricers** (Seller Snap, Aura/BQool class) are execution engines — "AI, automated, and rule-based options" for FBA/FBM sellers [5], Seller Snap sells "game theory tactics" auto-repricing [6]. **B2B/enterprise price-management platforms** (Zilliant, Intuilize, PriceShape class) attack the margin-erosion pain — "Manual, spreadsheet-based pricing creates silent margin leakage" [54]; Intuilize benchmark research reports "1.6% average margin erosion from delayed response to vendor cost increases, and up to 6.0%" [53 — vendor benchmark, unverified estimate] — at enterprise integration weight. price-rule-engine is deliberately the fourth thing: an inward-computed rules console. It ingests no competitor data and executes no price writes; it computes price decisions from the merchant's own landed costs with floor clamps and margin previews, and ships human-approved artifacts.

**Why refusing the first category is a grounded product decision, not a gap.** Competitor scraping sits on genuinely unsettled legal ground: "While scraping is not per se illegal, it has risks. In the United States, there is no single legal or regulatory framework that governs scraping" (Quinn Emanuel) [10]; Imperva frames legality as "depends on who you ask" with CFAA exposure [11]; Pricefy's own legal page answers "Yes, but with some caveats" for e-commerce sites [12]; Datahut documents the defining legal battles and the "legal grey area" resellers operate in [13]. And the execution half of that category carries live competition-law exposure: the UK CMA publishes explicit guidance on "pricing algorithms and competition law" [48]; Skadden warns algorithmic pricing may expose companies to "civil damages" and criminal liability [46]; European enforcement "starts to bite" [50]; MacKay's research shows algorithmic pricing can raise consumer prices "even in the absence of collusion" [51]; Spann et al. flag regulatory concerns including "the legality of price discrimination" [52]. A MENA merchant tool that avoids both risks by computing from the merchant's own cost truth is not a crippled repricer — it is the artifact an internal price review can actually defend.

**The pain is real and budgeted.** Dealavo: five pricing mistakes "each cost online shops 2–5 percentage points of margin," including changes "by gut feeling" and manual price-setting [55 — vendor claim, unverified estimate]. Conga's remedy vocabulary — "Set price bands: use floor, target, and stretch prices" [56] — is the practitioner convention this product productizes as the cost floor plus rule actions. The arithmetic conventions are stable public knowledge: selling price = cost × (1 + markup%) [15][18]; retail price = cost ÷ (1 − margin%) when targeting a gross margin [16][19]; margin% = markup% ÷ (1 + markup%), so a 50% markup is a 33.3% margin — the exact confusion retailers are documented getting wrong [17][20]. And landed-cost discipline is the acknowledged foundation: landed cost = product cost + freight + duties + taxes + fees [69][71][67][68]; "get it wrong" and gross profit is wrong [66]; "Ignoring landed cost leads to underpricing or margin loss" [69].

**The regional layer.** VAT-inclusive display is the norm and the trap: KSA standard VAT is 15% [29] with mandatory VAT invoice fields [28]; UAE is 5% with inclusive/exclusive formulas a daily operational reality [30]. The complaint class is concrete — a shopper advertised "3 for 599 AED" pays 628.95 AED at the till (599 × 1.05) and asks why [31] — so a MENA pricing console must preview the VAT-inclusive figure per market beside net, as a first-class column. Seasonality is structural: "Ramadan is fundamentally a promotion-driven retail season. Consumers are highly attuned to discounts and special offers, and they actively plan their purchasing" (Grand View Research) [37]; merchants are coached to run special Ramadan promotions [38] with shifted shopping hours and campaign timing [39]. A GCC DTC playbook claims Ramadan+Eid typically pulls 20–35% of the year's revenue [40 — unverified estimate]; a LinkedIn post reports a 54-day KSA Ramadan sales period [41 — unverified, second-hand]. Date-bounded markdown rules are therefore a v1 structural need — with the curated seasonal pack explicitly out of scope (honesty posture shared with cod-order-confirmation Q-15).

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **The spreadsheet** — the true status quo: price files with cost columns, markup formulas pasted down, and no effective-dating. Free, universally understood, and structurally silent on floor breaches (a formula that returns 84.50 when the floor is 96.14 just prints 84.50); every direct competitor's marketing implicitly fights it, and our cost-history timeline is the direct counter.
- **Marketplace seller dashboards' native price fields** — where price edits happen today, one item at a time, per channel, with no cross-channel view, no cost basis, and no preview; the multichannel seller keeps three dashboards open (the posture marketplace-listing-sync documents from its own research, B7-4 01).
- **Price A/B testing platforms** (Convert-class) — "Can you A/B test on price? Yes" [42]; guides [43]; tools from $112/mo [45] and $450 per launched experiment/month [44]. Excluded at v1 per roster (LOOP-10); recorded here so the exclusion is a decision made against a known category, not an oversight.
- **SaaS pricing software** (Schematic, Chargebee, Maxio class) — "pricing software" mindshare that actually prices subscription plans [1][2][3]; wrong domain (our buyer prices merchandise, not tiers), but owns the search term — positioning must say "merchandise price rules," not "pricing software," to avoid the mismatch.

## Market and demand evidence (with sources)

1. **Margin erosion is a named, benchmarked pain**: delayed response to vendor cost increases → 1.6% average / up to 6.0% margin erosion (Intuilize benchmark, vendor-sourced, unverified estimate) [53]; "silent margin leakage" from manual spreadsheet pricing (Zilliant) [54]; 2–5 margin points lost per named mistake (Dealavo, vendor claim, unverified estimate) [55].
2. **The band vocabulary is practitioner convention**: floor/target/stretch price bands (Conga) [56]; markup-on-cost and margin-on-price formulas are standard retail arithmetic [15][16][17][18][19][20].
3. **Landed cost is the acknowledged foundation of price**: components and formulas [66][67][68][69][70][71]; underpricing as the named failure of ignoring it [69].
4. **Pricing-psychology endings are convention, with Western evidence**: left-digit effect coined by Thomas & Morwitz (2005) [21][22]; ~60% of advertised prices ended in 9 (1997 Marketing Bulletin, via Mida) [24]; nine-endings the most-used practitioner tactic [25]; attribute moderators studied in 2025 [26]; demand-lift claims of 24–35% circulate via aggregators (unverified estimate — never cited as fact in-product) [27]. No MENA-specific ending study surfaced (honest gap → 12 Q-9): ending modes ship as configurable conventions.
5. **VAT-inclusive display is a GCC consumer-protection expectation**: KSA 15% standard rate [29], invoice field mandates [28]; UAE 5% with inclusive display formulas [30]; the 599-sign/628.95-till complaint class [31]. A pricing tool for this market previews VAT-inclusive display prices per market.
6. **Ramadan is a promotion-driven retail season**: promotion-attuned, planned purchasing [37]; special- Ramadan promotions advised [38]; shifted hours and timing [39]; seasonal revenue concentration claims 20–35% [40 — unverified estimate]. Date-bounded rules are structural; a curated seasonal pack is out of scope.
7. **The excluded categories are real and adjacent**: competitor-monitoring/repricing tools at $49–$157.95/mo entry bands [63][59 — unverified estimates]; Amazon repricers with full automation [5][6]; price A/B tooling $112–$450+/mo [45][44]. The exclusions are recorded decisions with legal grounding [10][11][12][13][46][48][50][51][52].
8. **MAP is a brand-side convention**: minimum advertised price as a brand policy for resellers [33][34][35], with monitoring tooling [32][36]; for a seller-side console a MAP figure is an optional input floor — US legal context noted honestly [36], GCC prevalence unknown (honest gap).

## Positioning statement

For MENA pricing leads who answer for blended margin, price-rule-engine is the rules console that turns landed costs into defensible price decisions: deterministic rules with priority and validity windows, cost-floor clamping that never lets a rule price below the floor silently, simulation with per-row margin preview and per-market VAT-inclusive display prices, and a proposal → approval → export chain that puts a human name on every price change. Unlike competitor-monitoring tools (Prisync, Price2Spy, Pricefy), it ingests no competitor data — the legal ground under scraping is unsettled and we do not stand on it — and unlike repricers (Seller Snap class), it executes nothing, because algorithmic repricing carries live competition-law exposure. Unlike enterprise price-management platforms (Zilliant, Intuilize), it is a bilingual, two-market, two-to-eight-seat console a pricing lead can run on the first afternoon: the merchant's own costs in, an approved price sheet out, and the arithmetic visible on every row.
