## Curated references

1. **Wave Invoicing** — https://www.waveapps.com/invoicing
   Take: the workflow shape the category taught a decade of freelancers — client → line items → totals → send/download — is the muscle memory our composer inherits (03 CP-1/CP-2 follow it deliberately; retraining the reflex is not the product's job); the calm free-tier posture validates our pricing floor (01). Not taken: the payments-and-payroll monetization spine (our LOOP-10 boundary); the Latin-first document rendering; the accounting-suite gravity that grows around the invoicing hook.

2. **Zoho Invoice** — https://www.zoho.com/invoice/pricing and the community thread https://help.zoho.com/portal/pt/community/topic/printing-two-languages-english-arabic-on-invoice
   Take: from the product — the free-forever posture and the clean client/series basics; from the community thread — the definitive negative reference: bilingual output as a PDF-font/template archaeology exercise ("the template must be Font RTL to display both the Arabic and English texts") is exactly the workaround our DocumentPreview replaces with a composed bilingual model. Not taken: suite-ecosystem IA (our setup surface is three screens of letterhead/tax/numbering, not a settings city); template-font bilingualism as a design idea.

3. **Invoice Ninja** — https://invoiceninja.com and https://invoiceninja.com/pricing-plans
   Take: template-shape control as a valued buying motive (developers and design-conscious freelancers pay for document fidelity — 01); the self-host/open-source trust posture validates invoice-document buyers' data sensitivity; the honest free tier (5 clients, unlimited invoices) is the acquisition-floor grammar we match. Not taken: payment-gateway-centric feature ladders; template flexibility WITHOUT bilingual field structure — flexibility that cannot express two scripts in one field is the gap itself.

4. **The two-column bilingual invoice template (waco3.io)** — https://waco3.io/blog/bilingual-invoice-template
   Take: the practitioner-grade insight that a bilingual invoice "labels every field in both the client's language and yours" and "clears accounting queues" — the receiver-persona (02 Tariq) argument in one line; the two-column label discipline informs our side-by-side-bilingual layout default. Not taken: the static-template dead end (a fixed two-column PDF cannot toggle numerals, re-pin tax schemes, or carry amount-in-words generation); the cross-border-only framing — our buyers are domestic-bilingual as often as cross-border.

## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T3-appropriate)

The Counter: a slate-inked workbench where the paper is the interface's second citizen and its first object. The composer is two materials — a cool, ruled editing pane (canvas, hairline slate borders, flat-line throughout, zero shadows) and a warm paper preview (oklch 0.985 warm white, 10px corners, ink typography at Inter-500 tabular figures) that sits beside it like a document on a clerk's desk. Nothing floats over the paper; nothing shadows it; the pane boundary is a single hairline, and the paper's warmth IS its elevation. The line-item grid's rows flow down the cool side while their consequences flow simultaneously down the warm side — edit a quantity here, watch the figure settle there in the same frame (≤100ms budget, 10). The tax block is the document's hinge: a scheme card on the cool side swaps the bilingual tax label and the rate, and the warm side re-renders the tax line with its breakdown reachable on focus — the accountant's proof, on demand, never in the way. The signature moment is the **numeral flip**: one segmented control — "Western 0-9" / "العربية الهندية ٠-٩" — and every figure on the paper crossfades ≤120ms into the other numeral system while the totals rail's stored figures sit unmoved beside it, two renderings of one truth visible simultaneously for the length of a blink. The grand total is the loudest thing on the paper (primary-deep, 10.72:1) because on a money document the number outranks the words. Success is a state chip and one quiet line of arithmetic ("issued INV-2026-0007 — 18,227.50 SAR"), the counter's kind of celebration. Motion is minimal everywhere else: things settle, nothing bounces, and the only thing that ever moves twice is nothing.

## Mood anchors

A clerk's counter in slate and warm paper · two scripts, one arithmetic · the number that outranks the words · the flip that changes clothes, never values · sequences that never gap · the paper you could hand to an accountant without a word of explanation.
