# 00 — Brief: invoice-generator-desk

Family: commerce_integrations · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

An invoicing composer for freelancers and SMBs in MENA whose clients read money documents in two scripts. The user composes a **sales invoice** — the money document a customer owes the business against — from a client record, a jurisdiction's tax scheme, and a stack of line items, and the product renders it live as a bilingual paper artifact: every label in both Arabic and English, every figure rendered in the numeral system the recipient expects. The product's two distinctive mechanisms are both display-side disciplines on canonical data, and both are deterministic to the digit. First, the **numeral-system toggle**: invoice amounts are stored canonically (currency minor units, Western-digit strings) and rendered either as Western digits (0-9) or Eastern Arabic-Indic digits (٠-٩) — one control on the document, applied to every figure at render time, never touching the stored value (rtl-rules §3: numeral choice never changes parsing semantics). Second, **jurisdiction-configurable tax fields**: a workspace-owned table of tax schemes — seeded with the GCC landscape from the research digest (Saudi Arabia 15%, UAE 5%, Bahrain 10%, Oman 5%, Qatar/Kuwait no-VAT regimes, plus custom schemes) — where each scheme defines the tax label in both languages (ضريبة القيمة المضافة / VAT), the default rate, whether seller and client tax-registration numbers print on the document, and the currency's minor-unit digits. Change the scheme, and the invoice's tax block, labels, and required-field gates change with it; the arithmetic is recomputed live and re-pinned at issue.

The arithmetic is the product's spine and it is declared **non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1): line totals are quantity × unit price minus line discount, tax is the scheme's rate applied to the after-discount subtotal, and every amount rounds half-up at the invoice currency's minor digits — the three-decimal currencies of the Gulf (Bahraini dinar, Kuwaiti dinar, Omani rial) round at three places, the two-decimal currencies at two. Every computed figure carries its breakdown on hover and in the export: the rate, the taxable base, the rounding rule. The same discipline governs the document's accountability furniture: invoice numbers come from a **number series** that assigns strictly sequential values at issue — numbers are consumed, never reused, never gapped (the sequential-numbering requirement of VAT regimes generally, per the research digest; missing or duplicate numbers are a named compliance defect in the UAE source) — and an issued invoice's content is frozen; corrections happen through void-with-reason (the number stays consumed) or a successor document, never through silent edits. The document also renders the **amount in words** in both languages — a deterministic number-to-words conversion (with the Arabic dual/plural group tables the language actually requires) — because formal Arabic money documents carry it, cheques demand it, and no mainstream invoicing tool generates it bilingually.

The product deliberately stops at the document. It does not collect payments (an abstract delivery channel sends the invoice; payment status is recorded by the user as **payment marks** — dated, referenced evidence, the same evidence-not-ledger posture as the portfolio's claim pipeline), it keeps no ledger or accounting (export is the handoff to whatever books the business already keeps), and it runs no recurring billing. Its counter-position (01): the invoicing category's majors monetize payment collection and accounting depth, are Latin-first in their document rendering, and treat bilingual output as a PDF-font workaround — a help-forum thread in the research digest literally walks users through an "RTL font template" to print Arabic and English on one invoice. This product inverts that: the bilingual money document is the primary object, the payments rail is somebody else's business, and the numerals are the buyer's choice.

## Buyer

Freelancers and small businesses (1–6 seats) billing MENA clients: Saudi and Emirati independent designers, developers, and consultants whose corporate clients' finance teams want Arabic-bearing invoices; bilingual agencies billing across GCC borders in multiple currencies; and solo professionals with one foot in Arabic-speaking commerce and one in international remote work (SAR for the Riyadh retainer, USD for the remote platform contract). The daily user is the person who sends the invoice; the secondary audience is the client's accountant — the person who either clears the invoice into a payment run or bounces it back for a missing registration number, a wrong tax label, or a figure they cannot read comfortably. Pricing posture (12 Q-3): a low flat solo tier with a free tier that never expires — the category's floor is genuinely free (01 pricing landscape), so the paid tier must sell the bilingual document craft, not the right to invoice.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px composer with side-by-side live document preview; responsive to 360px in list/read modes). Zones: invoices home (list, filters, saved views); invoice composer (header, client picker, jurisdiction card, line-item grid, totals rail with the numeral toggle, live document preview); issued invoice view (read-only document, render controls, delivery panel, payment marks, export); clients; setup (seller profile, tax schemes, number series); jobs console; activity log; ⌘K command palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three). No native app at v1; no public API at v1; no payment processing anywhere in the product (LOOP-10).

## Unique feature

Bilingual invoices with a numeral-system toggle and jurisdiction-configurable tax fields — defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the category's document rendering is Latin-first — mainstream tools added UI languages without making the bilingual money document a first-class object, and regional practitioners patch the gap with font templates, storefront printer apps, and two-column template hacks (01 demand evidence); (2) no surveyed competitor offers a numeral-system toggle on invoice output — an observed absence the product turns into one control, backed by the portfolio's canonical-storage discipline so the toggle is provably cosmetic; (3) jurisdiction-configurable tax fields match how GCC tax reality actually varies — rates from 0% to 15%, mandatory-Arabic document expectations in the Saudi regime, registration-number requirements that differ by scheme — a workspace table, not a hardcoded country dropdown.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No ledger, no double-entry accounting, no journal entries, no bookkeeping: the product ends at the invoice document, its payment marks (evidence), and its export artifact.
- No payment collection, no payment links, no card processing, no payment accounts: delivery of the document goes through an abstract channel (08) or the manual download/print path; what the buyer does with their bank is their business.
- No recurring billing, no subscription invoices, no auto-send schedules: every invoice is composed and issued by a human action (duplicating last month's invoice is the deliberate fastest path, 03 CP-2).
- No e-invoicing clearance, signing, stamping, or registry integration: the Saudi clearance regime and its integration waves are market context (01) for why Arabic-bearing documents matter; this product emits compliant-shaped documents with jurisdiction-configurable fields and explicitly does not perform clearance, QR signing, or government submission.
- No credit or debit notes at v1: issued invoices are immutable and corrected by void-with-reason or a successor invoice; credit/debit notes carry their own jurisdiction-specific field regimes and are a v2 conversation (12 Q-11).
- No read receipts, no opened/tracked telemetry on delivered invoices: delivery records state what was sent and when — surveillance-shaped features are out.
- No AI anywhere: the declaration is explicit; tax and total arithmetic, number-to-words, and numeral rendering are deterministic computation (07a/07b/07c); no generation, no suggestions, no "smart" affordances (ai-native-patterns §1).

## Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done (success signals)

A first-session user can: complete the seller profile with a bilingual legal name and a tax-registration number; pick the Saudi tax scheme and watch the tax block relabel itself bilingually at 15%; add two line items with a bilingual description on one of them and watch the totals rail recompute; flip the numeral toggle and watch every figure on the document render ٠-٩ while the stored values stay Western-canonical; issue the invoice and see the sequential number assigned from the series; download the PDF (manual path, zero credentials); record a partial payment mark and watch the invoice read "partially settled"; void a draft and confirm the series counter did not move — all on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI, and with the delivery channel unbound from the first minute (download/print works end to end).
