## Category overview

The freelancer/SMB invoicing category is a mature free-floor market whose majors monetize the money movement around the document, not the document itself. **Free-forever invoicing is the norm**: Wave gives invoicing away and sells payments and payroll around it [6][8]; Zoho Invoice is free for small businesses [7]; Invoice Ninja's free tier carries 5 clients with unlimited invoices (January 2026 pricing update) [2]; Square Invoices is free with per-transaction card fees [9][10]. The paid bands sit at $14–$25/mo for solo-to-small tiers (Invoice Ninja Pro $14/mo or $140/yr [2]; Wave Pro $19/mo [11]; FreshBooks $24/$39/$79 [13]; a challenger band under $10 exists — $7.95 entry per Billbooks' own counter-positioning [12]). Payment processing is where the category's economics live (Wave 2.9%+60¢ card / 1% bank [11][6]; Square free-plan invoicing at 3.3%+30¢ per the nvoyce critique [10]) — which is exactly the rail this product refuses to own (LOOP-10). The unserved layer is the **document itself for bilingual buyers**: the category's rendering is Latin-first, and the research surfaced the bilingual gap being patched by practitioners with workarounds rather than served by products — a Zoho help-forum thread instructing users to switch the invoice PDF template to an "Font RTL" face to print English and Arabic on one page [33]; a Shopify app ("Bilinguo Order Printer") existing solely to print Arabic/English order invoices "for GCC requirement" [34]; TallyPrime bilingual invoicing as a multi-step language-alias configuration [36]; a cross-border template vendor selling a two-column bilingual invoice layout because it "clears accounting queues" [38]; and a dedicated micro-tool (arabinvoice.com) whose entire product is the Arabic-English invoice [35]. A GCC practitioner forum states the legal reality plainly — both languages are not required, "one or the other is sufficient, but the ability to translate into Arabic is necessary" [37] — while the Saudi e-invoicing regime makes Arabic mandatory on tax invoices and simplified tax invoices (with credit/debit notes) in that market [19], and the broader GCC runs a 0%-to-15% VAT spread (Saudi Arabia 15%, Bahrain 10%, UAE and Oman 5%, Qatar and Kuwait not yet implemented) [22][23][24][25]. The wedge: a composer whose primary object is the bilingual money document, with the numeral system and the jurisdiction's tax block as first-class, deterministic controls — the document craft none of the majors sell, in the region whose documents need it.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **Bilingual workaround ecosystem** (the true incumbent): Zoho "Font RTL" template threads [33], the Bilinguo storefront printer app [34], Tally language-alias bilingual setups [36][penieltech], two-column bilingual template sellers [38], and arabinvoice.com [35]. Free to cheap, and every one proves demand; none composes the document — they format around its absence. The manual alternative we formalize (paste-the-PDF-you-already-have) is also this class's habit.
- **Docs-and-spreadsheets status quo**: the invoice template in a word processor or spreadsheet, hand-numbered, hand-translated. Flexible, free, and the incumbent every buyer already has; sequential numbering and tax-block correctness are exactly where it fails (the compliance defect class in [41]).
- **Payment-platform invoicing** (processor-attached invoices): invoicing as a feature of the payments relationship — create-invoice-to-charge-a-card. Counter-position: the money document as a payments lead-generator rather than a compliance-and-readability artifact; a MENA freelancer whose client pays by bank transfer gets nothing from that posture.
- **Regional accounting suites with invoicing modules** (ERP-first products whose invoice forms exist deep inside bookkeeping): heavier than the buyer needs, priced for the whole ledger, and bilingualism is a print-layout setting rather than the composing model. The no-ledger exclusion (LOOP-10) is the clean boundary.

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- **Late payment is the freelancer's structural reality — clear terms and readable invoices are the accepted mitigation**: 85% of freelancers are affected by late payments; 29% of freelance invoices arrive at least one day past due [29][30]; of late invoices, over 75% are paid within 14 days of the due date and 90% within about a month (2018 primary analysis) [28]; practitioner sentiment (paraphrased) frames late payment as a leverage/systems problem mitigated by staged payments and unambiguous terms [31]. A document the client's finance team can process without queries is the cheapest lever the freelancer owns.
- **Bilingual invoice demand is observable, specific, and currently patched**: the Zoho community thread [33]; the Shopify GCC-requirement printer app [34]; Tally's multi-step bilingual procedure [36]; the manager.io GCC forum ("one or the other is sufficient, but the ability to translate into Arabic is necessary" [37]); the two-column bilingual template marketed for clearing accounting queues [38]; a dedicated Arabic-English invoice generator [35]; a Quora existence question about Arabic billing software [46].
- **Mainstream tools localize the interface, not the money document**: FreshBooks added more UI languages [45]; a MENA challenger markets "full RTL support for Arabic; localized PDF invoices" as its differentiator versus Wave [44] — evidence both that the gap is real and that it is contested only at micro-scale today.
- **GCC tax reality makes the tax block non-trivial**: standard rates — Saudi Arabia 15% [22][23][24]; UAE 5% since 2018 [24]; Bahrain 10% since 1 January 2022 (doubled from 5%) [25][23]; Oman 5% [23][24]; Qatar and Kuwait not yet implemented (unconfirmed 5% framework asterisk in one source [23]; "yet to implement" in others [22][24]). Registration thresholds: UAE AED 375,000; KSA SAR 375,000; Bahrain BHD 37,500; Oman OMR 38,500 (secondary-source quality; mytaxman.ae concurs on UAE/KSA) [24].
- **Saudi e-invoicing (market context for the Arabic-bearing document)**: tax invoices and simplified tax invoices (with credit/debit notes) must be in Arabic mandatorily, other languages permitted alongside [19]; simplified invoices apply to B2C supplies below SAR 1,000 and do not entitle the recipient to an input VAT deduction [17]; Phase 2 clearance integration waves extend to taxpayers with revenue ≥ SAR 187,500 by 1 February 2027 [20]; e-invoices must be machine-readable and meet published security requirements [18]; required fields are enumerated in the regime's detailed guidelines and implementation resolution [15]. This product takes the market lesson (Arabic-bearing documents are the norm, field expectations are jurisdiction-shaped) and explicitly does not do clearance or signing (00 exclusions).
- **Sequential numbering is a general VAT-regime obligation**: most VAT/GST countries require unique sequential invoice numbers [39]; the EU VAT invoice content checklist codifies the field set [40]; UAE practice names missing or duplicate sequential numbers as a compliance defect [41].
- **The numeral-system split is a live display convention**: Western digits (0-9) dominate internationally; Eastern Arabic-Indic digits (٠-٩) are standard in the Gulf, Egypt, and the Levant, with both appearing on price tags in mixed use [21]. No surveyed invoicing product ships a numeral-system toggle on invoice output — an observed absence, documented as such.

Our posture (12 Q-3): free tier that never expires (the category made free table stakes; charging for the right to invoice is dead) + a flat solo/small tier in the $12–20/mo band selling the bilingual document craft — jurisdiction schemes, numeral rendering, bilingual amount-in-words, series integrity — with exports soft-metered within a generous window. No per-transaction economics: the product touches no money.

## Positioning statement

For freelancers and SMBs billing MENA clients, invoice-generator-desk is the invoicing composer that makes the bilingual money document the product: every invoice renders Arabic and English natively, figures flip between Western and Arabic-Indic numerals at one control without touching stored values, and the tax block follows the jurisdiction's scheme — rate, bilingual labels, registration-number gates — from a table the workspace owns. Unlike the free invoicing majors, it does not sell payments or accounting; it sells a document the client's finance team clears on first pass. And unlike the workaround ecosystem of RTL font templates and bilingual printer apps, the bilingual invoice is composed, not patched.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns)

- Category-user sentiment (roundups + fee critiques, paraphrased): free-plan invoicing that quietly monetizes the payment rail; template flexibility that still can't express a bilingual field structure; client-facing documents that look wrong to non-Latin readers.
- Regional-practitioner sentiment (help forums + template vendors, paraphrased): getting Arabic and English onto one professional invoice is a font/config archaeology exercise; accountants bounce invoices over missing tax registration fields and unreadable figures.
- Freelancer sentiment (late-payment threads, paraphrased): payment delays are leverage problems — the invoice's job is to be unambiguous, sequenced, and easy for the payer's process to accept [28][29][31].
