## Category overview

Two categories collide here. **Inventory apps** (Sortly, BoxHero, inFlow, Zoho Inventory class) productize stock tracking for small teams — scan a barcode, record what moved — and most carry a count/adjustment feature inside a broader tracking suite; they are Latin-first, per-seat or item-cap priced, and treat counting as one module among many (Q4, Q5). **Professional inventory counting services** (RGIS-class crews) sell the discipline itself — blind counts, tolerance-triggered recounts, variance resolution, accuracy reporting — as a human service for physical inventories (Q11, digest B). In between sits the actual status quo of the target buyer: a paper sheet, a spreadsheet, and a closing night (Q8 — the manual loop "count the stock, enter the numbers, cross-reference the sales, update the formulas, repeat"; a vendor claims 67% of retailers still use Excel, unverified estimate, vendor content). The wedge: a **guided cycle-count assistant** that productizes the professional counting discipline (blind modes, tolerance bands, recount conventions, IRA arithmetic) at app price, with **bilingual Arabic/English count sheets** as the default rather than an afterthought — Gulf retail labeling is Arabic/English as standard practice (Q12: Confetti Design; ESL vendors in UAE render both scripts; KSA POS buyers list bilingual support as a baseline requirement, Q7).

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **Spreadsheets and paper** (the incumbent every buyer already has): the manual loop is slow and frequency collapses (Q8 [13][14][15]; Mitti: "retailers should track inventory as often as they can… troublesome if manual" [16]). Free, flexible, zero structure — the exact behavior our sessions formalize.
- **Outsourced count crews** (RGIS-class, Q11 [17]): professional physical inventories with blind-count and variance-resolution discipline — the ceiling above us on crew scale; the floor below us on frequency (a service visits; a cycle count lives in the store weekly).
- **POS suites with count modules** (KSA/GCC bilingual POS class — Tijarah360, POSBytz, OverseePOS, Q7 [18][19][20]): bilingual and ZATCA-aware, but counting is a footnote inside checkout-first software; POS integration is precisely what we defer (LOOP-10) — we complement, not replace, until the stock-level channel binds.
- **Full enterprise WMS/ERP counting** (SAP-class physical inventory: create documents → record counts → post variances, Q11 [21]): the process model we inherit (documents, recounts, variance posting) is enterprise-grade; the shape is not — no store manager buys an ERP to count a backroom.

## Demand evidence (with sources)

- **Counting discipline is codified practice, not opinion**: cycle counting counts small preselected sections continuously vs 1–2 full physicals a year (NetSuite [22]); methods = ABC by value/demand, random sample, control group (DCL [23]); Class A counted most frequently (DCL [23], RF-SMART [24]).
- **Blind counts are a named convention**: item + location shown, quantity and dollar value withheld (Champion Business Solutions [25]; FCB: "quantity and dollar value are not shown to the counters" [26]; Uphance [27]) — used in cycle counts and physicals alike (Endpoint [28]).
- **Tolerance-triggered recounting is an established mechanism**: "a tolerance level is the pre-agreed amount a counted zone may differ from the book record before a recount is automatically triggered" (PICS [29]); tolerance is operator policy and varies (NetSuite shrinkage guide [30]).
- **Accuracy has conventional KPIs and anchors**: IRA = matching/checked records × 100 (Interlake Mecalux worked example 96% [31]); 98% IRA described as 3PL-grade (Forthmatch [32]); retail shrink "acceptable under 2%" (Finale [33]); NRF average shrink 1.6% of sales FY2022, 1.4% in 2021 (CURA [34], NetSuite [30], InVue [35]) — US-retail data, directional for MENA, marked as such.
- **Bilingual Arabic/English is the Gulf retail baseline**: bilingual labeling is the standard in KSA packaging (Confetti Design [36]); UAE shelf labels render Arabic & English (TechnoWave [37]); KSA POS buyers require bilingual support with ZATCA-compliant Arabic invoices (Tijarah360 [18], POSBytz [19]); Arabic label enforcement at borders is documented practice (USDA FAS GAIN [38]); barcode label supply chains operate across UAE (POSINUAE [39], Smart Arabia Labels [40]).
- **Scan-dependence is a known count pain**: Zoho community users requesting counts without scanning serialized barcodes [10] — type-and-search fallback is a requirement, not a nicety.

## Pricing landscape

App-class anchors (all unverified estimates, official/vendor pages): Sortly $0 free (1 user/100 items) → $49–$299/mo with item caps [1][2][3]; BoxHero ~$20 → $30/team/mo with freemium [4][5][6]; inFlow per-seat suite pricing [7]; Zoho suite tiers [9]. Service ceiling (RGIS-class) publishes no prices (digest gap #4). Our posture (12 Q-3): **per-store flat subscription with unlimited typed counters** — counting is done by whoever is on shift, and per-seat models (the category norm) tax exactly that; anchored at the app-class entry band.

## Positioning statement

For MENA store managers, inventory-count-assistant is the guided count-session tool that keeps the book honest without closing the store: cycle counts run on an ABC cadence that names why each item is due, blind sessions withhold book quantities until the reveal, tolerance bands trigger recounts automatically, and every count sheet, search, and report is bilingual Arabic/English by construction. Unlike tracking suites, it ends at the counted truth and its handoff — no orders, no purchasing, no forecasting — and unlike count crews, the discipline lives in the store every week, not once a year.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns)

- Spreadsheet-era sentiment (Q8, paraphrased): counting is manageable; the re-typing afterward is the tax, and frequency collapses because a full manual count costs a closing night.
- Suite-app user sentiment (Q4/Q5, paraphrased): paying per seat or per item cap for people who only count, and count features buried inside order-management chrome.
- Zoho community pattern (verbatim thread title, Q5 [10]): needing a stock count that does not require scanning serialized barcodes — counting must survive unscannable items.
- Gulf retail context pattern (Q7, paraphrased from bilingual-POS marketing itself): tools that assume English-first labels force bilingual staff to count against transliterated or English-only sheets.

## Market size (honest)

The long-tail of MENA retail stores with 200–5,000 SKUs — home goods, electronics accessories, groceries, pharmacies, gift shops — plus small chains at 2–3 doors. No auditable MENA count-tool TAM exists; any number would be invented, so none is offered. Envelope: per-store flat SaaS at app-class entry pricing ($15–$25/store/mo band against the $20–$49 anchors above, unverified-estimate context), with the counting discipline (not storage volume) as the value. The regional demand signals are structural (bilingual labeling standards, bilingual POS baselines, barcode label ecosystems) rather than measured adoption — recorded as an honest gap.
