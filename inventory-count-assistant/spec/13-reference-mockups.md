# 13 — Reference Mockups: inventory-count-assistant

Tier 3 obligations: ≥2 curated references (met: 4). Real-world names allowed in this file per hard rule 1.

## Curated references

1. **Sortly** — https://www.sortly.com and https://www.sortly.com/pricing
   Take: the mobile-first counting posture buyers already know (walk the shelf, find the item, record the number) and the visual-item-card conventions that make catalog browsing fast on a phone. Not taken: item-cap pricing tiers (they tax exactly the SKU counts our buyer runs); the asset-tracking generalization (photos-forward, tracking-adjacent — counting as one module among many); English-first labels with no session lifecycle.

2. **BoxHero** — https://www.boxhero.io and its Google Play listing — https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bgpworks.boxhero&hl=en_US
   Take: scan-to-count simplicity as the entry rhythm ("scan an item barcode, record what moved") and the honest team pricing shape ($20–$30/team/mo — the anchor our per-store posture sits against). Not taken: the realtime transaction ledger as the core object (we count against a book register in sessions, not track movements continuously); no blind/directed modes, no tolerance policy, no recount workflow; Latin-only labels.

3. **Zoho Inventory stock count module** — https://www.zoho.com/us/inventory and the community thread — https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/community/topic/stock-count
   Take: the enterprise count-sheet grammar (count document → recorded counts → posted variances — the SAP-class process shape, 01 [21], rendered at small-business weight) and the validation that counts must not be scan-dependent (the community thread asking for counts without scanning serialized barcodes is our type-and-search fallback's requirement letter). Not taken: the order-management suite scope we exclude (LOOP-10); reconciliation-by-form without guided sessions or tolerance bands.

4. **RGIS counting-service practice pages** — https://www.rgis.co.uk/industry-perspective/the-most-common-causes-of-shrinkage
   Take: the professional discipline we productize — blind counts, variance resolution as a managed stage, accuracy reporting as a deliverable; the service's own content is the source for the tolerance/recount conventions in 07c. Not taken: the service model itself (crews visiting annually vs a cycle count living in the store weekly) — the ceiling whose playbook becomes software.

## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T3-appropriate)

The Moss Tally Sheet: a flat-line counting instrument in Jarda Moss on cool paper — hairline borders, zero shadows, Changa's compressed Arabic horizontals carrying item names while Archivo's tabular figures carry every number. The guided count view reads as a walked tally sheet: the scan field sits at the inline-start edge like the corner of a clipboard, rows descend in zone order, and in blind mode the quantity column carries a diagonal tally-slash glyph — the count exists, the book does not. The signature surface is the **Ledger Reveal**: counting ends, and the masked column resolves in one 140ms crossfade into signed deltas with drawn tolerance brackets — "−7" in red beside a bracket that says "±2", "−1" in within-green beside a bracket that says "±2", the arithmetic visible at a glance and repeated in text for every screen reader. The reconciliation screen is the deepest surface by border weight alone; the close gate lists its blockers like a foreman's clipboard note — item names, counts, and the one decision still owed. Success is a state chip and one quiet line of arithmetic ("27 من 28 صنفاً داخل السماحية — 96.4%"), the store manager's kind of celebration. Crisp motion throughout: everything settles in ≤180ms, nothing ever bounces, and the only thing that moves twice is nothing.

## Mood anchors

A ruled tally sheet on a clipboard under fluorescent stockroom light · moss-green ink on cool paper · the diagonal slash that means "counted, not shown" · brackets that say how wrong is allowed · one crossfade when the book answers · numbers that never lie about which way they moved.
