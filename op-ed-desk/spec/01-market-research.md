SME-A pass · URLs retrieved 2026-10-16 (raw: research/batch2/op-ed-desk-research.md).

## Category overview

Two categories collide without meeting: submission/peer-review systems (Submittable for grants and litmags; Scholastica and the Editorial Manager class for journals) prove editors and reviewers work inside structured queues; the opinion-desk status quo is email to a shared inbox with published word guidelines (Washington Post: completed articles, ~800 words [1]; NYT: 650 suggested [2]; LA Times: exclusivity, email only [3]). None of the former is opinion-desk-shaped, bilingual, or linting-aware; the latter has no queue, no blind review, no feedback loop.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **Shared email inbox** (incumbent): free, universal, zero structure — what every guideline page [1][3] routes through; the product replaces it.
- **OJS-class open-source journal systems**: free workflow, same journal-frame mismatch.
- **Generic AI assistants**: free triage help; no queue, no anonymity discipline, no audit trail.
- **House style guides as practice** (AP [13], BBC [14], Al Jazeera standards [15]): the norm layer our linter encodes — they define rules; we enforce them at intake.

## Demand & market evidence

- Desks run policy at the door, manually: word bands across guidelines (650 [2], 800 [1], 700–900 [16], 500–750 [17]) and exclusivity rules [3][18] — a rules-engine job.
- Slush volume, scarce acceptances: The OpEd Project notes 1–2 pieces selected weekly for print [19]; practitioner sentiment describes heavy email loads at opinion desks (paraphrase, r/Journalism [20]); acceptance rates unpublished (unverified estimate: low single digits at major dailies).
- Anonymized review is evidence-backed in adjacent publishing: blinding minimizes reputation/affiliation/gender bias [21], changes outcomes [22]; its documented implementation risk is anonymization feasibility and alignment [23] — the risk our leak-flag mechanism answers.
- Arabic editorial standards are formalized at MENA broadcasters (Al Jazeera's 343-page standards book [15]); MSA required in Arabic delivery standards [24].
- Arabic checking tech is commercially validated [10][11][12]; integration into a review workflow is absent.

## Pricing landscape

Anchors: Submittable ~$29/mo entry to institutional five figures [6][7] (top tier unverified); scholarly systems institutional. Recommendation (12 Q-3): flat per-desk ~$49–99/mo, contributor side free — desks reject per-submission fees because volume is the pain they buy relief from.

## Positioning statement

For newspaper opinion desks drowning in email slush, op-ed-desk is the submission-and-review pipeline that turns arrivals into decisions — anonymized review removes name bias from the room; Arabic editorial style linting enforces desk policy at intake. Unlike grant portals and journal systems, it is shaped for the opinion desk: fast triage, blind rounds, feedback letters, bilingual by construction. It publishes nothing; it decides everything.
