# 00 — Brief: hiring-pipeline-desk

Family: business_ops · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

An ATS-lite pipeline desk for in-house recruiters: one governed line from "a candidate is in our pipeline" to "a hiring decision is recorded, with the evidence that produced it". The recruiter opens a **job opening** (bilingual title, EN and AR as first-class fields), authors its **interview kit** — 4–6 weighted competencies, each carrying anchored questions in **both languages** — moves **applications** across a stage board (Applied → Screening → Interview → Decision, plus terminal stages), and schedules **interviews** that assign each interviewer the same kit in the interview's declared language. Every interviewer fills an independent **scorecard**: a rating per competency on an anchored 1–4 scale with an evidence note per rating, plus one recommendation. Scorecards stay sealed — peers' cards are invisible until the interviewer's own card is submitted — and when all cards are in, the **debrief** computes the honest arithmetic: competency means, the recommendation tally, and every point where interviewers diverge. The recruiter records the decision (advance / reject / hold) with a rationale, and the whole trail — moves, interviews, cards, nudges, decisions — is the activity log an auditor or a department head reads later.

The unique feature is **bilingual structured interviews with scorecards and bias nudges**, and it is built as three inspectable mechanisms, not oracles. First, **bilingual by construction**: the kit carries EN and AR question variants for every competency; an interview is scheduled *in a language* ('ar', 'en', or 'bilingual' with side-by-side delivery), and the adapter refuses to schedule an interview whose kit has no questions in that language — the bilingual gap is caught at scheduling, not discovered in the room. Candidate names live as a pair (Latin / Arabic) rendered per-side direction with bidi isolation, so "Danielle Haddad" and "دانيال حداد" are one record, not two pipelines. Second, **scorecards as structured evidence**: anchored 1–4 ratings (no neutral midpoint — the scale forces a lean), evidence notes required per competency, recommendation required, and the independence seal — the evaluator-independence principle the structured-interview literature insists on (Bohnet: evaluators should not see peers' judgments before recording their own, 01). Third, **bias nudges as deterministic rules** (07c, exact formulas): an extreme rating (1 or 4) with thin evidence (fewer than 15 words) is nudged to justify itself; trait adjectives ("brilliant", "ذكي") without observable behavior are nudged toward evidence; rating divergence across interviewers (spread ≥ 2 on the 1–4 scale) is surfaced in the debrief by name. The nudges mark, they never block, and they never score a candidate — no candidate is ever ranked, auto-screened, or model-predicted anywhere in the product (the Upturn warning: predictions trained on past hiring reproduce its inequities, 01). The AI stance is **conditionally-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1): exactly one assist exists — **interview-question drafting** — which proposes kit questions when the capability is bound and consented, lands every proposal through a review-and-confirm gate, and degrades to a seeded, deterministic **template bank** (competency-class × seniority matrix of vetted bilingual questions) when unbound, rate-limited, or refused. Structured interviewing must survive the absence of the model — the kit, the cards, the nudges, and the debrief run with zero AI.

The gap this occupies is narrow and real (01): the global ATS category (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable class) sells full-funnel platforms whose center of gravity is sourcing, distribution, and analytics — the interview itself is one module among forty, priced per-recruiter or per-employee at tiers aimed at companies with dedicated recruiting ops; the regional bilingual ATSs (ZenATS, Talenera class) are CV-parsing and job-distribution machines wired to the region's job boards — exactly the two functions this product excludes. What no one sells is the *interview layer itself* for a 1–3 recruiter team doing bilingual hiring: the kit as a first-class bilingual artifact, the scorecard as sealed evidence, the nudge as an honest rule, the debrief as arithmetic you can defend — and nothing else. The demand-side signals are structural: structured interviews are among the few hiring interventions with a real evidence base (synthesis and review literature, 01), scorecard conventions are established practice (anchored scales, 4–6 competencies, 01), bilingual EN/AR roles are a countable live segment (150+ on one regional board at a time, 01), and Saudi Arabia's 2026 Nitaqat tightening — a new phase localizing 340,000+ private-sector jobs with profession-level quotas — means more structured, defensible hiring decisions per recruiter, not fewer (01; consulting-sourced, capture-honesty notes in-product, not modeled as v1 scope, 12). The boundary with the neighboring desks in this family — meeting-notes-action-desk (interview notes → actions) and helpdesk-triage-desk (pipeline stages vs ticket queues) — is recorded in 12-open-questions (LOOP-3, both flagged unilaterally: reverse mirrors pending orchestrator).

## Buyer

In-house recruiters (1–3 recruiter seats) at MENA businesses with 50–500 employees who hire bilingual staff: retail and logistics groups, clinics and school groups, professional-services firms, growing tech companies — the companies where the recruiter personally runs 6–15 open roles, screens by hand, and answers to a department head when a hire goes wrong. The economic buyer and the daily user are usually the same seat at single-company scale; at group scale the buyer is the talent/HR lead and the daily users are the recruiters plus a rotating cast of hiring managers and interviewers. Seat economics follow the work: **recruiter seats carry the price; hiring-manager and interviewer seats are included** — because the scorecard is the product's value and taxing the people who fill scorecards would tax the evidence itself (12 Q-3). This is a team tool, per-seat on recruiters, with the question assist metered within a generous window (P14).

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary: the pipeline board, kit studio, debrief console), responsive to 360px in read and scorecard-fill modes — scorecard completion is the one writing surface that must work on a phone, because interviewers submit cards from between meetings. Zones: openings home (list, filters + saved views, onboarding checklist); **the pipeline board** — the core surface — stage columns of bilingual candidate cards with drag-and-drop plus a full keyboard move path; application detail (timeline, interviews, decision record); **kit studio** (bilingual competencies, weights, questions per language, the question assist panel, coverage meters); interview detail with the scorecard form; debrief console (sealed/unsealed cards, means, divergence); candidates directory; interviews list; jobs center (imports/exports); activity log; settings (stage template, anonymized-screening toggle, capability consents, usage). ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1; no job-board distribution of any kind (LOOP-10).

## Unique feature

Bilingual structured interviews with scorecards and bias nudges — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **the bilingual kit is a schema, not a setting** — EN and AR question variants are required fields per competency with a coverage gate at scheduling; single-language interview tooling treats the second language as a locale problem (wrong script, wrong questions, wrong room), while the structured-interview evidence base says the *same questions, asked of every candidate* are the mechanism that makes interviews predictive (Levashina et al., McGill summary, 01); (2) **the scorecard follows the conventions practitioners already trust** — anchored 1–4 ratings (anchored scales beat generic labels, 01), 4–6 core competencies (01), a required recommendation, and Greenhouse-class "science-based survey methods" for the rating setup (01) — with one deliberate deviation, the dropped neutral midpoint, recorded in 12; (3) **the independence seal is structural** — peers' scorecards stay hidden until one's own is submitted, the evaluator-independence principle the bias literature calls for (Bohnet, 01), rendered as product grammar rather than a training slide; (4) **the nudges are honest rules with a named evidence base and named limits** — the behavioral-nudge literature (HBR 2022, 01) supports decision-time friction; the Aamodt synthesis title everyone should read says structure *reduces*, it does not *eliminate*, bias (Aamodt, 01) — so the product never claims cure, never blocks a decision on a nudge, and never scores candidates (Upturn, 01). The same input state always produces the same nudges (07c) — which is what makes them defensible.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No job-board distribution** — no posting to job boards, no board syndication, no board-account integrations, no "one-click publish" anywhere. Openings are internal records; a bilingual posting block can be copied out by hand if the team posts manually. The regional ATSs' core function (Bayt/board distribution, 01) is the counter-position.
- **No sourcing CRM** — no talent-pool database, no outreach sequences, no candidate mining, no "silver-medal" re-engagement flows. Sourcing is upstream of this product; the pipeline starts when a candidate is already in it.
- **No offer management** — no offer letters, no salary fields beyond optional expectations noted on the record, no approval chains for offers, no e-signature of any kind. The product ends at the recorded hiring decision; the offer is whatever process the company already runs.
- No CV parsing at v1 — candidate intake is manual entry plus paste-import; a parsing capability is an abstract, optional integration need (08) with the manual path always first (LOOP-5). (Arabic parsing exists as infrastructure others sell, 01 — we do not bind it at v1.)
- No candidate scoring, ranking, auto-screening, or model predictions of any kind — declared and enforced (07b has no such operation; Upturn posture, 01).
- No AI-written evaluations, no AI summarization of scorecards, no AI interview conduct — the single AI-touching operation is the question assist, confirm-gated per question (P15), degrading to the template bank (conditionally-AI declaration, 07a/07b/08).
- No Nitaqat/Saudization compliance modeling at v1 — quotas are real and tightening (01), but compliance is a workforce-systems concern with its own data; recorded as an open question with a recommended default of *not modeled* (12), never a silent scope grab.
- No onboarding, no employee records, no HRIS, no leave or payroll anything (attendance-shift-planner and its family own workforce time; this product owns the decision, not the employee).

## Success signals (definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

A recruiting team can: create an opening with bilingual titles; author a kit of 4 competencies with weights and EN+AR questions (typing by hand, and via the template bank with the assist unbound); request question proposals from the assist, accept one, edit one, dismiss one (P15 gate exercised); add a candidate manually with a Latin/Arabic name pair and paste-import a batch with named bad rows; move an application across stages by drag and by keyboard; reject one with a reason through the confirm ladder; schedule a bilingual interview and be *refused* when the kit lacks AR questions, then fix the kit and schedule; fill a scorecard as an interviewer, watch the evidence-thin nudge fire on an extreme rating, thicken the evidence, and submit; open the debrief and find peers' cards sealed, submit-then-read; see the divergence mark where two interviewers split 4 vs 2 on a competency; record the decision with rationale once all cards are in; read every one of those actions in the activity log; export the opening package and retrieve the artifact before expiry — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full flow completing with the question assist unbound (template bank exercised end to end).
