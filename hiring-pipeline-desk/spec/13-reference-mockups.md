3. **The kanban pipeline board as category convention — Eleken's ATS design analysis + Jobaffinity's own critique** — https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/applicant-tracking-system-design-how-to-make-recruitment-better-for-everyone · https://www.jobaffinity.com/en/blog/vue-kanban-ats
   Take: the board grammar itself — columns as stages, cards as candidates, the drag as the category's muscle memory ("many modern ATS platforms use Kanban-style boards… move candidates between stages with simple drag-and-drop", source 54); our PipelineBoard keeps the verb recruiters already know and adds what the convention lacks. 4. **The accessible drag-and-drop separate-interaction model — Level Access + the reorder-component pattern** — https://www.levelaccess.com/blog/the-importance-of-keyboard-accessibility-why-aria-widgets-dont-work-as-expected-in-voice-navigation-software · https://medium.com/@spkarthick91/accessible-drag-and-drop-building-a-reorder-component-for-screen-readers-44e38a8bacf7

## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T2-appropriate)

**The Tawthif Desk.** A hiring desk drawn on warm paper — #F9F4F3 canvas, cards one soft-lift step whiter, the board columns sunk one step into the well — with one vivid coral-rose doing the desk's own work and nothing else: Tawthif Coral #D65877 on the active rail item, the focus ring in deep coral-ink, the stage-progress fills, the big Western-numeral counts at the top of each column; every control a pill (radius D), every glyph filled-rounded and friendly — the user-plus of a candidate added, the calendar of an interview scheduled, the flag of a nudge (amber, with the number in the chip: "8 words"), the slate x-circle of a rejection (never coral — coral is the desk's energy, slate is the pipeline's terminations, and they never trade duties). The candidate card is the product's argument in one object: a name pair reading in opposite directions inside one quiet pill — "Nouf Al-Rashed" and "نوف الراشد" isolated, never merged — a tenure line in tabular figures ("in Screening 6 days"), a language chip saying what the room will speak ("العربية" in its own script inside a symmetric pill), and a tally that appears only when evidence exists to tally. The kit studio is two mirrored question columns — English running left-to-right, Arabic right-to-left in Baloo Bhaijaan 2's rounded joins, alignment links between the pairs, a coverage meter per language whose partial fill ("3 of 4") is the honest gap the scheduling gate will enforce later. The debrief console is the desk's quiet room: sealed rows in teal with the eye-off glyph ("Sealed — Dalal has not submitted"), and when the last card lands, the seal opens — the T2 signature moment — rows revealing in a ≤60ms stagger capped at five, the weighted means settling beneath in tabular figures with the tally's single 4% overshoot (the product's one flourish), the announcement carrying the whole summary either way. The nudge chips are the conscience, not the police: amber-marked, reason-carrying, dismissible with a reason that goes on the record — nothing is ever blocked, and the debrief's plum divergence marks ("4 vs 2 — Dalal / Tarek") say "the interviewers disagree", never "the candidate is bad". Board moves settle in 180ms; errors arrive without bounce; the assist panel, when it exists at all, is a draft-document glyph and a row of proposals each waiting for a click — no sparkles anywhere on the desk. It is a warm room run with discipline: the energy in the coral, the evidence in the ink, the method in the arithmetic.
