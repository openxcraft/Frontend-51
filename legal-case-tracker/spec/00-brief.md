# 00 — Brief: legal-case-tracker

Family: specialized · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A bilingual matter tracker for small litigation firms in MENA: one chronicle per case, running from intake to a gated closure. Every matter (القضية) carries its client, its court, its case number, its parties, and — the spine of the product — a **timeline** of events: hearings, deadlines, filings, notes. Hearings render as cards sitting in a recessed timeline bed, each with a live **countdown ring** whose zone (far / near / imminent / today / overdue) is derived from the seeded clock, never stored, never guessed. Deadlines are not typed in one at a time by a paralegal with a spreadsheet open: the firm configures **deadline rules** (workspace-level parameters — trigger, offset days, minimum lead, practice-area scope) and the engine generates them deterministically from events as those events happen — a hearing is scheduled, pre-hearing preparation deadlines appear; a verdict is recorded, the appeal window opens. Documents live beside the chronology as a **chain of custody**: an append-only, seq-numbered ledger of received / registered / version-added / sealed / checked-out / presented / checked-in / returned entries per document, verifiable against the per-version content fingerprints it declares. And a matter does not close by clicking a checkbox: the **closure gate** refuses while a future hearing is unscheduled-outcome, a past hearing is outcome-due, a deadline is open, or an original document is unreturned — the refusal names every blocker.

The unique feature is the **bilingual matter timeline with hearing countdown and document chain**, and it is one interlocking commitment, not three features. In Gulf litigation the same case is two scripts at once: the Arabic court name, judge name, and event title beside the Latin case number and transliterated party names — so every identity-bearing field is authored in both locales, rendered per-side direction, and searchable through Arabic-aware normalization (rtl-rules §4), with the workspace chrome defaulting RTL/Arabic and English a full citizen (05 direction model, LOOP-6 proposed). The countdown is the second axis: litigation teams live by the docket clock (02), so time is the product's semantic palette — green → amber → red as a hearing approaches, red-deep when a hearing passed without a recorded outcome (the data hole the product turns into a first-class state rather than a silent assumption). The custody chain is the third: originals — the power of attorney, the signed lease, the evidence file — physically move between client, firm, courtroom, and translation desk, and the chain of custody is the authentication convention courts already trust (research q7); here it is an append-only data structure with a verify operation, not a paper log book. The AI stance is **declared non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1, 07a): countdown zones are date arithmetic, deadline generation is rule arithmetic over anchors, custody sequencing is `seq = last + 1`, closure is a pure function over live matter state. Same input, same output, every time — which is exactly what an audit-facing artifact requires. No AI capability is bound anywhere, no workflow degrades when AI is absent, and no sparkle glyph appears in any surface (05).

The buyer's reality (01 evidence): a 2–10 lawyer litigation firm in Riyadh or Jeddah runs on the managing partner's memory, a shared calendar, and a documents folder — while the court side has already digitized (Najiz serves licensed lawyers, hearing attendance, and verification services — research q8), and the practice-management category on offer is billing-led suites whose calendaring pitch is "never miss a case deadline" as an integration promise (research q12) rather than a derived countdown surface. The litigation-specific sub-category exists and is thin (CaseFleet, priced below the suites — research q3/q6), and it is English-first with no bilingual schema, no custody chain, and no closure gate. This product is the litigation firm's instrument: the chronicle first, the clock always visible, the paper trail verifiable, the closure earned.

## Buyer

Small law firms (2–10 lawyers, typically one paralegal and one shared custody keeper) running litigation-heavy practices in MENA — commercial, labor, real-estate, and general civil claims in Gulf jurisdictions. The economic buyer is the managing partner who answers when a hearing is missed, an original goes missing, or a client asks "what happened on my case"; the daily users are the litigation lead attorney (runs the hearing board every morning), associates (record outcomes, manage filings), and the paralegal custody keeper (runs the document chain); the client-side contact (in-house counsel at the client company) reads the timeline and hearing dates in a free viewer seat (02 Hind). Per-seat for firm seats, viewer seats free (12 Q-3).

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary: docket home, matter chronicle, documents & custody, deadline rules), responsive to 360px in read and record-outcome modes. Zones: docket home (cross-matter hearing board + matters list — the morning surface), matter detail (the chronicle: timeline bed, parties, counts, closure gate), event detail (outcome recording, reschedule history, generated-deadline lineage), documents & custody (per-matter register + chain views + verification), clients directory, deadline rules console (the engine's parameters), closure & export, jobs console, activity log, settings. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

**Bilingual matter timeline with hearing countdown and document chain**, defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) no retrieved competitor treats bilingual ar/en matter identity as a first-class schema — locale is a setting elsewhere, here it is the data model (BilingualText by construction, both sides authored, never machine-filled); (2) the hearing countdown is *derived state with zones and consequences* (board triage, closure blocking, docket-home urgency sort) — not a calendar notification; (3) the document chain is an append-only custody ledger with per-version fingerprints and a verify operation — the authentication convention courts already respect (q7), productized; (4) the closure gate refuses with named blockers — a governance artifact the category's "mark as closed" pattern does not produce.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No court e-filing** — the product never submits anything to a court. Filing events are *records* that a filing happened ("filed on 10 Sep, document linked"); the e-filing channel itself is an abstract capability (08 N4) whose manual alternative — file at the court counter or through the court's own portal, then record the event here — is the primary path. Court platforms (Najiz-class) are the court's system, never our integration target (01 q8 boundary note).
- **No billing** — no timekeeping, no invoicing, no trust accounting, no payments. The category's billing-led suites own that money path; this product records matters, not fees (12 Q-3 pricing posture follows).
- **No document automation** — no template merging, no clause libraries, no document generation of any kind. Documents enter as authored bytes (uploaded or declared), are versioned and custody-tracked; drafting belongs to word processors and the drafting-suite class we are not.
- No legal research, no citation checking, no case-law search.
- No client portal beyond the read-only viewer seat (timeline + hearing dates of attached matters, nothing else — 02 Hind).
- No AI anywhere (declared non-AI): no outcome prediction, no document summarization, no "smart" anything. The engine is rules, and it says so.
- No contract-lifecycle management (obligation extraction, renewal windows — contract-lifecycle-manager's family; LOOP-3 mirror recorded in 12).

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

A litigation team can, in one first session on the Mock Adapter with zero provider-specific knowledge in the UI: open the docket home and read the cross-matter hearing board sorted by zone (an overdue outcome-due hearing is visible and red — the planted data hole, 07c m-003); create a client with a bilingual name and a matter with bilingual title, court, and case number (first hearing optional at intake); watch the stock deadline rules generate pre-hearing deadlines from the hearing's own date; reschedule a hearing with a reason and watch the generated deadlines regenerate forward-only; record an adjourned outcome with a next hearing and watch the gavel settle and the next hearing rise with its countdown already running (05 signature); complete a deadline; register an original power-of-attorney document and walk its custody chain (checked-out → presented at the hearing → checked-in), add a version, seal it, and verify the chain intact; attempt to close a matter and be refused with named blockers (unreturned originals ×2), then clear one and see the gate recount; export the matter package (timeline + custody ledger + deadline report) and retrieve the artifact before expiry; and read every mutation above in the activity log. The full loop completes with every abstract capability unbound: documents are declared with fingerprints and abstract storage references, exports download directly, deadlines generate from rules alone — the determinism contract holds end to end (LOOP-5 bar).
