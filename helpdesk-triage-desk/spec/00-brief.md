# 00 — Brief: helpdesk-triage-desk

Family: business_ops · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A triage console for bilingual support teams: the desk that sits between "a message arrived" and "an agent owns it." Tickets land from abstract inbound channels (an email-style address, a web-form hand-off, an ingest endpoint — or manual paste, which always works and needs zero credentials) and the console does exactly the four verbs practitioners already name as triage — **log, categorize, prioritize, route** (01: "the process of logging, categorizing, prioritizing, and routing support tickets before resolution begins") — and then stops. Every ticket is language-detected (Arabic, English, or mixed script, by a deterministic rule — 07c), severity-assessed by an inspectable impact×urgency matrix that names the signals it weighed, clustered by intent across both languages (an Arabic complaint about checkout and its English sibling land in ONE cluster), and routed to a queue through versioned rules whose every decision leaves a named reason in the activity log. SLA clocks run per severity tier; the monitor surfaces at-risk before breached; duplicates merge; spam closes with a record; escalations re-route with reasons. Resolution itself happens in whatever the team already runs — the responder acknowledges, logs the response reference that was sent through the team's existing channel, and closes with a disposition. This product is deliberately NOT a full helpdesk: no live chat widget, no knowledge-base authoring, no CSAT surveys (LOOP-10, below).

The unique feature is **bilingual intent clustering with severity routing**, built as two inspectable mechanisms, not oracles. First, clustering: **conditionally-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1). The local engine is deterministic keyword rules — each intent cluster carries authored bilingual signature terms, normalized per rtl-rules §4 (alef/hamza variants, taa marbuta/heh, yaa/alif maqsura, tashkeel stripping), and a ticket joins a cluster when it matches the cluster's threshold of distinct signature terms in either language. When the abstract **ticket-clustering capability** is bound and consented, it proposes new clusters and better assignments — but every proposal lands as `proposed` with a confidence band, and nothing becomes authoritative until a human confirms it (P15 trust checkpoint; the confirm gate is a first-class operation, 07b `confirmCluster`). When the capability is unbound, rate-limited, or fails, the keyword engine carries the whole workday — same screens, same rules, honestly labeled. This honesty is not modesty; it is the design: Arabic intent classification is a genuinely hard modeling problem (morphological richness, dialect diversity, labeled-data scarcity — 01 q7), so a product that *requires* a model to function would be a product that fails its buyer on day one. Second, severity: **never AI** — a deterministic, versioned matrix (requester impact tier × urgency signals from the ticket's own words and account facts) that computes P1–P4 with the ITIL-style convention practitioners already trust (01 q4), records every signal it weighed, and refuses to be a black box. The same inspectable-rules grammar that made labor-rule guardrails trustworthy in this family's planner and policy findings defensible in the claims pipeline applies here to the question "why is this P2 and where did it go."

The category gap is structural (01). Full helpdesks (Zendesk/Freshdesk-class suites) bundle triage into omnichannel platforms priced at $55–115/agent-month at the tiers where routing automation works (01 q1/q5, unverified estimates) — and their auto-routing fails opaquely when it fails: skill timeouts, ineligible tickets, capacity exclusions, skills that triggers cannot even see (01 q10, current complaints). Shared inboxes (Front/Help Scout-class) give small teams assignments and internal comments but not severity policy, not SLA clocks, not intent structure (01 q9). AI triage add-ons bolt onto existing desks and tag tickets — in one language (01 q6). Nothing serves the actual MENA reality: a 4–10 agent bilingual pod where Arabic and English tickets about the same fault must be seen as one incident, where the support lead answers for SLA breaches with an audit trail, and where the buyer already pays per-agent for the response tool and needs the *triage layer* beside it, not another suite. That sidecar seat, with bilingual clustering and inspectable severity routing as the spine, is this product.

## Buyer

Support leads at MENA businesses running bilingual (ar/en) support pods of 2–15 agents: e-commerce and delivery operations, fintech and wallet apps, SaaS companies with regional customers, telecom resellers, clinics and school groups with a customer-facing desk. The economic buyer is the support lead who owns the SLA answer — the person who explains to an ops director why P1 first-response was missed and to finance why per-agent suite pricing cannot be justified for a 6-agent pod. Daily users split into three shapes: the **triage specialist** (100–400 tickets a day, keyboard-first, allergic to false clusters and silent auto-rules), the **responder** (acknowledges, logs the response sent through the team's existing channel, closes with disposition), and the **viewer stakeholder** (ops/BI reading the weekly triage digest — read-only). Secondary: small BPO/outsourced-support operators who triage on behalf of clients and need per-workspace separation with an exportable audit artifact. Pricing recommendation: lead/triage seats carry the price, responder seats included cheaply, viewers free, clustering-assist runs metered within a generous window (12 Q-3) — because the category taxes exactly the per-agent seats a triage layer should not (01 pricing).

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary triage board and consoles), responsive to 360px in **monitor and disposition** modes (bulk triage actions are desktop/tablet; reading the SLA monitor and closing a ticket with a disposition works on a phone). Zones: triage home (intake pulse: new, unclustered, at-risk, breached counts + the onboarding checklist); the new-ticket triage queue (virtualized, filter chips + saved views, per-row language chip, severity badge, SLA state); ticket detail (message body rendered at its own script direction, detected-language declaration with the manual override, severity assessment panel naming its signals, cluster membership, routing controls, internal triage notes, activity); the cluster board (intent clusters with bilingual labels, ticket counts, language mix, drill-in) and cluster editor (bilingual label + signature-term authoring, the confirm gate for proposals); routing rules console; severity & SLA policy studio (versioned matrix + targets); SLA monitor; queues & members; jobs center + exports; settings (channel bindings as credentials, capability consent, usage). ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1; no live chat widget anywhere in the product (LOOP-10).

## Unique feature

Bilingual intent clustering with severity routing — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **cross-language clustering is absent from the category** — AI taggers run monolingual (01 q6) and shared inboxes have no intent structure at all (01 q9), while the MENA pod's core triage act is seeing that the Arabic "لا أستطيع الدفع" wave and the English "payment failed" wave are ONE incident; (2) **inspectable severity** — the impact×urgency matrix names every signal ("impact: key-account; urgency: outage term 'توقف' present; matrix KSA- Checkout v3 → P1"), the exact counter-position to documented auto-routing opacity complaints (01 q10); (3) **the keyword-rule floor** — clustering degrades to deterministic authored rules rather than dying, which the Arabic-NLP evidence says is the only honest posture (01 q7), and which makes the product fully useful with every abstract capability unbound (the 00 success signal tests exactly that state); (4) **SLA discipline as grammar, not module** — versioned policy, clocks with declared start-points, at-risk surfacing before breach, escalation with reasons, breach ledger in the export — the governance posture suites sell at their top tier, here as the pipeline's default.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No live chat widget** — no real-time customer-facing chat surface of any kind. Inbound is asynchronous message intake (email-style channel, web-form hand-off, ingest endpoint, paste); outbound customer communication happens in the team's existing tools and is only *logged* here (response reference). Real-time chat is a different product class.
- **No KB authoring** — no article editor, no knowledge-base management, no answer suggestions from articles. Triage routes to queues; it does not compose answers. KB authoring belongs to other products entirely.
- **No CSAT surveys** — no satisfaction questionnaires, no survey distribution, no NPS. The product's metrics are operational (first-response, resolution, backlog, escalation rate, reopen rate — the leading-indicator set, 01 q8) and are computed from triage state, never surveyed.
- No agent reply composition / full conversation threading — the ticket is the intake message plus internal triage notes; customer conversations live downstream. One response-reference log per SLA measurement point, nothing more.
- No AI severity, no AI routing decisions, no auto-applied clustering — the single AI-touched operation is the clustering-assist proposal stream, confirm-gated at cluster level (conditionally-AI declaration, 07a/07b); severity and routing are deterministic rules; humans confirm every promotion.
- No multi-channel customer engagement (social inboxes, voice, messaging-app channels) at v1 — channel kinds are abstract intake shapes; more bindings can arrive later through the same credential surface (08).
- No workforce management (agent schedules, capacity planning, QA scorecards) — queues name owners and language capability; that is the extent of people-data.

## Success signals (definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

A support team can: paste a bilingual batch of tickets (manual intake, zero credentials — LOOP-5) and watch language detection split them ar/en/mixed with the mixed-script one bidi-isolated; watch the severity matrix assess each with named signals and the SLA clocks start; author an intent cluster with bilingual signature terms and run the keyword engine, watching Arabic and English tickets land in the one cluster while an unmatched ticket stays honestly unclustered; bind nothing and still do all of the above (the tested default state); route a ticket by rule and watch the decision's named reason land in the activity log; override one severity with a reason and re-route; bulk-route a clean filtered set with the count confirmed; merge two duplicate tickets; mark one spam with a disposition note; watch the SLA monitor surface an at-risk ticket, escalate it, acknowledge it, log its response reference, and close it resolved-elsewhere; edit the severity matrix and watch the re-assessment Job re-open routing for affected tickets; export the triage log with the SLA ledger and retrieve the artifact before expiry — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full flow completing with the clustering assist unbound (keyword rules exercised end to end).
