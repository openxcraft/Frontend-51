## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T2-appropriate)

**The Nazim Desk.** A control tower drawn in one bright cobalt line on cool paper — flat borders, no shadows, no glass, no glow. The triage queue is the runway: rows of tickets marching in severity order, each row carrying its facts as ink (number, subject, requester) and its state as small labeled marks — the severity badge with its tier word set in Space Grotesk 500, the language chip saying what the body is and how it was read, the SLA countdown in tabular figures that tick toward a due-time printed beside them. Nothing on the row is only a color: P1 is a red-brown octagon with the word "P1 — Critical," at-risk is an amber glyph with deep-amber text, the cross-language cluster chip carries both its names — "Refund delay" and "تأخّر الاسترداد" — side by side in one bidi-safe line, two scripts reading in opposite directions inside one quiet rectangle, which is the whole product's argument made visible. The cluster board hangs like a departure board: confirmed clusters in cobalt-framed cards with their language-mix counts (7 ar / 4 en) in tabular figures; the assist's proposals arrive in violet ink with the word "proposed" and nothing else — no sparkles, no magic — and the one chord that matters, ⌘C on a proposed card, walks the triager to the confirm gate, which names what becomes authoritative before it asks for the hand. The SLA monitor is a strip of rulers: each severity tier a ruled line with at-risk and breached counts etched in figures, and the breach ledger behind it a timeline that holds every minute honestly — intake, the failed channel window, the late route — because the desk's job is to make the Friday answer assembled, not argued. And when a ticket leaves — the product's single most important act — the row's queue chip stamps in, the row settles one slot toward its queue with a crisp 160ms ease-out that never overshoots, the queue count ticks once, and the announcement says where it went and why: "Routed to Payments by rule R-04 — first response due 12:40." Then back to the queue. The desk is brisk because the morning is.

## Mood anchors

A control tower in one cobalt line on cool paper · two scripts sharing one quiet rectangle · a departure board of intents · rulers for clocks, timelines for breaches · one crisp settle when a ticket finds its queue — then the next row.
