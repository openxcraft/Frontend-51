## Curated references

1. **Zonos — rate sheets ("Create a custom service level with a rate sheet")** — https://zonos.com/docs/global-ecommerce/rating/rate-sheets
   Take: the mechanism precedent itself — carrier rate sheets uploaded as a first-class rating object with custom service levels, rating shipments accurately "when API connections aren't available" [10; 01's closest-mechanism competitor row]. It proves a merchant will hand a tool a rate table and trust the output — the wedge our paste-import (CP-1, ImportTableMapper) industrializes: parse with named issues, gate with the completeness check, version forever, and compare across cards. Not taken: its landed-cost/cross-border product frame (duty guarantees are a different trust problem — the customs-tariff-lookup lane, boundary recorded in 12 Q-6); the fallback posture — rate sheets as a workaround inside a live-API product rather than the primary object, so it grows no comparison workbench, no per-card zone resolution surface, no batch destinations, no pin-as-evidence workflow.

2. **Shippo** — https://goshippo.com and https://goshippo.com/pricing
   Take: the category's comparison-first posture ("find the best shipping rates" [4]) — proof that rate comparison is the daily habit buyers already budget for, not a settings page; and the plan-ladder transparency of its pricing page (01 anchors). Not taken: the live-API posture — comparison downstream of a carrier-account grant is exactly the inversion this product rejects (the negotiated card is the merchant's own data, in hand from day one, LOOP-5); the label-purchase pipeline (LOOP-10 exclusion); zero zone-break modeling, zero surcharge-stack authoring, and a total without its chain — the invoice-variance complaint class 01 mines.

3. **Aramex — Calculate Shipping Rates** — https://www.aramex.com/ae/en/ship/calculate-shipping-rates (with the same genre at SMSA Express — https://www.smsaexpress.com/shipping-rate)
   Take: the incumbent mental model every MENA shipping office already owns — one form (origin, destination, weight) that returns one number from one courier, free and authoritative; the profile pane of our workbench inherits exactly this shape and honesty, then answers it with every selected card at once. Their Arabic-first input reality is the bar our bilingual destination handling must meet (per-row `dir="auto"` isolation, 09). Not taken: one courier at a time — two tabs, two form fills, and manual surcharge math is the comparison pain itself (01 indirect-competitor row); list rates, never the merchant's negotiated card; no dimensional-weight modeling per divisor; no memory, no versioning, no export artifact.

4. **idshipthat — USPS & UPS Rates 2026: Zones & Weight** — https://idshipthat.app/shipping-rates
   Take: the printed rate-table genre — rate tables laid out by zone and weight tier, with the "weight break where each service stops being cheapest" as user-facing vocabulary [40]; the zone × break grid is the visual ancestor of RateCardGrid, and "weight break" is already in our buyers' mouths (02: Hana reads the 3 kg break shift as the renewal's real move). Not taken: single-carrier static tables — no editing, no versioning, no cross-card comparison; US-domestic zone numbering presented as if it were a standard (zone systems are carrier-specific by design [38] — the reason zones are per-card authored data here, 07a); no Arabic, no COD surcharge modeling, no evidence artifacts.

## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T3-appropriate)

The Tariq Rate Book: a customs-house tariff page opened flat on a shipping manager's desk and rendered as a workbench. Warm paper, ledger-rule hairlines instead of module borders, book amber as the customs-house stamp color — fills and washes only, never white-carrying text (05's computed contrast contracts). The three-pane workbench reads as one continuous schedule: the profile form as the entry stub on the inline-start; the Settle between the panes as the clerk's arithmetic — three figures (actual / volumetric / chargeable) always visible as text with units, the winner underlined in amber-deep with its derivation set like a printed rule beneath it ("35×25×15 cm ÷ 5000 = 2.63 kg → 3.0 kg at the 0.5 kg step"); and the results table inline-end as the tariff schedule itself — currency-grouped labeled sections, rows sorted ascending within each group, the cheapest row wearing the amber wash with a green total and a "cheapest" text badge, deltas as sign + number + arrow, every no-coverage row naming its fix. The card editor is the rate book's own pages: zones as coverage chips with the probe as a lookup window that reports its match in plain text ("الاحساء → Zone Z2 — matched coverage entry الأحساء via the alef rule"); the price grid as a printed schedule whose cells edit in place (Esc restores, Tab commits and advances); the surcharge stack as typed rule-lines that state their own arithmetic ("fuel 12.5% of the 40.00 transport charge = 5.00 SAR"). The breakdown sheet is the product's soul and its export at once: one ordered list of arithmetic — which weight won and why, the matched zone and the coverage entry that matched, break and base, overage in counted steps, each surcharge as rule-then-amount — typeset like a stamped tariff page with the card-version chip as its provenance stamp; the export artifact is this sheet, typeset (06: "the artifact IS this sheet"). Motion stays clerical: the Settle's ≤120ms opacity crossfade marks which weight won the billing decision, the results re-sort instantly, money never counts up while being read, and success is a state chip plus one line of arithmetic ("الأرخص: 17.60 ريال على 'سلسلة التوصيل'" — 11 D) — the arithmetician's kind of celebration. Icons are thin editorial outlines on the line-1.5 grid — concentric arcs for a zone (a ring of coverage), a beam-scale for weight, an isometric cube for the box, a ruler-with-slash for the divisor — with directional glyphs flipping in RTL and semantic ones never flipping. No tracking-blue, no delivery-orange, no KPI tiles, no sparkle: the nearest famous neighbor is courier chrome, and the distance strategy is to be a book instead of a brand (05 brand-distance check).
