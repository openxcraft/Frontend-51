# 00 — Brief: shipping-rate-comparator

Family: commerce_integrations · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A rate comparison workspace for shipping managers who run more than one courier relationship. The product's raw material is the thing every merchant already owns and no comparison tool treats as data: **the negotiated rate card** — the pricing table a courier channel sends after a sales negotiation, with its own zone system (which destinations cost what), its own weight breaks (0–1 kg, 1–3 kg, 3–5 kg…), its own dimensional-weight divisor (the number that decides whether a bulky-light box bills by scale or by tape measure), and its own surcharge stack (fuel percentages, remote-area fees, cash-on-delivery handling). Today that card lives in a spreadsheet, or in an email attachment nobody re-reads, and the "comparison" happens in a manager's head at 4pm with a calculator. Here, the card becomes structured, versioned, inspectable data: zones with destination coverage, breaks with prices, a divisor, surcharge definitions — entered by hand or pasted from the courier's own table — and the card is validated before it can rate anything (every zone covers at least one destination, every break has a price, the break ladder is contiguous from zero).

The second half is the comparison engine, and it is deliberately deterministic arithmetic, not an oracle (ai-native-patterns §1 declaration: **non-AI**; every figure below is computable by hand). A **shipment profile** — destination, actual weight, box dimensions, optional declared value and cash-on-delivery amount — runs through every active card the manager selects. Each run performs the same traceable chain per card: normalize the destination and resolve the card's zone (a destination can be Zone 2 on one card and Zone B on another — zone systems are carrier-specific, research digest Q4/Q38); compute volumetric weight from the box dimensions against that card's divisor; take chargeable weight as the greater of actual and volumetric, rounded up to the card's billing step; look up the weight break; apply base price plus overage beyond the table; apply each surcharge by its own rule; and produce a quote **with its full arithmetic visible** — the manager never sees a total without the chain that produced it. Results sit side by side, sorted within each currency, with the cheapest per card set flagged, no-coverage and out-of-range results named honestly, and every quote pinned to the card version that produced it. The same profile can be re-run after a card is updated to see exactly what the renewal changed — which is the product's quiet second job: **negotiation evidence**. Comparisons export as procurement-ready artifacts, and a comparison can be pinned as the documented basis of a courier renewal discussion.

The product stops at the quote, deliberately. It does not book, print, or dispatch anything at v1: label purchase is an abstract channel seam declared in 08 (the manual path is copying the chosen quote's reference into whatever booking system the merchant already uses), no tracking page exists (delivery status is somebody else's system), and claims management is out (a disputes workflow is a different product). It also refuses two tempting lies: it does not fetch live courier rates at v1 (a rate-quote capability is an abstract, optional binding — the workspace's own cards are the primary data, and they are always in hand), and it does not convert currencies (no FX data is invented; comparisons group quotes by currency and rank within each, recorded in 12). What it owns completely is the arithmetic between "here is what I ship" and "here is what each relationship will charge" — the exact surface where spreadsheet merchants lose money to surcharges they forgot to model (01 evidence).

## Buyer

Shipping managers at MENA e-commerce merchants and distributors running two or more courier relationships — the person who owns the shipping line of the P&L, answers for COD re-attempt fees, and re-negotiates rate cards every renewal cycle. Daily users are the manager themself and the operations specialists who decide, parcel by parcel or batch by batch, which courier today's shipments ride on; the finance/procurement analyst is the secondary seat, consuming pinned comparisons as the evidence file for the next negotiation (personas: Khalid, Noura, Hana, Adel — 02). Economic buyer = the shipping manager; this is a per-seat team tool, not a solo gadget, because the card library and comparison history only pay off when the same workspace feeds both daily routing and the annual renewal.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px comparison workbench with profile form and results side by side; responsive to 360px in list/read modes). Zones: cards home (library, filters, onboarding checklist); card editor (zones, breaks, surcharges, dimensional policy); paste-import surface; shipment profiles library; comparison workbench (profile + live chargeable-weight calculator + results table); comparison detail (breakdown sheet, pin, export); comparison history (singles and batches); jobs console; settings (defaults, normalization disclosure, capability consent state); ⌘K command palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three). No mobile app; no public API at v1; no label production anywhere in the product (LOOP-10).

## Unique feature

**Zone-based rate cards with dimensional-weight modeling** — defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) every retrieved rate-comparison tool in the category is live-API-shaped — it rates shipments through carrier connections the merchant must grant, at checkout or at label time; none treats the merchant's own negotiated rate cards as the primary data object, yet the negotiated card is exactly what MENA merchants hold (regional couriers publish single-origin calculators, not multi-card comparisons — Q9); the closest mechanism precedent found is a landed-cost tool's rate-sheet upload for when "API connections aren't available" (Zonos docs, Q10) — a workaround there, the whole product here. (2) Zone systems are carrier-specific by design ("the exact numbering, mileage bands, and fee structure vary" — Q38), which is precisely why a same-destination quote must resolve through each card's own zone table — a structural step no single-zone-tool and no live-API mirror performs honestly. (3) Dimensional-weight divisors differ by carrier and service (139 in/lb retail, 166 negotiated, 5000 cm³/kg express, 6000 air freight — Q16/Q18/Q27); a comparison that ignores which divisor applies is wrong in the exact place bulky-light e-commerce boxes live (01 complaint evidence: surprise charges on invoices).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No label printing, booking, or dispatch at v1** — the chosen quote's handoff is an export/copy artifact; a label/booking channel is an abstract seam (08) with the manual path primary. No courier API bindings, no label PDFs, no pickup scheduling.
- **No tracking page** — no shipment status, no event feeds, no delivery timelines; the product ends at the quote.
- **No claims management** — no disputes, no damage/refund workflows, no carrier-liability tracking.
- **No live courier rate fetching at v1** — a rate-quote capability is an abstract optional binding (08 N1, consent-gated); the workspace's own cards are the primary and complete data path (LOOP-5).
- **No storefront or order-source import** — shipment profiles are authored or pasted by the user; importing live order streams belongs to the order-side products (LOOP-3 boundary with cod-order-confirmation: their manifest is an export; entering its destinations here is a human's choice, never a live join).
- **No currency conversion** — no FX rates are bundled or invented; comparisons group quotes by currency and rank within each group (12 Q-9).
- **No route optimization, no fulfillment planning, no multi-stop logic** — a quote is a price answer, not a logistics plan.
- **No AI anywhere** — declared non-AI; rate and dimensional-weight arithmetic is deterministic computation (07a/07b/07c); no generation, no suggestions, no "smart" affordances (ai-native-patterns §1).

## Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done (success signals)

A first-session user can: paste a courier rate table and watch it parse into a draft card with bad rows named row-by-row; fix the two bad rows and activate the card after its completeness check; enter the standard box profile (2.4 kg, 35×25×15 cm) and watch the live calculator settle chargeable weight at 3 kg — volumetric winning, the arithmetic shown; run a comparison across three cards and read why the cheapest one is cheapest (zone 2, break 3 kg, fuel on base, COD fee off); run a batch of six pasted destinations through the same box and see per-destination cheapest-per-card with one honest no-coverage row; pin that comparison, export it as a table artifact, and re-run it after a card price edit to see the delta — all on the Mock Adapter, zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI, with every abstract capability unbound from the first minute (hand-entered cards and copy-paste handoff work end to end).
