# 00 — Brief: abandoned-cart-recovery

Family: commerce_integrations · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A recovery console for D2C merchants whose shoppers fill a cart and vanish. Shopping carts that were started but never submitted arrive from a **cart source** — the storefront or marketplace platform the merchant already runs, an abstract channel this product does not own — and each cart lands in the recovery queue as a first-class work object: line items in both of the merchant's languages, a total, a shopper locale (ar or en), a jurisdiction, a consent basis, and a deterministic priority score for queue ordering. The product's unit of authorship is the **recovery sequence**: a versioned, inspectable ladder of message steps — when to write, on which channel kind, in which template, with which incentive — where every message body is authored **bilingually** (an English column and an Arabic column, side by side, per step) and routed per shopper locale. The console never sends anything itself: delivery is delegated to an abstract **message channel** (email-class, rich-message-class, plain-text-class kinds — capability language, never vendors), and when no channel is bound the product is still complete, because the **send-list export** hands the merchant copy-ready, per-shopper messages to dispatch through whatever they already use (LOOP-5).

The signature mechanism is **quiet-hours-aware scheduling**. Every sequence step carries a delay from its anchor event, and the scheduler computes each send's due time in the *shopper's* timezone against a per-jurisdiction send-window policy — window rows the merchant configures from retrieved statutory anchors (US federal 8am–9pm and its stricter state variants are the only hour-level rules our research retrieved; GCC windows ship as honest workspace policy, labeled as such, because no retrieved source publishes GCC messaging hours). A send whose due time falls outside the window is **deferred to the window's next opening** — visibly: the send ledger shows the state `deferred`, the local time that triggered it, the policy row that governed it, and the exact rescheduled time. Nothing silent ever ships. Opt-outs are the harder stop: an opt-out event cancels every scheduled and deferred send for that shopper mid-flight, writes a suppression-ledger entry, and holds future carts out of recovery at intake. Around the sequences sit **value-band routing** (cart total → band → sequence variant → incentive policy, so a 40-riyal cart never gets the 15% code built for a 1,500-riyal one — the flat-discount pain practitioners report in the wild) and a deterministic recovery report that reconciles carts, sends, deferrals, and recovered revenue from recorded events, never from vendor promises.

The AI stance is **conditionally-AI**, and it is one surface wide: bilingual recovery-copy generation (draft subject/body variants from a base template) may be bound to an abstract generation capability; every generated variant is draft-by-default with provenance `generated`, promotes only through a human review-diff into a *draft* template, and still needs explicit template approval before any sequence can bind it (two human gates, P15). When the capability is unbound, rate-limited, or fails, the product degrades to the **template library** — the seeded, versioned, bilingual stock the merchant already curates — and nothing else changes: scheduling, banding, deferral, suppression, reporting, and export are deterministic rules with no model anywhere near them. The conditionally-AI declaration is enforced structurally (07a/07b/08): no AI operation exists on the adapter outside `generateMessageVariants` and its draft-retrieval/promotion pair.

## Surface

Responsive web console, desktop-first (≥1280px, keyboard-first — the queue is a speed surface) with a mobile check-in view (≥360px) for queue review and report reads. Zones: recovery queue (home), cart detail with the send timeline, sequence studio (steps + bilingual template binding + the schedule preview), template library with the generation dock, routing & incentives, quiet-hours policy center, send ledger (the outbox with deferred states), suppression ledger, recovery report, jobs center, settings. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). Direction default **bilingual-en** (proposed, LOOP-6): English-first chrome for the operator desk with full Arabic chrome as a first-class citizen — while the *shopper-facing* copy inside every template is authored bilingually by construction. No buyer-facing surface is rendered by this product (LOOP-10: no onsite personalization, no storefront).

## Unique feature

**Bilingual recovery sequences with quiet-hours-aware scheduling** — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the category's timing doctrine is genuinely contested (Omnisend 30–60 minutes vs Klaviyo 2–4 hours for the first email — 01), so step delays are versioned, editable policy with a deterministic **schedule preview** that renders the whole timeline — including every quiet-hours deferral — against any timezone *before* activation; nobody else's flow builder makes the clock legible; (2) quiet-hours enforcement exists in the category as a buried compliance checkbox (a platform help article — 01), while here it is the scheduling model itself: per-jurisdiction window rows, recipient-timezone evaluation, visible deferral states, and deferral statistics in the report; (3) bilingual (ar/en) message authoring as a first-class two-column surface with per-locale length budgeting — the regional reality every MENA merchant lives and every Western tool treats as a locale dropdown; (4) margin-aware value-band routing of sequences and incentives — the practice recovery vendors describe (value-proportional discounts, 01) made inspectable and editable, so discount discipline is a policy artifact, not a habit.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No send infrastructure** — the product owns zero delivery. Outbound messaging is an abstract message channel (email-class / rich-message-class / plain-text-class) declared with consent semantics and a manual alternative; the send-list export with copy-ready per-shopper messages is the always-available path (LOOP-5).
- **No onsite personalization** — no on-site widgets, pop-ups, exit-intent overlays, or storefront rendering of any kind. The product's boundary is the outbound message; what the shopper sees when they return is the merchant's storefront, not ours.
- **No coupon fraud tooling** — no per-recipient unique-code generation, no redemption verification, no abuse detection. An incentive is a code string the merchant's own systems issue plus the policy that governs when it is offered; guarding it is another product's job.
- **No checkout, no storefront, no buyer chat** — carts arrive as data from the abstract cart source; replies return as typed events. A chat thread would recreate the group-chat chaos this product replaces.
- **No AI outside copy generation** — scheduling, banding, priority scoring, deferral, and reporting are deterministic rules; the single conditionally-AI surface degrades to the template library (ai-native-patterns §1/§8).
- **No broadcast/campaign blasting** — recovery sequences are anchored to a cart and a shopper with per-cart state; one-to-many campaign sends are out of scope (the buyer's existing marketing platform owns them).
- **No deliverability analytics beyond delivery/click events** — open-rate diagnostics, spam-placement tooling, and sender-reputation management belong to the bound channel, not this console.

## Success signals (definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

A retention team can: paste-import carts from the manual path (zero credentials, LOOP-5); stand up a three-step bilingual sequence with quiet-hours preview in under 30 minutes; watch the schedule preview defer a 23:40-local send to the next window opening before anything ships; work the queue (override a sequence, cancel a recovery, record a manual shopper event, suppress a shopper); export the copy-ready send list and the recovery report and retrieve both artifacts before expiry — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full flow completing with every abstract capability unbound. Replaying the identical session a second time produces byte-identical state (determinism oracle, 07c).
