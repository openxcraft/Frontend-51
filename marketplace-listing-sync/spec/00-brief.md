# 00 — Brief: marketplace-listing-sync

Family: commerce_integrations · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A listing manager for multichannel sellers whose catalog is bilingual (Arabic + English) and whose channels are not. The seller keeps **one master catalog** — each product carrying paired locale fields (title, description, bullet highlights, brand, attributes) plus images and the commercial data (price, stock level) — and the product maps that catalog onto each sales **channel** through an explicit, inspectable **field mapping**: this master field, in this locale variant, with this transform, into that channel attribute. The mapping is a versioned object the seller can read line by line — never an implicit "sync" that decides for them. On top of the mapping runs the product's second spine: a **per-marketplace compliance checklist** — a versioned rule pack per channel (title ceiling, forbidden characters, required attributes, image minimums, bilingual parity, bullet counts) evaluated by deterministic arithmetic against the mapped result, so every listing carries a live readiness verdict: blocked with named blockers, or ready, or ready-with-warnings. The same input always produces the same verdict — which is what a listing operation that feeds real channels requires (00's deterministic declaration is structural, 07a/07b/07c).

The product ends at the listing's readiness and its handoff. A ready listing **publishes** through an abstract channel capability when one is bound, or exports as a channel-ready file the seller uploads by hand — the manual path is primary and permanent (LOOP-5). The buyer's evidence that this is the right shape is the failure class the category itself documents: most listing rejections trace to missing required attributes, bad images, product-ID conflicts, or rule violations — data problems, not connection problems (research digest §B.3: sellenvo; salest.io paraphrase — "most failed marketplace exports come down to missing data rather than a broken connection"). Channel-agnostic feed-tool vendors already sell "prebuilt feed maps with required fields, validations, and mapping guidance to reduce SKU rejections before feed submission" (godatafeed) — for Latin-first catalogs. What no retrieved competitor carries is the bilingual pair as the catalog's spine: a mapping where the **locale variant is a first-class dimension** (this channel takes the Arabic title in one attribute and the English title in another; that channel takes Arabic only; this one truncates the English title at a different ceiling than the Arabic one), with per-marketplace checklists that treat bilingual parity as a rule, not an accident. MENA sellers get the advice "use both English and Arabic keywords, adapt product descriptions to local preferences" from every practitioner source (digest §B.4) — and a tool to enforce it per channel.

The AI stance is **conditionally-AI** (declared in 07a/07b/08): exactly one surface — **bilingual field-mapping suggestions** — may be produced by an abstract mapping-assist capability when a workspace binds one, ranking candidate master-field→channel-attribute pairings with disclosed confidence and a deterministic token-overlap fallback formula the mock implements exactly. Every suggestion is draft-by-default: it applies only when a human accepts it, edits it, or rejects it, and the manual mapping editor is the full citizen — when the capability is unbound, rate-limited, or fails, the product is a complete manual mapping desk (the degradation is a UI state, not a hole). No AI writes listing content: titles, descriptions, and bullets are the seller's own bilingual data or their own edits; no AI touches prices (price rules are price-rule-engine's family, LOOP-10); no AI judges compliance — the checklists are arithmetic.

## Buyer

Multichannel sellers at MENA trade and D2C businesses running 100–5,000 SKUs across two to five channels — the home-goods, electronics-accessories, fragrance and bakhoor, gift, and grocery-import sellers of Riyadh, Jeddah, Dubai, and Cairo who sell on regional bilingual marketplaces, global marketplaces with regional sites, and Arabic-first storefront platforms. The economic buyer is the founder or e-commerce lead who answers when a channel freezes listings for rule violations or when a catalog expansion dies in week two under copy-paste. Daily users: the **catalog manager** (owns master data and field mappings — today a person with a spreadsheet and three seller dashboards open), the **channel owner** (owns one channel's rule pack and publish decisions), and a **viewer** seat for the founder who reads readiness without editing. 2–6 seats; this is a team tool with per-seat economics (12 Q-3). Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px; the mapping table and the bilingual product editor are keyboard-dense work surfaces), responsive to 360px for readiness reads and job monitoring on the road. Zones: channels home (channel cards with connection and readiness state); channel setup with its rule-pack editor; catalog (master products, filters, import); master product editor with side-by-side bilingual columns (P23); **field map studio** — the core surface — a mapping table (master field + locale variant + transform → channel attribute) beside the channel's attribute catalog and, when bound, the suggestion panel; listings board (state chips per channel); listing detail with the mapped preview, per-rule checklist results, and channel overrides; readiness console (findings across a channel); publish & exports; jobs center; settings. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

**Bilingual field mapping with per-marketplace compliance checklists** — defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the mapping table as an inspectable, versioned object with locale-variant selection and declared transforms (truncate at word boundary, template composition) — the discipline feed-management vendors productize for single-locale catalogs, here extended to the pair dimension no verified competitor carries; (2) the checklist as workspace-editable rule data with the category's real numbers seeded as demonstration values (title ceilings that moved 200→75 characters in enforced waves, image minimums, forbidden character sets — 01 §2), each rule carrying blocker/warning severity so readiness is a verdict, not a vibe; (3) readiness-before-publish as the product's gate: publish is refused per listing with named blockers (a channel-capability write is never attempted with a blocked listing), and the manual export path carries the same checklist verdict into the file the seller uploads — so the product is honest even with zero channels connected.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No repricing** — listings carry a price value and a stock level as data, and the publish path can update those fields on channels; but no price rules, no margin simulation, no scheduled price changes, no competitor-aware logic — price-rule-engine owns that family (boundary in 12 Q-5; roster row).
- **No order management** — the product's state ends at the published listing. No order import, no fulfillment, no courier selection; a listing's downstream orders belong to the channel and to order-side products (cod-order-confirmation owns pre-dispatch confirmation; boundary in 12 Q-6).
- **No ad management** — no sponsored-placement logic, no campaign budgets, no ad copy variants.
- **No machine translation of catalog content** — the bilingual pair is the seller's own data; an abstract translation-assist is NOT declared as an integration need (translation authoring is another family's world — 12 Q-7 records the translation-review-hub candidate as text-handoff only).
- **No listing analytics** — no sales performance, no search-rank tracking, no channel revenue views (analytics family owns analysis; exports are the interop).
- **No count-session ingestion** — stock truth from cycle counts arrives only as seller-entered data at v1; inventory-count-assistant's adjustment sheets are exportable artifacts on their side and nothing here consumes them live (12 Q-4 — the count-verified-quantity feed is an owner decision, never a v1 default).
- **No buyer-facing surfaces** — the product renders no storefront; it manages the seller's side of listings only.

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

A catalog manager can: create a channel from a bilingual-marketplace archetype with its seeded rule pack and edit one rule's number; import a 24-product bilingual catalog by paste (zero credentials, LOOP-5); open the field map studio and map the Arabic title, English title, and stock level to channel attributes — with the mapping-assist capability unbound, entirely by hand; bind the simulated assist in the mock and accept a high-confidence suggestion, edit a medium one, and reject a wrong one; run the compliance check and read the readiness console — four products blocked with named blockers (an over-ceiling title, a missing Arabic description, zero images, a forbidden character); fix each blocker and watch the verdicts flip to ready; create listings in bulk for the ready set; publish to the bound channel capability with one injected channel rejection surfacing as a typed error on one listing while the rest succeed; export a channel file for the unbound storefront channel and retrieve the artifact before expiry; edit a master title and watch dependent listings go stale with re-validate-and-re-publish offered — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full loop completing with every abstract capability unbound except the explicitly exercised publish-capability and assist fixtures.
