## Category overview

Two categories collide here. **SMB procurement / purchase-to-pay platforms** (Precoro, Procurify, Tradogram class) bundle our exact mechanics — approval routing with thresholds and conditions [14], live budget impact [12], budget control with soft/hard stops [40], committed-spend tracking by cost center [39][41][42], quote upload attached to requests [35] — inside suite-shaped products that also carry POs, receipts, invoices, supplier modules, and payments. Aura's SMB comparison names "approval and budget control" as Precoro's strength and "finance-led spend and payments" as Procurify's [2]: our pairing exists, but as a module inside a suite, priced and shaped for teams that want the whole procure-to-pay machine. The second category is the **status quo itself**: the MENA practitioner's own description — "WhatsApp for the update, email for the approval, a spreadsheet to reconcile it later" [28] — and its global equivalent, the multi-channel email-approval sprawl that makes "every request… an exception" [63]. In the middle sit the conventions the product productizes: delegation-of-authority matrices as banded role-to-threshold policy (practitioner guides [15][16][17], tier examples [19], board-ratified institutional DOA documents [20]); the 3-quote rule as internal policy and audit best practice, explicitly "not a law" [22], with institutional thresholds ("$10,000 or more with the same vendor, a minimum of 3 documented quotes" unless legitimate sole-source [23]); and commitment-based budget checking by cost center, the convention enterprise ERP owns [41]. The wedge: a focused, bilingual, inspectable approval-chain instrument for teams that need the discipline without the suite — nothing in the verified landscape treats the DOA matrix + budget envelope + quote comparison triad as the product, and nothing treats ar/en metadata as first-class schema.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **The WhatsApp/email/spreadsheet status quo** (the true incumbent): Saudi practitioner sentiment (paraphrased) — "WhatsApp for the update, email for the approval, a spreadsheet to reconcile it later" [28]; approval sprawl makes every request an exception [63]; requisition pains named as "time-consuming data entry · lengthy approval processes · multiple communication channels" [27]. Free, flexible, already everywhere — and structurally unable to produce a routing preview, a commitment projection, or an audit trail.
- **ERP purchasing modules** (enterprise PR release-strategy class): header-level value-based release is enterprise precedent [26]; commitment management by cost center with over-budget reports [41]. The mechanics are proven at enterprise shape and price; SMBs do not buy an ERP for an approval chain.
- **Regional Arabic tools** (Disty [30], Supplies Hub [31], Madara [32]): order- and supplier-shaped regional products — centralizing orders, comparing products, quotations and POs — none positions the approval chain, DOA matrix, or bilingual request metadata as the product. Pricing not published in the retrieved set (honest gap).
- **Government e-procurement normalization (context, not a competitor)**: KSA's اعتماد/Etimad platform, launched 2018, unified government tenders and procurement digitally [33] — regional proof that digital procurement approval is normalized national practice, which lowers the cultural cost of adopting an approval-chain instrument in the private sector.

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- **The approval wait is the measured pain**: median requisition-to-PO cycle time is 55 hours (Procurify 2026 Procurement Benchmark & KPIs Report) [57]; top performers complete requisition-to-PO in under 40 hours while others take over 90 [58]; "most of the delay is waiting, on approvals and on data" entry [60]; one business-case account reports an average of 11 working days from request to approved order against a ~42-hour benchmark [61]; internal approvals + vendor selection consume ~3 days of a ~10-day procurement lead time [62]. Delay concentrates exactly where this product operates.
- **Non-adoption is the headline demand signal**: Spendesk research states 64% of finance teams have no [dedicated procurement software] [38]; the practitioner fix prescribed is standardization — "consistent request forms, documentation requirements, and approval criteria across departments" [64]; SMB procurement-process guides are mainstream chamber-of-commerce advice [65].
- **DOA matrices are established governance practice**: role-to-threshold mapping guides [15][16]; the canonical banded example ($500 supervisor / $10,000 manager / $50,000 VP) [17]; tiered examples [19]; board-ratified DOA threshold documents at institutions [20]; thresholds are entity-specific policy ("a $50,000 threshold may be appropriate for a mature market subsidiary but too high for a small emerging-market entity") [15] — ship editable policy data, never constants.
- **The 3-quote convention is policy-shaped**: "For most private businesses, no. It is a common internal policy and an audit best practice, not a law" [22]; institutional threshold example: ≥$10,000 aggregate with the same vendor requires a minimum of 3 documented quotes unless a legitimate sole-source exception [23]; RFQ = soliciting pricing to compare bids when specifications are known [21].
- **Budget commitment tracking is the category's own vocabulary**: budgets by department/cost center/GL/project with committed + actual tracked in real time [39]; soft/hard stops [40]; approved/committed/spent/remaining views [42]; commitment reports by cost center showing over-budget amounts [41].
- **MENA context makes it concrete**: the WhatsApp/email/spreadsheet triad as lived practice [28]; global vendors running KSA-targeted procurement pages ("centralizes requests, approvals, POs, receipts, and invoices. Set up custom approval workflows") [29]; regional spend platforms (Pemo) card- and payment-first with approval flows attached, CBUAE-supervised [47][48][49]; government digital procurement normalized since 2018 [33].

## Positioning statement

For ops and finance teams at MENA businesses that buy through requests, procurement-approval-chain is the approval instrument that turns spend-approval into inspectable policy: a delegation-of-authority matrix routes every request through the right chain for its amount, budget envelopes project the commitment before anyone signs, and vendor quotes compare with the lowest named and every other selection justified in writing. Unlike procure-to-pay suites, it ends at the approved request and its export — no POs, no invoices, no payments — and unlike the WhatsApp-and-email status quo, every decision carries its rule, its band, its evidence, and its name, in both languages.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns)

- SMB buyer sentiment on suites (q1/q2 comparison threads, paraphrased): paying for PO/invoice/supplier modules to get the approval discipline — the suite is the tax, the chain is the need.
- Finance sentiment on approval delay (q6/q15 sources, paraphrased): the wait is approvals and data entry, not the purchasing itself [60][27] — routing that names the next signer attacks the actual bottleneck.
- Approver sentiment (q4 DOA guides, paraphrased): authority lived in a spreadsheet nobody trusts at audit time; board-ratified DOA documents exist but the runtime doesn't [20].
- Requester sentiment in MENA (q7, paraphrased): approval requests vanish into a manager's chat list; "did you see my request?" is the real approval-tracking system [28].

## Market size (honest)

MENA SMB ops/finance teams buying through requests rather than cards — a slice of the global SMB procurement-software category whose entry tiers clear $99–$499/mo [44][8]. No auditable MENA-specific adoption or TAM figure exists (research honest gap); any number would be invented, so none is offered. Envelope: paid finance/ops seats with free requesters, the shape the region's bilingual request volume demands.
