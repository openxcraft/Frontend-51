# 00 — Brief: procurement-approval-chain

Family: business_ops · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

An approval workflow for company purchase requests at MENA ops/finance teams: a **purchase request** (spend not yet made) is drafted with its lines and its **vendor quotes**, submitted into a **delegation-of-authority (DOA) matrix** that routes it through the right approval chain for its amount, and either dies with a recorded rejection or emerges as an **approved request** — the commitment evidence the company hands to whoever actually buys. The product's spine is deterministic: the same request state, the same matrix, and the same budget state always yield the same routing, the same budget findings, and the same quote comparison — the properties an audit-facing artifact requires. The AI stance is **declared non-AI** (ai-native-patterns.md §1): no operation generates, scores, or transforms content by model; threshold routing is arithmetic, budget checking is subtraction against declared envelopes, quote comparison is ordering and deltas. There are no smart anything, and the specs must not leak AI affordances.

The unique feature is **budget-aware threshold routing with vendor-quote comparison**, built as three interlocking inspectable mechanisms. First, **threshold routing**: the DOA matrix is workspace-owned, versioned policy data — scope-keyed rules (workspace default, per cost center, per category) each carrying ascending amount bands, every band naming an ordered approval chain of positions ("≤ 5,000 → Department Manager; 5,001–50,000 → Department Manager → Finance Manager; above → + General Manager") — the banded role-to-threshold convention practitioners already run on paper (research q4: $500 supervisor / $10,000 manager / $50,000 VP; board-ratified DOA documents). Routing computes at the header level from the request total, exactly the release strategy enterprise ERP uses, and it computes **before** submission as a preview the requester can read ("who will sign this, and why"). Second, **budget-awareness**: budget envelopes (cost center × category × fiscal period) hold allocated amounts and derived commitments — in-flight plus approved requests commit against the envelope, so every submission projects `committed + this request` and yields a named state: within, approaching (≥ the workspace near-limit percentage), or over — advisory by default, blocking per envelope policy, with the soft/hard-stop convention the category sells as "budget control" rendered as inspectable policy data. Third, **vendor-quote comparison**: quotes attach to the request with typed totals, lead times, terms, and optional document references; comparison is deterministic — lowest total named, every other quote's delta in amount and percent, and selecting a quote that is **not the lowest requires a written justification** before submission passes — the 3-quote-rule discipline (research q5: common internal policy and audit best practice, "not a law") encoded as workspace policy (minimum quote count above a threshold, sole-source exception with recorded justification) rather than as a hard-coded rule.

The buyer's reality (01 evidence): the status quo at MENA SMBs is the practitioner's own description — "WhatsApp for the update, email for the approval, a spreadsheet to reconcile it later" (research q7) — while the category's tools are suite-shaped and Latin-first: Precoro/Procurify/Tradogram bundle approval routing inside procure-to-pay platforms priced from $99 to $499+ per month with POs, receipts, invoices, and supplier modules riding along; regional spend platforms are card- and payment-first; regional order-shaped tools skip the approval chain entirely. Spendesk's own research headline — 64% of finance teams have no dedicated procurement software (research q8) — names the gap: teams that need the *approval discipline* without the suite. This product is that instrument: it starts at "someone wants to spend money the company has not yet spent" and ends at "the request is approved, its commitment is recorded against the budget, and the package is exported" — no accounting posting, no vendor payments, no catalog (LOOP-10). The bilingual spine runs end to end: request titles, line descriptions, vendor names, and position titles are authored in both locales, rendered per-side direction, and searchable through Arabic-aware normalization — the same bilingual-metadata discipline this portfolio applies to contracts (contract-lifecycle-manager) and claims (expense-claim-flow), applied here to the spend commitment.

## Buyer

Ops and finance teams (2–6 finance/ops seats) at MENA businesses with 50–500 employees that buy through requests rather than cards: trading and distribution companies, contracting and facilities operators, clinics and school groups, retail head offices, growing tech companies. The economic buyer is the finance manager or ops manager who answers when an unapproved purchase embarrasses the company, a budget is silently overrun, or an auditor asks "who authorized this and on what evidence"; daily users split into three shapes — the **requester** (department staff who draft requests and collect quotes, Arabic-first or English-first), the **approver** (department managers, finance, and GM seats whose authority comes from the matrix), and the **policy owner** (the finance admin who authors the DOA matrix, budget envelopes, and quote policy). Requester seats are free (12 Q-3): taxing the person asking permission to spend is the inverted posture. Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary: request register, approval workspace, matrix studio, budgets console), responsive to 360px for requester drafting and one-handed approver review (read + decide; matrix/budget authoring stay desktop). Zones: requests home (my queue vs all requests, filters + saved views); request composer (lines, quotes, live routing preview rail); request detail / approval workspace (document-paper surface: lines, quote comparison, budget evidence, approval-chain rail, decision bar); budgets console (envelopes with commitment bars); approval-matrix studio (rules, bands, chains, policy pack); org & delegation (positions, members, delegation windows); vendors & cost centers (reference data); activity log; jobs & artifacts. ⌘K command palette (P05 — the register exceeds three list surfaces) with bilingual normalized search. No native app at v1; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

Budget-aware threshold routing with vendor-quote comparison — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **the routing engine is inspectable policy data, not a black box** — the DOA matrix is versioned, banded, scope-keyed, and every routing decision records which rule and which band fired; the preview shows the chain before submission, which is precisely the transparency the email-approval status quo cannot give; (2) **budget-awareness is commitment-based, not report-based** — envelopes track in-flight plus approved commitments in real projection (the cost-center commitment convention enterprise ERP owns, here as affordable workspace data), with near-limit and over-limit as named states attached to evidence; (3) **quote comparison carries the audit convention** — lowest named, deltas computed, non-lowest selection justified in writing, quote-count policy above thresholds with sole-source exception recorded; (4) **bilingual by construction** — no verified competitor in the category treats ar/en metadata (vendor names, line descriptions, request titles) as first-class schema rather than a locale setting. The deterministic stance is the fourth wall: same state → same routing, same findings, same deltas — never vibes.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No accounting posting** — approvals end at the approved-request record and its export; no ledger writes, no journal entries, no GL codes owned here (a cost-center scope key is organizational, not accounting). The export package is the handoff to whatever posts the money.
- **No vendor payments** — no payment execution, no payment scheduling, no payment rails, no vendor bank details. Vendor records are a name directory for quotes only.
- **No catalog management** — no item catalogs, price lists, preferred-catalog punch-outs, or contract pricing; request lines are free bilingual descriptions with quantities and estimated unit prices.
- No purchase-order generation at v1 — the approved request exports as an approval package (request + quotes + comparison + budget evidence + chain); issuing a PO is the downstream system's job (08 N2 manual alternative is the primary path).
- No vendor management beyond the name directory — no onboarding, no performance scoring, no compliance tracking, no negotiations.
- No goods receipt / invoice capture / 3-way matching — receiving and AP belong to purchasing and accounting systems.
- No AI of any kind — declared non-AI (07a/07b/07c honor it by construction; no extraction assist, no smart routing, no spend-analysis claims).
- No multi-currency at v1 — single workspace currency (SAR or AED at creation); quote amounts are entered in workspace currency (12 Q-13).
- No corporate cards / spend controls on cards — card-first platforms are the counter-position (01); this product starts before the money moves.

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

An ops/finance team can, in one first session on the Mock Adapter with zero provider-specific knowledge in the UI and every abstract capability unbound: author a DOA rule with two bands and read the policy pack's quote rules; create a budget envelope with an allocation and watch a request's projection land in the near-limit state; draft a request with bilingual lines; enter three vendor quotes by hand (no document capability needed) and watch the comparison name the lowest and its deltas; select the non-lowest quote and be refused until the justification is written; read the routing preview naming the exact chain and the band that fired; submit and watch the chain step; as the approver, return the request with a note; as the requester, edit and resubmit and watch the new chain version with the old one preserved; approve through the final level and watch the commitment land in the envelope; read the activity log entries for every mutation above; export the approval package and retrieve the artifact before expiry — with the full loop completing on manual paths alone (typed quotes, in-app review, download-and-copy export; LOOP-5 bar).
