## Indirect competitors (3)
- **Global video suites' auto-captions** (the "good enough" floor): free, disposable quality.
- **Human subtitling vendors**: the quality incumbents (Happy Scribe's own human-tier pricing [2] proves the premium); cost/turnaround is the attack surface.
- **General assistants for caption cleanup**: free but no time-linking, no typography policy, no export discipline.

## Demand signals
- Arabic video consumption is regionally dominant (mobile-first video across MENA — see campaign/social data in bilingual-content-calendar 01 [3][4][5] as proxy demand evidence); captioned video is an accessibility + sound-off-viewing necessity.
- Transcription vendors explicitly sell Arabic [1][3] — demand proven by their marketing spend; none sells Arabic caption CRAFT.
- Education/lecture captioning (a core buyer) is accessibility-driven — captions are the deliverable, quality-graded by readers.

## Positioning gaps
1. **Arabic typography discipline**: no competitor treats line-breaking, tashkeel, or numeral policy as product features (they're user luck).
2. **Reading-speed gates**: caption timing quality is judged by professionals; tools leave CPM/WPM to human memory.
3. **Burn-in specs as deliverable**: post houses need reproducible render instructions, not just files.
4. **Transcript-linked refinement (not ASR replacement)**: teams already have transcripts from vendors [1][3]; the refinement gap is unowned.

## Market size (honest)
Post-production and education teams are a focused pool: low-thousands of regional teams/shops. Per-seat + duration-metered pricing aligns with category norms ([2][4][5] per-minute/seat hybrid patterns). Envelope: seven-figure USD ARR potential in-region; strategically completes the content family's video chain (clip-cutter cuts, caption-lab perfects).
