# 02 — User Research: caption-lab

SME-B pass · Paraphrased sentiment only (marked).

## Persona 1 — Mariam, Post-Production Lead (primary)
**Role**: runs a 4-person subtitle/QC team at a Dubai post house; delivers Arabic-captioned client video weekly.
**JTBD**: "When a 40-minute lecture lands with a raw transcript, I want captions that respect Arabic line rules and reading speed without my team hand-checking every line, so delivery day isn't a firefight."
**Triggers**: client rejected captions for mid-word breaks; tashkeel rendered as clipped marks in a burn-in; reading speed left viewers pausing.
**Objections**: "ASR tools dump text and call it captions"; "my styles must reproduce in the client's render farm"; "per-minute fees punish long lectures".
**Onboarding motive**: import one real lecture transcript and export corrected captions + style spec in the first session.
**Verbatim (paraphrase, subtitler sentiment)**: "Captions are typography under time pressure; tools treat them like text dumps."

## Persona 2 — Adel, E-learning Media Specialist
**Role**: produces course videos for a Riyadh training company; captions are an accessibility requirement and a comprehension aid.
**JTBD**: "When I caption a course module, I want reading-speed gates per audience (students vs professionals), so nobody pauses to read."
**Triggers**: learner complaints about fast captions; inconsistent numeral rendering (Arabic-Indic vs Western mixed mid-course).
**Objections**: budget per-seat small; must export to the authoring tool's expected formats; dialect-laden audio produces messy transcripts (he imports, doesn't transcribe).
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "Accessibility isn't the bonus feature; it's the reason captions exist."

## Persona 3 — Huda, Broadcast Digital Desk Editor
**Role**: digital desk at a regional broadcaster; clips and captions news video daily; speed is existence.
**JTBD**: "When news breaks, I want the caption refinement pass to be minutes, not a shift, so the desk ships first."
**Triggers**: desk fell behind on a breaking story due to caption fixing; burn-in styles varied across desk members.
**Objections**: must be FAST (keyboard-driven); style presets must be desk-shared; file formats must drop into the desk's existing player chain.
**Verbatim (paraphrase, news desk culture)**: "If it adds a step, it's a problem; if it removes ten, we're buying."

## Voice derivation (SME-J)
Lab voice: bench-craft precision — measurements spoken plainly ("this line reads at 21 CPM — your gate is 17"). Arabic فصحى بيضاء; fixed terminology (سطر=caption line، سرعة القراءة=reading speed، حرق=burn-in). English: professional-neutral. Numbers always carry their unit — this is a measurement product.
