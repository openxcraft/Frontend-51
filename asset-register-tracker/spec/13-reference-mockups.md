## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T3-appropriate)

The Steel Ledger. The register renders as a ruled accounting page that never ends: one continuous steel-ruled table under a cool paper sky, rows thin as ledger lines, the tag column in tabular steel figures so the eye can walk the column like a thumb walks a printed register. There are no cards floating over dashboards, no shadows, no glass — a border step is the only elevation, and state is a quiet chip at the row's inline-end edge: slate for in-stock, steel for held, amber for past-due, plum for corrected, grey for frozen. Open an asset and the page becomes its **custody chain**: a vertical rail of events, each a ruled entry with actor, parties, condition, and — where the moment was signed — an amber seal ring around a pen-mark, the single warm note in a steel system, reserved for the instant responsibility changed hands. Corrections never vanish: they hang off the chain as plum-ruled addenda that quote the entry they correct, because a register that rewrites itself is worthless. The label sheet is the product's most physical surface: a print-fidelity grid of square-cut labels, each carrying the QR mark, the Latin-digit tag, and the asset's name in both scripts — one label, two readers — bordered by a cutting guide that says, in both languages, exactly what it is. The scan companion is the ledger's pocket twin: camera or typed tag, one card, one thumb, three taps to a signed hand-off, offline-tolerant like a clipboard with a conscience. Success is a state chip and one line of arithmetic — "27 أصلاً تجاوزت موعد استرجاعها" — and the register goes back to being still. Everything moves at ≤120ms or not at all; the loudest thing on any screen is the truth.

## Mood anchors
