## Category overview

Three product classes surround this product, and none of them *is* it. **TMS/CAT platforms** (memoQ, Phrase, Smartcat, Crowdin, XTM, Lokalise class) carry translation production end-to-end — projects, TMs, MT, vendor workflows — with review folded in as a step for people who already live inside the platform [q1]. **QA checkers** (ApSIC Xbench, Verifika, QA Distiller class) run batch error scans — terminology, consistency, missing/untranslated text, numbers — over bilingual files, inside desktop CAT workflows, and report; the review *conversation* then happens elsewhere [q2]. **LQA services and modules** (Argos-class in-country review; Smartling LQA; MQM/DQF scoring) evaluate translated content against objective error schemas, typically post-hoc and analyst-facing [q5][q7]. translation-review-hub is the reviewer-layer workspace between them: it assumes translation exists, gives the ar/en reviewer a side-by-side surface to *decide* in, and computes the deterministic checks (terminology, length-fit, consistency) at decision time. Demand signals are structural, not speculative: terminology inconsistency is a named rework driver in localization practice (RWS: "inconsistent terminology leads to rework, delays and efficiency…" [q3]); per-string translation length limits are a productized TMS feature (Smartling Help [q4]) while open-source platforms still discuss max-length checks as an idea (Weblate discussion [q4]); and Arabic specifically stacks locale + script-direction + layout decisions simultaneously (Smartling [q8]) with RTL-adjacent text corruption and label-overflow as documented failure classes (Localazy; Weglot: "Arabic translations often end up longer than their English-language counterparts, potentially breaking carefully-crafted layouts" [q8]).

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **Spreadsheets and tracked-changes documents** — the true status quo: bilingual tables in a shared sheet, comments in margins, sign-off by email. Free, universally understood, and structurally unable to enforce terminology or compute length budgets at read time; every direct competitor's marketing implicitly fights it.
- **PerfectIt-class style checkers** (http://www.proz.com/forum/software_applications/308807-qa_tools_like_xbench_or_verifika_with_perpetual_license.html) — document-level style/consistency checking inside word processors; monolingual-first, no segment pairing, no bilingual glossary enforcement.
- **Ad-hoc assistant use** — teams paste segment pairs into general-purpose chat assistants asking "find terminology issues". Zero seat cost, zero enforcement memory: no glossary persistence, no audit trail, no sign-off gate — findings don't survive the session (pattern paraphrased from q3/q5 practitioner content; the PsychoPy Arabic localization study independently reports contextually inaccurate AI translations as a challenge class [q3]).
- **Agency in-country review services** (https://www.argosmultilingual.com/language-quality-evaluation/linguistic-quality-assurance-lqa ; https://poeditor.com/blog/in-country-review) — the ceiling above us: human-led LQA engagements. We don't replace the humans; we give internal teams and small LSPs the workspace layer that makes the same review defensible and repeatable in-house.

## Market and demand evidence (with sources)

1. **Terminology consistency is a budgeted pain, not a nice-to-have**: RWS — "When terminology is inconsistent, even experienced linguists struggle… leads to rework, delays" (https://www.rws.com/blog/terminology-management-in-localization); Acolad frames the corporate practice as identify→localize→approve→centralize (https://www.acolad.com/en/services/translation/corporate-terminology-management).
2. **Term severity is a category convention**: critical-vs-advisory glossary terms in Xbench-class tools (https://snapintel.io/blog/best-translation-qa-tools-in-2026-xbench-verifika-and-the-alternatives-compared); termbase-driven QA is core curriculum for translators (https://mitratranslations.com/en/automatic-qa-reports-a-beginner-guide-to-qa-checker-verifika-and-apsic-xbench).
3. **Length limits are productized practice**: per-string translation length limits as a shipped TMS feature (https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004155413-Set-Translation-Length-Limits); expansion-rate guidance is canonical W3C/IBM content for European targets (https://www.w3.org/International/articles/article-text-size.en.html; https://www.tcworld.info/e-magazine/translation-and-localization/changes-in-text-size-and-how-to-tackle-them-1190); 30–50% European expansion figures circulate in practitioner guidance (https://www.getblend.com/blog/fixing-linguistic-ui-bugs); CJK-style *contraction* overflow shows count-only heuristics fail (https://chatscontrol.com/blog/text-contraction-cjk-translation). **No published ar↔en expansion table surfaced in our searches** — budgets must be declared, not assumed (recorded as honest gap → 12 Q-11).
4. **Arabic stacks the hard problems**: locale + script-direction + layout at once (https://www.smartling.com/questions/arabic-content-localization); RTL layout breakage as challenge #1 (https://localazy.com/blog/6-challenges-of-localizing-your-app-to-arabic-and-how-to-solve-them); Arabic translations overflowing layouts (https://www.weglot.com/blog/arabic-localization); engine-level RTL bugs in the wild (https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/issues-with-arabic-language-support-small-font-size-and-lack-of-right-to-left-rtl-text-direction/89158; https://discussions.unity.com/t/arabic-localization-our-experience/597598).
5. **Review-as-workflow has mature definitions to align with, cheaply**: LQA "against a defined schema of objective errors" (https://help.smartling.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260806295070-LQA-Overview); LQA across the lifecycle, not only post-hoc (https://www.interproinc.com/what-is-linguistic-quality-assurance); in-country review as a distinct, purchasable layer (https://poeditor.com/blog/in-country-review).
6. **Quality frameworks give the artifact vocabulary**: MQM dimensions incl. Terminology (https://www.w3.org/community/mqmcg/mqm-terminology; https://www.themqm.org); DQF-MQM error typology (https://community.translatorswb.org/t/dqf-mqm-error-typology/10827); fit-for-purpose posture (https://www.acclaro.com/resources/fit-for-purpose-a-new-better-way-to-think-about-translation-quality-in-2025).
7. **MSA glossary fragmentation is real**: "a singular Modern Standard Arabic is a misconception" (https://blog.jarrousse.org/2024/03/27/modern-standard-arabic-the-missing-glossary) — workspace-level glossaries, not universal termbase claims, are the honest posture; Arabic technical-term standardization has decades of case studies (https://www.trans-int.org/index.php/transint/article/view/591).

## Positioning statement

For MENA localization teams reviewing ar↔en content, translation-review-hub is the reviewer-layer workspace that turns already-translated documents into defensible sign-offs: side-by-side panes that respect each script's direction, deterministic terminology enforcement against the team's own glossary with critical/advisory severity, length-fit warnings against declared character budgets, and a sign-off gate that refuses to pass unexplained critical findings. Unlike QA checkers that batch-report inside translator CAT workflows (Xbench, Verifika), it is where the human review decisions actually happen; unlike TMS platforms (memoQ, Phrase, Smartcat, Crowdin), it does not ask the team to run translation production to get a review surface — and unlike any of them, the ar/en pair with per-pane direction, dual-way jobs (en→ar and ar→en), and a no-published-expansion-table honesty about length budgets is the product's center, not a locale checkbox.
