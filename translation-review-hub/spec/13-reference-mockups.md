## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T2-appropriate)

**The Jisr Desk.** A reviewer's desk drawn in one deep bridge-teal line — flat borders on cool paper, no shadows, no glass, no gradient. The review workspace reads as a ruled bridge: the segment navigator marches down the inline-start edge like a ledger column (numbers Western, one physical order in both directions); the center is the span itself — source pane and target pane facing each other across a hairline seam, each flowing its own way (English leftward, Arabic rightward, or mirrored for the ar→en job), the pair number sitting ON the seam like a keystone so the two texts are never mistaken for one document. The findings rail on the inline-end edge is the desk's margin: red-lined critical chips, amber advisories, plum length meters, slate sibling links — every one carrying its verdict in text, because a reviewer trusts ink, not tint. The LengthFitMeter is a ruler drawn beside the target pane — the limit etched, the current length a solid fill, the ratio typeset in tabular figures; over-budget, and the ruler's mark goes plum, then red — arithmetic you can read at arm's length. The whole product leans toward one moment: the **sign-off gate**, a simple bordered frame at the workspace's end that lists its blockers like a customs manifest and refuses, in plain language, until the manifest is empty — and when it clears ("The Gate Clears", the T2 signature), the blocker rows fold away in a crisp ≤60ms stagger and leave a single teal seal with actor and timestamp. Everything else is stillness: chips arrive and dissolve in ≤140ms, nothing bounces, nothing sparkles, and the only thing that ever moves twice is nothing.
