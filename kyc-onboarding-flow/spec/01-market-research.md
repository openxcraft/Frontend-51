## 1. Category frame

The buyer already pays for the failure. An abandoned KYC application is pure customer-acquisition cost with no offsetting revenue — the ad spend worked, the install worked, the motivation existed, and the funnel ate it. The retrieved abandonment band is broad but unambiguous: 40–70% of prospects abandon financial onboarding before completion (Lorikeet, https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/kyc-kyb-completion-rate), 60–80% average onboarding abandonment across sectors depending on verification-step count (Jumio, https://www.jumio.com/how-to-reduce-customer-abandonment), 68% of consumers abandoning financial applications in Signicat's Battle to Onboard research (https://www.signicat.com/beyond-compliance/boost-conversion-make-app-onboarding-abandonment-temporary), and even the institutional-client version of the pain prices at "an estimated $3.3 billion annually in lost business" (Fenergo, vendor claim, https://www.fenergo.com/blogs/customer-onboarding-expectations-vs-reality). Within that funnel, one step dominates: **document upload, with 30–40% drop-off — the highest of any step** (Lorikeet, URL above) — and the named causes are "document burden" (the required identity document not at hand, or unwilling to photograph it — OneID, https://oneid.uk/news-and-events/identity-verification-abandonment-cost) and discomfort with the verification process itself (53% abandonment among uncomfortable users — Dojah, https://dojah.io/blog/customer-onboarding-fintech-identity-verifcation). The category that sells against this pain splits into four clusters: full-stack identity verification platforms (applicant-flow SDK + decisioning engine + review console bundled), per-check verification APIs, enterprise onboarding suites (bank-side), and the in-house flow built on an SDK — the status quo at most MENA fintechs. Nobody in the retrieved set sells **the applicant flow and review console as the product, capability-abstract, bilingual-ar, with resumable multi-session applications as a first-class entity**. That is the wedge.

## 3. Indirect competitors (5)

- **The in-house flow built on an IDV SDK** (the incumbent that actually runs MENA fintech onboarding): a product team embeds a verification SDK, wraps it in three screens, wires it to a ticketing board for manual review, and rebuilds resume behavior with a session cookie. Zero license cost beyond the checks; the hidden costs are engineering time on flow edge cases (timeout, document-not-at-hand, rework loops), a review process living in a general-purpose ticket tool with no reason-code discipline, and no audit chain an auditor respects. This product must beat it on the operations lead's own ergonomics (the 30-minute spine stand-up, 00 success signal 3) and on evidence continuity (the two-click dispute test).
- **Enterprise onboarding suites (bank-side)** — Fenergo-class institution onboarding platforms ("Digital Customer Onboarding in Banking: The Roadmap" — https://www.fenergo.com/blogs/customer-onboarding-expectations-vs-reality): institutional/client onboarding with KYC/AML orchestration. They serve the bank's middle office, price at enterprise levels, and treat the applicant-facing flow as one channel among several. Our product is applicant-first, fintech-scale, and deliberately decisioning-free — complementary layers, not substitutes; their abandonment figure ("one in five client onboarding applications are abandoned due to KYC and AML challenges") is demand evidence both ways.
- **Form platforms with approval workflows** — Jotform-class form builders with approvals (form + review + decision in one tool, at a fraction of IDV pricing): they prove the flow+console shape buyers want, but their capture step is a file input, their "review" is an approve/reject toggle without evidence dossiers, and identity verification is a plugin they don't own. The wedge between them and the IDV platforms — flow craft WITHOUT the engine — is exactly this product's declared position (capability-abstract, 08).
- **The national e-KYC utility layer (UAE)** — the CBUAE nationwide e-KYC platform (created by Federal Decree-Law No. 30 of 2024; FacePhi analysis: https://facephi.com/observatory/en/uae-e-kyc-platform-aml-compliance, Oct 8, 2026; GTReview launch coverage: https://www.gtreview.com/news/mena/uae-central-bank-announces-nationwide-kyc-platform-for-banks-and-fintechs, Apr 15, 2026): standardized KYC data exchange across banks and fintechs. It reduces document burden (reusable identity data) — and it is infrastructure, not a flow: every fintech still needs the applicant experience and the review accountability layer on top of whatever utilities they consume. The product treats such utilities as one more abstract verification capability binding (08 N1), never a threat model.
- **Branch/paper onboarding** (the regional fallback): walk into a branch or agent location with your document. It converts (a human guides capture, exactly our Capture Coach's logic, with a person instead of a frame) at the cost of physical distribution and zero async resumability. The product's guided-capture thesis is that the branch's guidance quality — patient, corrective, per-item feedback — is what must be digitized; the checklist-degrading assist (07c) is that thesis made honest.

- Digital wallet pull: Middle East and Africa digital wallet market "USD 8.24 billion in 2026" with Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar holding significant share (Credence Research, https://www.credenceresearch.com/report/middle-east-and-africa-digital-wallet-market).
- Regulatory formalization: CBUAE identity-proofing guidance — "Identity proofing comprises three actions: (1) collection and resolution, (2) validation, and (3) verification" (https://rulebook.centralbank.ae/en/rulebook/guidance-licensed-financial-institutions-digital-identification-customer-due-diligence) — the exact three-action split this product's step model mirrors (collect → capture quality → submit for verification, 07a).

### 4.2 The abandonment economics (why a flow product, not more automation)

- The band: 40–70% (Lorikeet), 60–80% (Jumio), 40–60% poorly-designed digital KYC + "70% of users abandon KYC flows that take longer than three" minutes (Perspective, https://getperspective.ai/blog/fintech-customer-experience-2026-onboarding-trust-drop-off), "above 60% when KYC is slow" (Didit), 68% / 63% / 38%-historic trajectory (Signicat via Incode and The Skins Factory, §1).
- The step that kills: "Document upload typically shows the highest drop-off (30-40%)" (Lorikeet); "38% of consumers abandoned a financial application because they didn't have the required identity document" (Signicat 2022 via Sortment, https://sortment.com/blogs/fintech-kyc-onboarding-playbook); "Document burden. The user does not have the required document to hand, or does not want to photograph and upload it. · Biometric discomfort." (OneID).
- The time budgets: "the average amount of time after which consumers abandon applications is 14 minutes and 20 seconds" (Signicat, https://www.signicat.com/blog/best-practices-for-customer-onboarding); "KYC processes taking over five minutes see a 40% average drop in completion" (ConvertCart, https://www.convertcart.com/blog/conversion-rate-optimization-statistics).
- The review-side cost: "Manual review is the largest variable cost driver, with each case typically running $25 to $50 in analyst time" (Oscilar, vendor claim, https://oscilar.com/blog/kyc-compliance) — every application that reaches review with a blurry document burns the most expensive minutes in the funnel; the deterministic capture gates exist precisely to keep junk out of the human queue (07c §2.1).
- Capture-quality causality: "A blurry ID is not just a UX problem. It creates blind spots for fraud. Document verification starts before OCR reads a name or date of…" (NV Global, https://www.linkedin.com/posts/nvglobal-ai_kyc-documentverification-fraudprevention-activity-7506270245846712321-7AGw); the public failure taxonomy: "rotation, perspective distortion, glare, blur, poor lighting, and curved documents" (OCR Studio, https://ocrstudio.ai/blog/why-capture-conditions-matter-the-ux-and-ui-impact-of-id-scanning); "Focus, resolution, glare, motion blur, framing" (Microblink, https://microblink.com/resources/glossary/document-capture).

### 4.3 The resumability seam is unoccupied

- Best practice is named, product is absent: "Save & Resume: Enable users to save their progress and return later, particularly for longer forms" (WeWeb, https://www.weweb.io/blog/multi-step-form-design); "Automatically save progress at regular intervals and on step changes, with a reliable resume mechanism across sessions and devices. Provide a clear save status" (WebEyez, https://webeyez.com/insights/guides/multi-step-form-ui-ux-design-guide); "Saved forms not only prevent data loss via timeout but allow users to return to resume their form at a time that suits them an hour or months" later (Smart Communications, https://docs.smartcommunications.com/articles/smartiq/user-sessions-and-form-timeout/a/keepalive); the timeout taxonomy — "idle, absolute, rolling" (Descope, https://www.descope.com/learn/post/session-timeout-best-practices).
- The nudge lifecycle is folk practice: "day-one reminder, day-three nudge, day-seven winback" (Sortment); "If a user drops off during KYC or ID upload, trigger a reassurance message" (CleverTap, https://clevertap.com/blog/improve-conversion-rate-fintech-apps). No retrieved tool couples the nudge to a durable application entity with a resume token, a declared window, recall marking (P11), and cross-device continuation — the observed absence this product occupies.

### 4.4 The flow-craft precedents

- "The practical implementation is one question per screen. Each step in the flow should present a single decision or input requirement" (digia, https://www.digia.tech/post/progressive-disclosure-mobile-ux); "Progressive disclosure defers advanced or rarely used features to a secondary screen, making applications easier to learn and less error-prone" (NN/g, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure).
- "The strongest onboarding patterns let users see a version of the product before all verification steps are complete" (The Skins Factory, https://www.theskinsfactory.com/uiux-design-blog/fintech-onboarding-ux-design) — the preview-before-verification principle; "risk-based flows" named as a drop-off reducer (Eleken, https://www.eleken.co/blog-posts/fintech-onboarding-simplification).
- Real-time capture gating: "During capture, the system evaluates framing, glare, blur, and edge alignment in real time. When requirements are met, the ID is auto-captured" (Incode developer design guide, https://developer.incode.com/design-and-ux/id-capture-design); the degradable checklist floor: Persona's light/glare/framing/focus/surface/device checklist (§2, Persona row).

### 4.5 Arabic-language demand and national ID conventions (market context)

- Document conventions: "Emirates ID (primary KYC document) • Proof of address (tenancy contract / utility bill) • Salary certificate or employment proof" (LinkedIn practitioner list); "For expats and foreign professionals, a valid residency visa copy and an original Emirates ID card may also be requested" (Identify Middle East, https://identifymiddleeast.com/kyc-documents). In KSA the equivalent primary documents are the national ID and the Iqama residence permit for expatriates (KYC-chain regulatory series, https://kyc-chain.com/regulation-focus-series-article-8-the-uae-and-the-financial-services-regulatory-authority; paycompliance.com). The product encodes NONE of these as rules — document types are workspace-configured data (07a DocumentType), and these citations justify only the bilingual-ar direction proposal and the seeded fixture realism (07c).
- Bilingual appetite: the review-console category's English-first posture (all §2 vendors) against an applicant population that is Arabic-dominant — the persona split (02) and the bilingual-ar direction proposal (LOOP-6) carry this; no retrieved vendor ships an Arabic-first applicant flow with RTL-correct capture guidance.

## 5. Positioning statement

**For MENA fintech onboarding teams** who watch motivated applicants die at the document step and reviewers drown in a ticketing board, **kyc-onboarding-flow** is the applicant flow + review console that treats interruption, guidance, and accountability as the product — resumable multi-session applications with recall-marked answers and coached deterministic capture gates on the applicant side; an evidence-dossier review console with reason-coded decisions and an immutable audit chain on the other. **Unlike** full-stack IDV platforms (Sumsub, Onfido, Veriff, Jumio, Persona — engine-bundled, per-check-priced, flow-as-widget), **we** are capability-abstract: the buyer's existing or future verification engine plugs in as an abstract capability (08 N1) and its assessments become review inputs, while the flow, the resumability, and the console remain ours — bilingual-ar, RTL-correct, WCAG 2.2 AA with the fintech overlay as contract rules, and honest about every degradation. The verification market commoditizes at $0.80–$4.00 per check (§6); the flow that keeps applicants IN the funnel is where conversion is won.
