## A. Curated references (what we take, and what we deliberately do not)

1. **Incode developer docs — "ID Capture" design guide** (the strongest published capture-interaction precedent) — https://developer.incode.com/design-and-ux/id-capture-design (retrieved 2026-10-17, q3; page states: "During capture, the system evaluates framing, glare, blur, and edge alignment in real time. When requirements are met, the ID is auto-captured.")
   Take: the real-time gate-evaluation loop as the capture step's core mechanic — their published design guidance validates evaluating framing/glare/blur/edge-alignment DURING capture (not after upload), which is exactly the loop our five deterministic gates formalize with published thresholds (07c §2.1); the frame-guide overlay concept (a document-shaped guide in the viewport) with a readiness state. Not taken: **auto-capture as the only commit path** — their pattern captures automatically "when requirements are met", which hands the shutter to the machine; our contract is the inverse (07b rule 3: the evaluation loop NEVER blocks capture — gates advise, submit enforces), because a 21:40 applicant with a glare-heavy lamp must always be able to take the shot she judges best, and a screen-reader user must never wait on a silent auto-trigger; the SDK-embedded posture (guidance lives inside a vendor SDK — our guidance is product surface with contract copy); their livery and chrome (contributing zero color per LOOP-11).

2. **Persona help center — "How to take a photo the scan can read"** (the degradable checklist floor) — https://help.withpersona.com/articles/RP2dHiXcsiW5c341xGVKPS (retrieved 2026-10-17, q3; page states: "A full checklist for capturing an ID during verification, covering light, glare, framing, focus, surface, and the device itself.")
   Take: the six-factor checklist as the shipped FLOOR of capture guidance — light, glare, framing, focus, surface, and the device itself is the taxonomy our static bilingual checklist instantiates (11 §D2's per-gate guidance strings), and their publishing it as a HELP article proves the industry's floor is exactly checklist-grade; our contribution is promoting it from help-center folklore to first-class surface state (the checklist renders live in the viewport, and the assist capability degrades TO it, never below it — 08 N2). Not taken: the checklist's location in their product (help center, one tap away from the failure) — ours renders where the failure happens, with the failing gate named; their SDK/engine-bound posture (the checklist sells their verification — ours is capability-abstract, provenance-labeled "إرشاد وفق القواعد" / "rule-based guidance"); English-first guidance strings (ours are authored bilingual at فصحى وسطى with RTL-correct layout).

3. **Socure — "How Socure Designs Accessible Identity Verification"** (the a11y frontier of the category) — https://www.socure.com/blog/accessible-identity-verification (retrieved 2026-10-17, q7; page states: "Learn how Socure designs Predictive DocV to meet WCAG 2.2 accessibility standards, so identity verification works for everyone.")

4. **The Skins Factory — "Fintech Onboarding UX: Why 68% of Users Quit Before…"** (the flow-craft design-agency reference) — https://www.theskinsfactory.com/uiux-design-blog/fintech-onboarding-ux-design (retrieved 2026-10-17, q12; page states: "The strongest onboarding patterns let users see a version of the product before all verification steps are complete.")
   Take: the **preview-before-verification principle** — letting applicants see meaningful state before every check completes; our expression is the resumability system itself (the Progress Spine shows the whole application's state from the first screen — completion is visible before completion exists) plus the trust symmetry at review-and-submit (the applicant sees EXACTLY what the reviewer will see — CP-4.1), which extends their principle from "see the product" to "see your own file as evidence"; their trust-design framing for verification steps that "feel" like the wall where abandonment concentrates. **Motion choreography (stately; event-driven ONLY — 10's no-refold rule).** When a step completes (submitStep confirmed), its card executes **the fold**: a 320ms emphasized-decelerate translation into the spine slot with a depth settle — the card's layered shadow collapses into the fold-mark chip (the shadow's occlusion becomes the mark) — leaving the navy fold-mark as the step's permanent completed state; the next step card settles in beneath (280ms fade, never spin-in — 06 StepCard). On resume (resumeApplication), the completed folds **unfold in a 5-element cascade** (≤60ms stagger, capped at 5 per the stately rule — a longer application renders the first 5 simultaneously with the rest static): the application visibly remembers where it was (05's "the fold state IS the resume state"). The submission fold settles the ENTIRE spine at once — every fold-mark confirms in a single weighted beat (the one full-ceremony moment, 00's trust beat). After that, the spine is inert: folds fire on step-completion events, NEVER on re-renders — a resumed application does not replay its history (10); a glance at the spine tells both the applicant and the reviewer how much of the document is done.

**A11y implications.** The spine is a navigation region with an ordered-list semantic; step ordinals announce on focus ("الخطوة 3 من 5 — المستندات" / "step 3 of 5 — documents" — 06); fold states are text+icon (fold-mark chip + "مكتملة" / "complete" described state), never hue alone; fold-in announces politely once ("اكتملت الخطوة — {title}" — 11 §D1); the resume cascade announces its SUMMARY once, not per-fold ("استؤنف الطلب — خطوتان متبقيتان" / "Application resumed — 2 steps remaining" — 11 §D1); backward navigation is free pre-submission (04) with the spine's roving tabindex moving by ↑/↓ with stable physical mapping in both directions (09); 400% reflow keeps the rail's reading order intact with identical labels. **Reduced motion:** all steps render at final state simultaneously; the fold collapses to the chip + announcement; the cascade is skipped and its summary announced; the submission settle becomes a single simultaneous state change (06 ProgressSpine contract; motion-language §5).

**Mood anchor.** A document folded along its creases and picked up exactly where it was left — the application as a physical object that remembers.
