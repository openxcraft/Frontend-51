## Original-direction sketch (prose — primary screen S3, outline editor)

The outline editor is a reading desk laid over a story's skeleton. The inline-start rail lists chapters as slim paper strips, each carrying a rollup sync seal — two-tone, reading "٦ من ٦" when a chapter is aligned, amber-edged when it is not. The main field is the step board: cards stacked in reading order like galleys on a paste-up desk, each card a miniature of its layout — a faint media block on the inline-start edge, ruled text lines beside it — so the eye reads the story's rhythm before reading a word. Selecting a card raises it one layer (layered-depth, never a bounce) and opens the inspector on the inline-end: the layout picker at top rendered in the story's direction, with a small toggle that flips the whole preview between right-to-left and left-to-right — the product's thesis in a single control — then media, then two locale tabs labeled العربية and English, each editing in its own direction. A thin crimson thread runs down the board's inline edge: the chapter progress spine, doubling as the reorder affordance. Below sits the sync ribbon: one row per step, two cells — Arabic and English — colored by derived state. Here the product stops looking like a builder and becomes what it is: a bilingual editorial bench where two editions of one story are kept honest, step by step.

## Mood anchors

A reading room at dusk, paper stacked in layers · crimson margin ink, never alarm red · the direction toggle as the desk's single satisfying switch · sync seals closing chapter by chapter · calm as a professional courtesy.
