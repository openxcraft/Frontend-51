## Original direction (prose sketch — identity-driven, T2-appropriate)

**The Masruf Ledger.** A claims pipeline drawn in one working vermilion on warm paper — soft-lift surfaces, no glass, no gradients, the duotone glyphs carrying the pipeline's three verbs: capture, compute, approve. The claims home is a ledger column: one row per claim, the total in tabular figures set large in vermilion at display size, the chain-position chip ("with Salem — level 1 of 2") doing the reassurance work that a status dot never could. The composer is where the product's soul sits: the item rail down the middle, and pinned to the inline-end edge a totals rail drawn like a receipt's closing lines — kind splits, subtotal, grand total — where every number arrives with zero layout shift because the space was reserved before the money was. Under every per-diem and mileage item, the **computation breakdown** renders as a small ruled arithmetic: "3 full days × SAR 525.00 / 1 day × 75% — SAR 1,968.75", the rate-table name and effective date set like a footnote citation, clickable through to the table itself — a claimant and an approver arguing over the *same* numbers is the entire product. The receipt panel is bilingual by construction: an Arabic tax invoice renders right-to-left inside the English chrome, its draft fields arriving one settle at a time in plum (the machine-proposes color — never a decision color), each with its confidence stated in words and its region of the paper highlighted when focused; the manual form is the same five fields in the same order, so the capability's absence is invisible to muscle memory. The approval workspace stacks its evidence honestly — items, then the findings rail (blocking rows on the vermilion tint, each stating measured-vs-required like a customs form), then the decision bar — and the gate refusal reads like a ledger entry, not a scolding: "Not yet. Item 4 has no receipt above the SAR 75.00 line." And once, at the end of the line, the product allows itself its single flourish: **the Reimbursement Stamp** — finance records the payout, the claim card lifts once, and a round seal stamps in with a controlled ≤8% overshoot, the amount and method announced aloud for anyone who cannot see the ink — the manual record made to feel as real as the money it evidences. Everything else is stillness at 150–250ms; nothing bounces but the stamp.

## Mood anchors

A vermilion working ink on warm paper · arithmetic you can cite like a footnote · a bilingual receipt at its own direction inside a foreign chrome · a customs manifest for a gate · one round stamp when the money moves — then the next claim.
