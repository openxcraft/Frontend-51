# 00 — Brief: expense-claim-flow

Family: business_ops · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A claim pipeline for finance teams and their employees: an expense claim moves from capture to reimbursement evidence in one governed line. An employee builds a **claim** out of **items** of three kinds — **receipt** items (a scanned or pasted receipt with an amount), **per-diem** items (a destination tier and travel days, computed by rule), and **mileage** items (a distance, computed by rule) — and submits it into an **approval chain** routed by the workspace's threshold matrix. Finance reviewers see every item with its deterministic **policy findings** (missing receipt above the policy threshold, duplicate receipts, late submission, per-diem double-dip), decide (approve / reject with reasons / query back), and when the chain completes they record the **payout** — the manual disbursement evidence (method, reference, date, actor) that closes the claim. Every consequential action lands in the activity log, and the exportable claim package (claim ledger + findings + approval trail + payout record) is the audit artifact. No corporate cards, no ERP posting, no travel booking (LOOP-10): the pipeline starts at "an employee spent money" and ends at "finance recorded that it was reimbursed" — the money moves in whatever payroll or banking process the company already runs.

The unique feature is **localized per-diem/mileage rules with bilingual receipt OCR assist**, and it is built as two inspectable mechanisms, not oracles. First, the rules: **rate tables** are workspace-owned, versioned, effective-dated tables of per-diem destination tiers and mileage rates per vehicle class, in the workspace currency, with a source note on every table ("captured from the 2026 travel policy memo, verify against current policy") — because in the GCC, per-diem and mileage rates are **company policy, not statute** (research q9/q13: no government per-diem or mileage table surfaced for KSA/UAE; Alaan's own policy example is AED 400/day UAE-domestic, unverified estimate), while the global convention the category leans on — GSA/State destination-tiered per diems with 75% first/last-day proration, IRS mileage rates that changed mid-year 2026 (72.5¢ → 76¢/mi from July 1) — is exactly the *shape* of dated, tiered rates that a Gulf policy desk needs to encode and trust (q4/q5). Every computed item shows its **computation breakdown**: the tier's daily amount, the days, the proration applied, the rate citation, the arithmetic — so a claimant and an approver can argue about the same numbers. Second, the OCR assist: **conditionally-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1) — receipt capture offers an extraction draft (vendor, date, amount, tax, receipt number) with per-field confidence bands in either Arabic or English; **every field is confirmed by a human before it writes to the claim** (P15 trust checkpoint), and when the capability is unbound, rate-limited, or fails, the same form stands ready for manual entry — the manual path is not a fallback bolted on, it is the same surface (08 degradation map).

The gap this occupies is specific (01): the category's center of gravity is card-first spend platforms (Ramp, Brex, Alaan, Pemo — corporate cards with expenses riding them), focused expense plans priced per active member (Expensify), and AP-first suites (Airbase) — all Latin-first, all presuming the card or the ERP integration as the system of record. The regional reality the roster targets — companies that reimburse out of pocket, hold policy in bilingual memos, collect VAT-bearing Arabic receipts (ZATCA regulations require tax invoice details in Arabic alongside any other language, q7), and route approvals through a delegation-of-authority matrix (q15) — has no claims-pipeline-first product serving it. The boundary against the procurement side (company purchase requests vs employee expense claims) and the money-document side (reimbursement claims vs sales invoices) is recorded in 12-open-questions (LOOP-3, both flagged unilaterally — reverse mirrors pending orchestrator).

## Buyer

Finance teams (2–6 finance seats) at MENA businesses with 50–500 employees who reimburse out-of-pocket expenses: trading and logistics companies, professional-services firms, clinics and school groups, retail head offices. The economic buyer is the finance manager who owns the expense policy and answers when reimbursement is late, a duplicate slips through, or an auditor asks for the evidence; daily users split into three shapes — the frequent claimant (regional sales and field staff, Riyadh↔Dubai weeks, receipts in two languages), the threshold-routed approver (department heads who see claims only above their matrix line), and the payout processor (the accountant who records what actually moved). Pricing recommendation: finance seats carry the price, employee submission seats are free, OCR extractions are metered within a generous window (12 Q-3) — because the category's per-active-member anchors (Expensify $5–9; Rydoo $9/user/mo annual, minimum 5 users — unverified estimates, q2/q3) price exactly the seats a claims pipeline should not tax.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px for the finance queue and policy studio), with a first-class **mobile capture mode** (≤767px) for claimants: photograph or paste a receipt, review the extraction draft, add the item to the open claim — one-handed, in a taxi. Zones: claims home (my-claims vs finance/approval queues by role, filters + saved views); claim composer (header, item rail, live totals with computation breakdowns); receipt review panel (OCR draft fields with confidence + the manual-entry form as the same surface); approval workspace (item table, findings rail, decision bar, approval-chain steps); policy studio (rate tables with effective dating + policy packs with the approval matrix); payouts & export; jobs center; settings. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No native app at v1 (mobile capture is web); no public API at v1; no email-inbox ingestion at v1 (abstract capability, 08 N1).

## Unique feature

Localized per-diem/mileage rules with bilingual receipt OCR assist — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **rules as inspectable, dated, versioned tables** — the GSA/State per-diem convention (destination tiers, lodging/M&IE split, monthly publication) and the IRS mid-year mileage change prove that rates are *dated data*, not constants; the product encodes them as workspace tables with effectiveFrom dates and version pins per claim, so a March claim computed under the 2026-01 table and a July claim under the 2026-07 table both show which table they used; (2) **computation you can read** — every per-diem and mileage item carries its breakdown (days × rate, proration percentages, rate citation), the same inspectability that made labor-rule guardrails trustworthy in this family's planner (attendance-shift-planner's named-violation grammar) applied to money; (3) **bilingual receipt reality** — Saudi tax invoices must carry Arabic (ZATCA), Arabic receipts present RTL text, diverse numeral systems and layout variations that challenge extraction (q11, arXiv 2406.04493), so the OCR assist is confidence-banded per field, never auto-applied, and the form renders either script with bidi isolation — the claimant confirms, the machine proposes; (4) **the approval chain as first-class data** — threshold-routed multi-level steps (the DOA-matrix convention, q15) with delegation, reasons, and a gate that refuses to approve while blocking findings stand unresolved — the governance posture enterprise suites sell, here as the pipeline's default grammar.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No corporate cards — no card issuance, no card-feed import, no spend controls on cards. The product starts from out-of-pocket receipts, per-diem and mileage claims; card-first platforms are the counter-position (01).
- No ERP/accounting posting — approvals end at the payout *record* (manual evidence); no ledger writes, no journal entries, no accounting-suite connectors. The export artifact is the handoff to whatever posts the money (08 N2).
- No travel booking — no flights, hotels, or itinerary search; trips appear only as the date window a per-diem item references.
- No payroll processing — payout records evidence; wage calculations belong to payroll systems.
- No automatic policy enforcement beyond declared rules — findings are computed by the deterministic engine (07c); overrides are human, reasoned, and logged.
- No AI-written policy, no AI approval decisions, no auto-applied OCR — the single AI-touching operation is the receipt-extraction draft, confirm-gated per field (conditionally-AI declaration, 07a/07b).
- No receipt-fraud forensics (AI-generated-receipt detection claims circulate unverified, q10 — recorded as an honest non-claim in 01); duplicate detection is the deterministic rule the evidence supports.

## Success signals (definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

A finance team can: author a rate table with two destination tiers and a mileage rate with an effective date; set a policy pack's approval matrix and receipt-required threshold; as an employee, create a claim, add a mileage item and watch the computation breakdown cite the rate and dates, add a per-diem item with proration and watch the arithmetic, attach a receipt and run the OCR assist and confirm its draft field-by-field (or type the fields by hand with the assist off); submit and watch the chain route to the right approver level; as the approver, hit the approval gate refused with a named blocking finding (missing receipt), resolve it by attaching the receipt, and approve through the final level; as finance, record the payout with method and reference; export the claim package and retrieve the artifact before expiry — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full flow completing with the OCR capability unbound (manual entry exercised end to end).
