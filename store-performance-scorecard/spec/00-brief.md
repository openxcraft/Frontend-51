## What it is

A **store scorecard** workspace for multi-store retail chains: one chain, its stores, its shift-level operating reality, and a like-for-like comparison engine that answers the question every retail-ops lead actually asks — *which of my stores is genuinely over- or under-performing, compared fairly?* The chain registers its stores (bilingual names, format, region, trading start), declares each store's **operating patterns** (versioned shift windows — morning/afternoon/evening/night classes, Ramadan patterns with their own effective dates), and imports shift-level sales and labor records (paste or file, always; an abstract sales-data channel when the workspace binds one). From that state the product computes deterministic **scorecard runs**: each store's KPIs for a comparison window, its like-for-like delta against the same matched weeks of the prior year, its percentile position inside its peer group, and a weighted composite score — every formula, weight, and eligibility rule published on an in-product definitions surface and pinned to the run that used it.

The product's spine is the discipline the retail finance world has run for decades and no retrieved tool productizes at store level: **like-for-like comparison** (research S1, S7). Comps exclude what is not comparable — stores too new to have a prior-year counterpart, periods a store did not trade, closed locations — and the exclusion itself is a declared, versioned **comp policy** with a method toggle backed by the published comp-calculation conventions (store-level eligibility vs store-period-level eligibility; S1's Method 1/Method 2). The second spine is the one no retrieved competitor ships at all: the comparison is **shift-aware**. A mall store trading three shifts and a community store trading one long one cannot be compared on daily totals; the product buckets every record into its shift class, normalizes KPIs per class (sales per open hour, sales per labor hour, transactions per open hour), and compares stores on **matched shift classes only** — a store with no night shift is never ranked on night-shift KPIs. The practitioner instinct is documented at the vendor-research level (S8: staffing aligned to real demand; traffic-normalized metrics that "reveal what raw sales figures hide"); the shift-class grammar itself is this product's own.

The MENA honesty layer is the third spine. A plain week-matched year-on-year window quietly misleads in this region, because lunar seasons drift ~11 days earlier each Gregorian year: this year's week 8–14 window sits wholly inside Ramadan while last year's sat mostly outside it. Every scorecard run therefore carries a **season-composition disclosure** — what share of each comparison window each named season window occupied — and flags **season misalignment** when the two windows' compositions differ beyond a declared threshold, offering a **season-anchored basis** (this named season vs the same named season last year) as the honest alternative. The grammar is recorded as kinship with the sales-cohort-view comparison posture (12, LOOP-3); the data is entirely this product's own. The AI stance is **declared non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1): every number on screen is deterministic arithmetic over imported records — no generation, no scoring intelligence, no narration — and the spec leaks zero AI affordances. The product deliberately ends at analysis and its handoff (LOOP-10): it does not schedule (business_ops owns planning), it touches no POS hardware, and it joins no loyalty data — those exclusions are fenced in 12 with the boundary recorded on both sides where siblings have authored.

## Buyer

Retail chains in MENA running 5–100+ stores — grocery and fresh markets, pharmacy chains, F&B groups, specialty and fashion retailers — plus the operations consultancies serving them. The economic buyer is the **retail-operations or category lead** who answers for store performance in the monthly review and controls the store-standards budget. The daily user is the **store-performance analyst** who today rebuilds the same comparison in a spreadsheet every week: POS export, pivot table by store, a YoY column, a mental adjustment for "the new store" and "the Ramadan weeks," and an evening lost to VLOOKUP drift (02). Secondary seats: the regional manager who reads the ranked board and drills their region, and the BI/data owner who is tired of ad-hoc scorecard requests and wants a governed definitions surface. The chain class is regionally real at scale (research S4: a Dubai-headquartered group operating ~2,400 locations) and the vendor-research record names the exact job — identifying underperformers and benchmarking best practices "across dozens or hundreds of locations simultaneously" (S8).

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first analysis workspace (≥1280px full experience; the ranked scorecard table and shift lens are dense, keyboard-first surfaces; 768–1279px read-and-drill with collapsed rail; <768px read-only: saved scorecards, store cards, definitions). Zones: chain overview; stores directory with peer groups; store detail (its scorecard card, shift breakdown, annotations, coverage); the scorecard workspace (window + basis + peer group + ranked store table); the shift lens (the signature cross-store, per-shift-class comparison grid); data and imports (shift records, channel binding, coverage); definitions and policy (KPI formulas, comp policy, weights, season windows); jobs and activity console; settings. ⌘K palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three) reaches stores, scorecard runs, season windows, jobs, and actions with bilingual normalized search (rtl-rules §4). No mobile app; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

**Shift-aware like-for-like store comparison.** Defensible on three grounds (01 evidence): (1) the LFL/comp discipline is a published, named convention at chain level (S1, S7) — but the retrieved tooling either bundles flat period-over-period reporting inside a POS suite (Lightspeed/Square/Shopify/LS Retail/KORONA class) or sells traffic-sensor analytics as a platform (RetailNext class); none exposes comp-method policy, week-matched YoY at store level, and eligibility transparency as a first-class, inspectable construct; (2) shift-level normalization (per open hour, per labor hour, matched shift classes) appears in the vendor record only as staffing-alignment narrative (S8) — no retrieved product makes the shift the unit of store comparison; (3) the season-composition disclosure answers the MENA comparison-lie (lunar drift smearing week-matched windows) deterministically — no modeling, no deseasonalization, just disclosed composition and a season-anchored alternative basis.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10, from the roster row)

- **No scheduling** — no shift planning, no roster authoring, no labor-rule guardrails. Planning is business_ops territory (attendance-shift-planner); this product consumes shift *definitions* as declared operating-pattern data and shift *records* as imported analysis inputs. Boundary mirrored with attendance-shift-planner (12 Q-5) and shift-swap-marketplace (12 Q-6).
- **No POS hardware** — no terminals, printers, scanners, traffic-counter sensors, or any device. Sales and traffic data arrive as records through paste/file import or an abstract, kind-not-vendor sales-data channel.
- **No loyalty data joins** — no member data, no stamp or reward joins, no customer-level metrics. The roster row fences it; the boundary is mirrored with loyalty-stamp-card (12 Q-7).
- No demand forecasting or statistical seasonal adjustment (deseasonalization is modeling — refused; composition disclosure is the honest deterministic alternative).
- No staff-level productivity scoring or individual performance management — the unit of analysis is the store (and its shifts), never a named employee.
- No pricing/markdown analytics, no basket-mix analytics, no e-commerce channel analytics — store-space performance only.

## Success signals (spec-level definition of done)

Against the Mock Adapter, with zero provider knowledge anywhere in the UI:

1. **A first scorecard in the first session**: the seeded chain opens with 12 stores, 104 weeks of shift records, and operating patterns already governed; the analyst selects last 4 weeks, runs the scorecard, and reads the ranked table — scores, like-for-like deltas, comp badges, coverage marks — with every formula one click away on the definitions surface.
2. **The shift-lens discovery**: RYD-07 reads healthy on totals (+3.1% YoY) but its evening-shift sales-per-labor-hour sits in the bottom decile of the peer group — the analyst opens the shift lens, sees the matched-class grid, drills to the raw evening rows, and pins a context annotation — without ever leaving the keyboard-first surface (09).
3. **The honesty tests**: the new store (RYD-12) is excluded from like-for-like with its reason shown; the renovated store's incomplete weeks are marked, not silently averaged; the Ramadan window's composition flag fires (100% vs 38%) and the season-anchored basis re-ranks honestly; a store with <60% coverage is kept out of ranking with the gap named.
4. **The degraded-path test**: with no sales-data channel bound and only pasted records, the entire flow — import, run, drill, export — completes identically (LOOP-5 bar, 08).
5. **Determinism test**: the same seeded world, run twice, produces byte-identical scorecard state (07c §6).
