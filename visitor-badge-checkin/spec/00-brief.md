# 00 — Brief: visitor-badge-checkin

Family: business_ops · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A bilingual (ar/en) front-desk check-in system for MENA offices. Two surfaces share one deterministic state machine: a **visitor-facing kiosk** (tablet at the lobby, one-thumb touch, large type, Arabic-first with an immediate language switch) and an **admin-facing desk** (desktop web for the office administrator who owns the lobby). The kiosk walks a visitor in — pre-registered guests type a six-character code (or scan the code from their invitation) and confirm; walk-ins fill a three-field form; returning visitors are recognized by a normalized name-and-phone match and confirm last-known details rather than retyping them. The desk is where the day is run: today's board of expected, arrived, and in-building visitors, the pre-registration composer, the visit record with its full timeline, the visitor log with retention-aware exports, and the badge studio. Every mutation is deterministic registry state — this is a **declared non-AI product** (ai-native-patterns.md §1): no operation generates, scores, or transforms content by model; check-in is a state machine, not a judgment.

The unique feature is the **badge spec**. Every badge the product issues is rendered from a versioned template the office admin designs once: which fields appear (visitor name in the visitor's own script, host name, company, date, validity window), the layout grammar for dual-script rendering (RTL-first field order with bidi-isolated Latin runs), the visitor-type color band (guest, vendor, contractor, interview candidate, VIP), and the check-out code that travels on the badge. The badge lifecycle is modeled as data, not as a print event: a badge is *ready* when check-in completes, *issued* when the desk or kiosk confirms delivery (printed or shown as an on-screen pass), and *voided* by check-out, end-of-day expiry, or correction — a misspelled name voids the old badge and issues a replacement with the correction chained to the original (the audit instinct the portfolio's custody products share, applied to lobby artifacts). Pre-registration and host notify complete the loop the roster names: an invitation carries the visit window, the purpose, the host, and a check-in code; at arrival the host is notified through the workspace's notification channel, and the notification is a recorded event on the visit timeline — who was told, when, through which channel — because "the host knows you're here" is a check-in system's core promise, not a side effect.

The buyer's reality (01 evidence): the incumbent is a paper logbook and a sheet of handwritten badge stickers, with the receptionist calling the host's mobile across the office. Category tools exist and are mature — per-location SaaS from $99 to $362 a month — but they are Latin-first, English-default, and shaped for global corporate real-estate portfolios; the regional alternatives are bilingual but enterprise/integrator-shaped, bundling access-control hardware the roster explicitly excludes (LOOP-10). The office administrator at a 60–400-person Gulf office — who greets visitors in Arabic, hosts them with coffee and dates (research digest sources 38–42), and answers when a visitor is still sitting in reception twenty minutes after arrival — is served by neither end honestly. This product is that administrator's instrument: bilingual by construction, kiosk-first, badge-specced, and priced per front desk rather than per enterprise.

## Buyer

Office administrators and front-desk leads at MENA businesses running 60–400 staff with a staffed or semi-staffed lobby: professional-services firms, clinics' administrative wings, schools' front offices, media and production houses, retail head offices, and growing tech companies. The economic buyer is the office administrator or office manager who owns the lobby experience and the visitor record; daily users are the reception team (desk surfaces) plus every host employee who pre-registers a guest or receives an arrival notification (thin host mode, deliberately free — the notification loop needs hosts, so hosts must never be the paywall). Visitors themselves are first-class but transient participants: they use the kiosk or the invitation link, never an account. One to three desk seats per workspace; unlimited hosts and unlimited visitors. Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Web application, two modes: **kiosk mode** (tablet-primary, 768–1280px touch surfaces, one-thumb targets ≥56px, idle welcome loop with language switch, keyboard-accessible for receptionist-assisted check-in) and **desk mode** (desktop-first ≥1280px, responsive to 360px for host-mode reads: my expected guests, arrival notifications). Zones: kiosk welcome → check-in (code / walk-in / returning) → badge-ready; desk today board, visits list, pre-registration composer, visit detail (timeline + badge panel), visitor log & exports, badge studio, settings. No native app at v1 (kiosk and visitor link are web); no public API at v1; no door/gate/turnstile integration of any kind (LOOP-10 — the badge is visual identification for people in the building, never a credential for hardware).

## Unique feature

Pre-registration with host notify and badge spec, as one deterministic loop: invitation (window, purpose, host, check-in code) → arrival (kiosk code/walk-in/returning match) → host notification (recorded event) → badge (spec-resolved, issued, voidable, correctable) → check-out → retention-aware log. Defensible on three grounds (01 evidence): (1) the badge spec itself — a versioned, dual-script-aware badge template studio — is absent from the category (competitors print fixed layouts with logo/name/type settings; nobody treats the badge as a designed artifact with RTL field grammar); (2) bilingual ar/en operation end to end with an Arabic-first kiosk default and normalized returning-visitor matching is not offered as a first-class construct by any per-location SaaS in the evidence, and the bilingual regional class is integrator-shaped rather than office-admin-shaped; (3) the whole loop is a declared non-AI deterministic state machine — inspectable transitions, recorded notifications, chained corrections — which is exactly the posture a lobby record needs and the opposite of the category's drift toward screening/AI add-ons we exclude.

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No access-control hardware: no door, gate, turnstile, elevator, or parking integration; badges are visual identification only and are never credentials (the badge QR is a check-out token, not a key).
- No watchlists: no denied-party screening, no ID verification against government databases, no sanctions or blocklist checks of any kind.
- No NDA signing: no legal document execution, e-signature, or document-review flows at check-in (versioned attestations with audit trails belong to policy-acknowledgment-portal; LOOP-3 boundary recorded in 12).
- No employee time-and-attendance (attendance-shift-planner's domain); the visitor log records visitors only.
- No meeting-room or desk booking, no calendar management, no invite delivery beyond the visitor's own check-in code (meetings have dates; the product never books rooms).
- No package/delivery handling, no food-ordering concierge features, no lobby digital-signage content management.
- No AI anywhere in the loop (declared non-AI): no face recognition, no smart-anything, no vibe-detection.

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

An office administrator can, in one first session on the Mock Adapter with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI: create the workspace's badge template in the studio (choose fields, set the guest type band, preview with a seeded bilingual sample) and watch the preview re-render per direction; pre-register a visitor with an Arabic name, a window, a purpose, and a host, and see the invitation record with its deterministic check-in code; on the kiosk surface, check that visitor in by code, watch the host-notification event land on the visit timeline, and see the badge resolve from the spec; check in a walk-in with a three-field form; watch the returning-visitor fixture match a normalized name variant; correct a misspelled badge and see the replacement chain; check both visitors out; open the visitor log, compute the in-building list, and export the day's log before artifact expiry — and the entire loop must complete with every abstract capability unbound: invitations issue with codes displayed for copy, host notifications fall back to the desk's visible arrival board (the manual alternative), badges render as on-screen passes with print-ready artifacts downloadable directly (LOOP-5 bar).
