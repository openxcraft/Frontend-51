## Category overview

Visitor management is a mature, well-priced category with a settled core loop: pre-registration invites, kiosk/tablet check-in, host notification, badge printing, and a digital visitor log (sources 4, 13, 16, 34, 35). The leaders are per-location SaaS aimed at global corporate workplaces — Latin-first, English-default, sold to workplace/real-estate teams. A second class serves the same loop on iPad-first simplicity for SMBs (sources 13, 15, 16). A third, regional class — Gulf security-integrator kiosk systems — is genuinely bilingual (Arabic/English, RTL layouts advertised; sources 18, 20) but is enterprise/integrator-shaped, bundling access control and access-control hardware, sold through lead-gen channels with no published self-serve pricing. The wedge this product occupies is the empty cell between them: a bilingual-ar, kiosk-first, badge-specced, office-admin-shaped system with no hardware, no screening, and no legal-document execution — priced per front desk like the SaaS class, spoken like the region.

## Indirect competitors (4)

- **GCC security-integrator kiosk VMS class** (Tektronix, entry2exit, Wipaq, ExpediteIoT, Shyphan — sources 17–21): genuinely bilingual Arabic/English with RTL layouts [18][20], but enterprise/integrator-shaped, hardware-bundled (access control — excluded from our scope), lead-gen pricing, no self-serve office-admin posture. The ceiling above us on regional fit; the floor below us on buying simplicity.
- **Paper logbook + handwritten badge stickers** (the true incumbent): free, flexible, works during power cuts — and cannot tell who is in the building, cannot notify a host without shouting, and produces no exportable record. Our visitor log with in-building list is this workflow, formalized.
- **The call-the-host norm** (receptionist phones the host's mobile): zero tooling, culturally universal; unrecorded, unreliable when the host is in a meeting, and exactly what the recorded host-notification event replaces with an audit trail.
- **Calendar invites as de-facto pre-registration** (team calendar holds the visit; lobby improvises): pre-registration without a check-in artifact — no code, no badge, no arrival state. We ingest the *intent* (the host composes the invitation here) without touching calendar systems (no scheduling, LOOP-10).

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- **Category spend is real and per-location**: official Envoy pricing $362/location/mo Premium with $2/user, $5/resource, $12/device fees [7]; Vendr market data $99–$149 Standard / $199–$299 Premium per location [8]; LobbyTrack $100/mo/location [10]; YAROOMS visitor module $99/location/mo [63]; Eptura/Proxyclick published tiers $630–$1,890/site/yr (Visitly, unverified estimate) [59]. Buyers already pay monthly per front desk.
- **The market is growing** (analyst estimates conflict — all unverified): base figures USD 1.5B (2022) [47] to USD 2.39B (2026) [48], CAGRs 9.05%–18.4% [44][47], projections USD 4.22B by 2031 [48] to USD 6.77B by 2034 [43]. The spread is honest evidence of category heat, not precision.
- **Unstaffed/semi-staffed front desks drive self-serve demand**: practitioner threads ask for systems that run when the front desk is not always staffed [5]; kiosk best practices emphasize visible placement and customized welcome screens [23]; tablet kiosks are the dominant form factor (smaller, cheaper) [22].
- **Touchless/QR pre-registration is established behavior**: pre-registered guests receive a unique QR code before arrival; walk-ins scan a lobby code [64][65][68]; multilingual branded invites with QR codes are a marketed feature [35].
- **Badge printing conventions are settled**: thermal paper/adhesive vs ID-card printer classes [31]; Avery 5395/8395 sticker sheets and Brother QL-class thermal printers are the de-facto small-office targets [29]; label costs $0.02–$0.33/badge in a K-12 context [30]; conventional fields = logo, company, type, name, photo [28]. Nobody markets a *badge spec studio* — templates are settings, not designed artifacts (the 00 wedge).
- **Bilingual GCC demand is visible in the integrator class**: "fully bilingual — Arabic and English" [18]; "full Arabic support with right-to-left layout" [20] — regional buyers are being served bilingual VMS today, through integrators rather than self-serve SaaS.
- **Visitor data is regulated**: name, photo, ID scan, company, phone, email, visit history are personal data under GDPR [53]; no fixed retention period — "no longer than necessary" [50][54]; Saudi PDPL requires processing records kept for the processing period plus five years (Securiti's reading [74]; Zasio notes the five-year figure from draft Executive Regulations [70]) and SDAIA breach notification within 72 hours [75]. Retention-aware logs are a compliance feature, not a nicety.

## Pricing landscape

Entry anchors $99–$149/location/mo (Vendr Standard band [8]; LobbyTrack $100 [10]; YAROOMS $99 [63]) · mid band $199–$299 (Vendr Premium [8]; Visitly ~$167 [11]; Eptura/Proxyclick tiers $630–$1,890/site/yr, unverified estimates [59][61]) · leader tier $362/location/mo official (Envoy [7]; instacheckin claims a 100 visitor-entries/month cap at this tier — third-party claim, unverified [9]). Our posture (12 Q-3): **per-front-desk (per-location) pricing** with a free floor for small offices (one location, capped monthly visits), unlimited hosts and visitors on every paid tier, and exports/logs unmetered — the lobby loop must never ration safety records; metering, if any, attaches to outbound notification volume on the abstract channel (08), which degrades to the in-app arrival board for free.

## Positioning statement

For MENA offices with a lobby to run, visitor-badge-checkin is the front-desk check-in system that greets in Arabic and English, tells the host the moment a guest arrives — as a recorded event, not a shout — and issues badges from a spec the office designed: dual-script layout, visitor-type bands, validity window, and a check-out code, with every void and correction chained. Unlike the global per-location platforms, it is bilingual by construction and badge-first by design; unlike the regional integrator systems, it ships without access-control hardware, without watchlists, and without an integration project — the office administrator sets it up in an afternoon, and the record it keeps is retention-aware and exportable.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns)

- SMB/front-desk sentiment (Q1/Q5 threads, paraphrased): front desks are not always staffed — buyers want self-serve check-in that still feels welcoming [5]; kiosk placement and welcome-screen customization are the make-or-break details [23].
- Buyer-side sentiment on leader pricing (Q2 comparisons, paraphrased): $362/location/mo plus per-user and per-device fees reads heavy for a single lobby; alternatives cluster at ~$100 [8][10][11].
- Integrator-class friction (Q4 pages, paraphrased): regional bilingual systems are sold as projects with contact-sales pricing — no self-serve path visible on any retrieved page [17][18][19][20][21].
- Privacy-operator sentiment (Q10, paraphrased): retention is the recurring open question — laws say "no longer than necessary" and operators want configurable windows rather than defaults they must reverse-engineer [50][54].

## Market size (honest)

MENA offices running 60–400 staff with a staffed or semi-staffed lobby — a wide but unmeasured slice of a global category whose base-year analyst figures disagree by more than 2x (USD 1.5B–2.39B; sources 44–48, all unverified). No MENA-specific TAM figure exists in any retrieved source; any number would be invented, so none is offered. Envelope: per-location SaaS at the category's entry-to-mid band ($99–$199/location/mo), with the free floor as the wedge into the paper-logbook incumbent.
