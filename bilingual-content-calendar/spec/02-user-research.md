# 02 — User Research: bilingual-content-calendar

SME-B pass · Verbatims are paraphrases of recurring public sentiment (marked), never invented quotes.

## Persona 1 — Dana, Social Media Lead (primary user, economic buyer)
**Role**: leads a 4-person bilingual social team at a Jeddah retail brand; plans ~120 posts/month across 5 channels in ar+en.
**Context**: lives in the calendar grid; her nightmare is a Ramadan grid rebuilt from scratch every year and variant drift between the ar and en versions of a campaign.
**JTBD**: "When I plan a seasonal campaign, I want the grid to already know the moments, the dual calendar, and which slots still miss an Arabic or English variant, so planning is arrangement, not archaeology."
**Triggers**: last Ramadan's grid lived in a spreadsheet with a Hijri column maintained by hand; an en-only post shipped during an Arabic-first moment; agency handoff required reformatting the plan three times.
**Objections**: "another per-user fee on a big team"; "we already publish through our management suite — will this duplicate?"; "Ramadan mode must match how *we* work, not a template".
**Onboarding motive**: see next Ramadan on the grid with moments pre-placed in the first session.
**Verbatim (paraphrase, social-lead sentiment in planner-tool discussions)**: "I don't need more software; I need the calendar to carry what's already in my head."

## Persona 2 — Farah, Bilingual Content Coordinator (daily user)
**Role**: maintains the grid, drafts briefs for writers, chases variant completion.
**JTBD**: "When a slot has English but no Arabic, I want that gap visible and assignable, so nothing ships one-language by accident."
**Triggers**: parity gaps discovered at publication time; writers asking "what's due for Eid?"; recurring copy-paste of moment briefs.
**Objections**: slot creation must be faster than her spreadsheet; variant linking must be automatic, not a manual relation field.
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "The plan is only real when both languages exist."

## Persona 3 — Yousef, Agency Account Director (multi-client)
**Role**: runs 6 client calendars at a Cairo agency; each client has different season priorities and approval cadence.
**JTBD**: "When I show a client their seasonal plan, I want it to look like the season they live — dual calendar, moments, both languages — so the meeting sells the thinking, not the tool."
**Triggers**: client meetings where the plan's cultural fluency is the differentiator; handoffs to client-side executors via messy exports.
**Objections**: workspace-per-client separation (same demand as Hussein in arabic-copy-studio); export formats must match client habits.
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "Clients buy cultural fluency; the calendar is where it shows first."

## Persona 4 — Reem, Regional Marketing Manager (reviewer)
**Role**: reviews plans before client/brand exposure; protects brand timing rules (no promo during certain observances, etc.).
**JTBD**: "When I review a season grid, I want timing conflicts and parity gaps surfaced before I approve, so my review adds judgment, not proofreading."
**Triggers**: a promo slot landed on a culturally sensitive date; a variant diverged from approved messaging.
**Objections**: review must be doable on mobile during her travel days; comments must anchor to slots, not float.
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "I approve plans, not surprises."

## Voice derivation (SME-J)
Planner voice: organized-calm — the tone of a well-run newsroom desk. Arabic UI copy فصحى بيضاء with fixed terminology (خانة=slot، حملة=campaign، لحظة=moment، نسخة مطابقة=variant). English: professional-neutral. Errors are timing-aware (never blame the planner for a Hijri method difference — explain it).
