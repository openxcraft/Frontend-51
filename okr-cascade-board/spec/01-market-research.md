## Category overview

Three currents collide here. **Dedicated OKR platforms** (Weekdone, Perdoo, Profit.co, Mooncamp, Tability class) productize the OKR ritual — cascade views, check-ins, grading — for a global, English-first market, priced per-user in a $3–$16 band with free tiers at the bottom (Q2, Q3). **Performance-suite OKR modules** (15Five, Lattice, WorkBoard class) carry OKRs as one module inside a performance-management platform whose center of gravity is reviews, feedback, and engagement (Q3) — a gravity this product explicitly refuses (LOOP-10: no performance reviews, no 360 feedback). And the **2025 retirement of a major enterprise OKR module** (Microsoft Viva Goals, retired 2025-12-31, publicly attributed to "limited adoption and usage", Q9) both validated the category's difficulty and left a named migration pool with no first-party successor (Q9 sources 41, 45). What no current sells: the **bilingual EN/AR objective authoring surface** (pairs as schema, not locale), a **leadership-visible cadence coverage surface** with derived at-risk zones, and deterministic, inspectable grading — packaged for the Gulf leadership team that today runs its quarter on a bilingual spreadsheet and a Sunday meeting (research digest sources 27, 28, 38–40).

## Market & demand evidence (with sources)

- **OKR failure is a rhythm problem, not a framework problem** — the convergent position of practice literature: failures root in behavior/rhythm (WorkBoard, source 4), partial application (Atlassian community, source 2), transparency and accountability gaps (OKR Institute, source 5); academically, Herkenrath (2023, cited by 7) studies failing OKR companies through the lens of "trust-based agreements between leaders and employees" (source 9). The check-in cadence is our product's answer to the documented failure mode.
- **The canonical cadence demand**: "OKRs don't require daily tracking. But regular check-ins preferably weekly – are essential to prevent slippage" (Doerr, quoted at source 23, p.117). The cadence is contested in practice (Profit.co argues bi-weekly to avoid "meeting fatigue and fake updates", source 24; Teamflect prescribes weekly team-wide plus monthly individual, source 21; Tability prescribes weekly "no exceptions" for weeks 2–12, source 25) — which is why cadence is a workspace setting with a recommended default in our product (12 Q-5), not a doctrine.
- **Vendor claim, marked**: "Teams that check in weekly complete 43% more OKRs than those reviewing monthly or ad hoc" and skipping the weekly rhythm makes teams "3x more likely" to miss (okrstool, source 26). Vendor-published, no methodology surfaced — quoted only as a marked claim, never restated as fact.
- **Writing guidance is established and citable**: objectives qualitative, key results 3–5 measurable outcomes (What Matters, source 29; the retired module's own writing guidance, source 31; Balanced Scorecard Institute, source 32); the "We will [Objective] as measured by [Key Results]" formula (source 34). The deterministic lint (07c) formalizes exactly this public guidance — no invention required.
- **Gulf business runs bilingually, side by side**: parallel Arabic/English texts are a documented regional convention "even when the target audience is local" (Nickerson 2013, academic, cited by 75, source 38); English functions as the region's business lingua franca (Hodges 2023, cited by 27, source 39); UAE institutional guidance addresses MSA register in bilingual professional communication (source 40). The bilingual pair schema matches the convention; tool-language skews English (bilingual-en direction proposal, LOOP-6).
- **The retirement signal**: the enterprise module retired 2025-12-31 "due to limited adoption and usage" (source 42); no replacement was named (sources 41, 45); practitioner sentiment (paraphrased) holds that an enterprise-grade OKR reporting capability is "yet to see available" elsewhere (source 46) — a named unmet expectation in the category.
- **Continuous-performance context validates the ritual, not the review**: the management literature documents the shift from annual reviews to continuous check-ins (sources 35–37) — supporting check-in cadence as a surface while the review artifact itself remains excluded (LOOP-10, and deliberately: the suites bundling OKRs with reviews are the counter-position above).

## Pricing landscape (unverified estimates unless official page named)

- Dominant model per-user: "Usually $3–$16/user/month" (Synergita roundup, source 20). Official anchors: Perdoo Premium $9 / Supreme $11 per user/mo (source 16, official); Weekdone OKR focus ~$8/user/mo (source 12); Mooncamp from €7.20/user/mo annual, free ≤5 users (source 15); 15Five Perform $11/user/mo annual-only contracts (source 19).
- The spread pain: for a 25-person team, "$49/month flat to over $6,000/month once contract minimums kick in" (Tability pricing study, source 17); flat-rate comparators around $129/mo all-in exist (source 18).
- **Our posture (12 Q-3)**: leadership/admin + team-lead seats priced (the people who author the cascade); KR-owner member seats included at zero (taxing the check-in would tax the cadence); drafting-assist runs metered within a generous window (P14); the plain bilingual editor and every deterministic workflow unlimited. Anchored at the category's per-seat mid band but charged on an order of magnitude fewer seats — the cadence needs owners, so owner seats must never be the paywall.

## Positioning statement

For bilingual leadership teams in MENA, okr-cascade-board is the OKR cascade board that keeps the quarter honest in two languages: objectives and key results are authored as EN/AR pairs (never machine-translated), the check-in cadence is a leadership-visible coverage surface with derived at-risk zones rather than a private dashboard, and grading is deterministic arithmetic that exports with its whole trail. Unlike dedicated OKR platforms, it is bilingual by schema and priced on the leadership seat instead of every participant; unlike performance suites, it refuses reviews and feedback on principle (LOOP-10); and unlike consultancies, the ritual stays in the building after the engagement ends.

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns)

- Dedicated-tool sentiment (Q2/Q3 comparison threads, paraphrased): per-user pricing punishes exactly the participation the method needs; check-ins decay into private dashboards nobody reads.
- Performance-suite sentiment (Q3 roundups, paraphrased): OKR modules arrive bundled with review machinery teams did not ask to switch on.
- Retirement-thread sentiment (Q9, paraphrased): enterprise buyers discovered their OKR data lived inside a module that could be switched off — portability and export were afterthoughts until they were the whole problem.

## Market size (honest)

Gulf leadership teams attempting quarterly OKR programs — a slice of the global OKR-platform category whose per-seat band ($3–$16) and flat-rate comparators ($49–$6,000/mo spread for a 25-person team) are documented above. No auditable MENA-specific OKR adoption figure exists; the regional evidence is consultancy marketing (sources 27–28) and the bilingual-convention literature (38–40) — directional, not quantified. Any MENA TAM number would be invented, so none is offered. Envelope: leadership-seat SaaS with metered assist — the same shape the category already pays, on fewer seats.
