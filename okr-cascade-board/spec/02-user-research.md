## Persona 1 — Layla, Strategy & PMO Lead (primary, admin seat)

**Role**: strategy/PMO lead at a GCC retail group (~400 employees, 4 divisions). She owns the quarterly planning ritual: drafts the company objectives with the CEO, walks division heads through alignment, and answers to the board when the quarter's headline numbers miss. Today the cascade lives in a bilingual spreadsheet she maintains herself — company goals in English, division commitments pasted in from four different documents, two of them in Arabic — and "who is on track" is a question she answers by walking the halls.
**Context**: bilingual by necessity — presents to the board in English, negotiates with division leads in Arabic; her spreadsheet has a hidden column where she hand-syncs the two goal texts.
**JTBD**: "When I run the quarterly cycle, I want one cascade the leadership team reads the same way in both languages — company objectives, team alignment, and an honest weekly signal of what is stalling — so the quarter stops being a spreadsheet I reconcile by hand and a set of hall conversations I have to conduct."
**Triggers**: a board question ("which of the five company objectives is behind?") she could not answer from the spreadsheet; a division lead whose Arabic reading of an objective differed from the English text on the slide; the moment she caught herself inventing a progress percentage in a steering meeting.
**Objections + responses**: "another dashboard nobody opens?" → the cadence dashboard is the leadership surface: coverage, overdue, at-risk — a 30-second read before the weekly meeting (00); "we tried OKRs and they died in month two" → that failure mode is the product's center: derived at-risk zones, the overdue trail, the named rhythm (01 sources 4, 5, 23); "my Arabic objectives read like translations" → the pair is authored, the assist drafts both sides from her focus brief, and the lint warns when one side reads soft (03 CP-2).
**Onboarding motive**: author one real company objective as a bilingual pair, link her first team objective, and watch the first real check-in land on the rollup — in the first session (P22).
**Verbatim (paraphrase, persona voice)**: "I don't need another number. I need the number I can say in both languages without checking my notes."

## Persona 2 — Omar, Division Lead, Store Operations (Arabic-first lead seat)

**Role**: leads Store Operations (12 branches, ~200 staff). He receives company objectives each quarter and must answer them with his own team's objectives — in Arabic for his branch managers, in English for the executive readout. He has filled out check-in spreadsheets for three different initiatives and abandoned each within weeks because nothing happened when he skipped one.
**Context**: Arabic-first reader; his English is functional but his judgment lives in Arabic; he writes objectives in Arabic and translates them upward himself today.
**JTBD**: "When the company sets its objectives, I want to translate them into objectives my branch managers actually own — in Arabic, with measurable key results I can defend — so my division's commitment is legible upward without me rewriting it twice."
**Triggers**: a company objective written in English that his managers misread twice before he re-explained it; a White Friday season where his stockout KR mattered and no one asked him for a number until it was over; check-in fatigue from tools that asked him everything and showed him nothing.
**Objections + responses**: "I'll write the objectives in Arabic and someone translates later" → the pair schema makes both sides first-class at authoring time, and the length-parity lint catches the lopsided draft (05/06); "if my team misses, this becomes a stick" → nothing in the product grades people; at-risk marks key results with their trigger named, and the archive of a mid-cycle objective needs a reason on the record (03 CP-4, 12 Q-9); "another Sunday form?" → one structured check-in per KR per period — value, confidence, blocker — and the dashboard shows him who else has not checked in, including his peers (03 CP-3).
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "Give me the objective in my language and I'll give you the number in yours."

## Persona 3 — Noura, E-commerce Lead (English-first, metric-fluent lead seat)

**Role**: leads the digital commerce division; grew up on funnel metrics; writes her own objectives in precise English ("raise checkout conversion from 2.1% to 3.0%") and regards most OKR tools as decorative. Her frustration is theater: check-ins that say "on track" with no number, and grading that arrives as a surprise at quarter end.
**Context**: English-first; her Arabic is conversational but she drafts business text in English; she needs the Arabic side of her objectives to exist because her merchandising team reads Arabic.
**JTBD**: "When I commit my team to a quarter, I want key results with real baselines and targets, check-ins that update the number I can audit, and a grade at close that is arithmetic — so the ritual produces evidence instead of adjectives."
**Triggers**: a previous OKR tool where progress was a self-reported percentage with no value trail; a grading meeting where the score was announced from a slide nobody could reconstruct; a quarter where "help/support" language in a KR let a dependent team claim credit for her outcome.
**Objections + responses**: "deterministic grading sounds rigid" → the formulas are published in-product and pinned at close (07c); re-baselining a broken target mid-cycle is a first-class, logged action (03 CP-4); "will the AI write my KRs?" → the assist proposes drafts she edits; the lint (task-shaped KRs, soft verbs) is always on, and everything runs with the assist off (08); "I don't want my team ranked" → no people analytics exists in the contract — there is no operation that ranks persons (07b, LOOP-10).
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "If the check-in doesn't change a number, it didn't happen."

## Persona 4 — Tariq, Key-Result Owner (thin participant seat, included)

**Role**: merchandising supervisor who owns two key results inside the e-commerce objective. He will never open the cascade board by choice; his entire relationship with the product is a due check-in, a value he knows, a confidence he feels, and a blocker he wants someone to hear. On his phone, between the floor and the stockroom.
**Context**: reads Arabic natively, English partially; wants the check-in form to render in the workspace locale he set once.
**JTBD**: "When my key result's check-in comes due, I want to post my current value and my honest confidence in under two minutes from wherever I am — so I've done my part without learning a whole platform."
**Triggers**: a weekly ritual email that links him into a desktop-only board he abandons; a blocker he raised verbally that never reached the meeting; being asked for "percent complete" when he only knows the count.
**Objections + responses**: "I don't know what confidence means" → the 1–5 scale carries anchored labels and the first check-in surfaces them inline (06 ConfidenceDial); "what happens if I miss one?" → the overdue zone is visible to leads, not silently fatal — and two consecutive misses is the derived at-risk trigger, honestly named (07c); "who sees my blocker?" → blockers surface on the cadence dashboard digest and the objective detail — the escalation path is the ritual itself, not a person-to-person score (03 CP-3).
**Verbatim (paraphrase)**: "Two minutes, one number, one honest gut-check — that's all I'm good for on a Monday."

## Voice derivation (SME-J)

Board voice: **operator-plain** — declarative, rhythm-forward, numbers carried in text (copy-voice §1). States what the cadence shows and what it does not: "3 of 9 key results have not checked in for period 2" is the register; adjectives carry evidence or are cut. Progress is never narrated hopefully. Arabic UI copy is فصحى بيضاء utility register. Fixed terminology (one concept, one term, everywhere): الهدف=objective · النتيجة الرئيسية=key result · دورة الأهداف=cycle · تحديث التقدم=check-in · مستوى الثقة=confidence · العائق=blocker · تسلسل الأهداف=cascade · المواءمة=alignment · درجة الإنجاز=grade · مساعد الصياغة=drafting assist · مساحة العمل=workspace. **Vocabulary boundary (LOOP-10 enforcement in copy)**: HR-appraisal terms (تقييم الموظفين-shaped phrases, "performance rating" idioms) never appear in either locale; the product grades objectives and key results, never people — the copy matrix (11) is scanned against this at review. Numerals Western per ledger row (progress, percentages, confidence digits, period indices — a data surface; storage canonical, display formatted per rtl-rules §3).
