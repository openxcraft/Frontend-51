# 00 — Brief: okr-cascade-board

Family: business_ops · Tier: 3 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A bilingual OKR cascade board for leadership teams in MENA: one cycle (a quarter), one tree of objectives — company objectives at the top, team objectives aligned beneath them, every objective carried by 3–5 measurable key results — and one check-in cadence that keeps the tree honest week after week. The board is the product: the cycle view renders the cascade as a first-class surface (company tier, team lanes, alignment links between them), each objective card rolling up the weighted progress of its key results, so a leadership team reads the state of the quarter in one screen — which objectives are moving, which are stalled, which carry low confidence, and where the tree itself is broken (a company ambition nobody has staffed, a team objective that answers to no company goal). The artifact grammar is orthodox OKR practice, deliberately: objectives are qualitative and bilingual, key results are numeric with a baseline and a target, and grading at cycle close is deterministic arithmetic the team can defend — never a model's opinion (01).

The first half of the unique feature is the **bilingual objective writing assistant**. In a Gulf leadership team the company objective is spoken in the room in two languages — the board meeting runs on English slides while the operating conversation runs in Arabic — and today that duality is resolved by whichever language the person who wrote the spreadsheet favored (01; research digest sources 38–40 document the side-by-side bilingual convention of Gulf business communication). This product makes the bilingual pair a first-class authored structure: every objective title, objective description, and key-result statement exists as an EN side and an AR side, each human-authored, each rendered at its own direction with bidi isolation, never machine-translated by the product itself (an empty side is an honest, visible state — the same parity discipline the portfolio's bilingual products enforce). The writing assistant is **conditionally-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1): when the drafting capability is bound and consented, it proposes objective and key-result drafts in *both* languages from a short focus brief the author types, lands every proposal through a review gate (accept / edit / dismiss — P15, draft-by-default), and when the capability is unbound, exhausted, or refused the product degrades to the **plain bilingual editor** — the same pair columns with a deterministic writing lint that is always on: task-shaped key results flagged, soft "help/support" verbs flagged, length budgets enforced, pair-length parity warned. The plain editor is the floor, not a stub — a full cycle, from company cascade to graded close, is authorable with the assist off (tested against the Mock Adapter's assist-off configuration).

The second half is the **check-in cadence** — the feature the OKR failure literature says is the actual difference between adoption and abandonment: "regular check-ins preferably weekly are essential to prevent slippage" (Doerr, quoted at research digest source 23) while failure post-mortems converge on rhythm, not framework, as the root cause (sources 2, 4, 5, 9). The cadence is a workspace-configurable ritual (weekly or bi-weekly — the cadence is genuinely contested in the practice literature, source 24, so it is a setting with a recommended default, not a doctrine): each key result has one named owner, each period opens a structured check-in — current measured value, confidence on an anchored 1–5 scale, blockers named in free text, help-needed flag — and the cadence dashboard shows the leadership team the truth of the ritual: who checked in on time, who is in the grace window, who is overdue, and which key results carry two consecutive misses or a confidence of 2 or below (the derived at-risk zone, computed by rule, never by sentiment). Check-in copy never fakes: an unsubmitted draft does not count as progress, an unanswered period renders as missing, and mid-cycle corrections keep their revision trail. The cadence produces the governance artifact the category's enterprise tier sells as a module: an exportable cycle package with the full check-in history and the deterministic grade sheet.

## Buyer

Leadership teams at MENA businesses running (or attempting, or restarting) quarterly OKR programs: the strategy/PMO lead who owns the quarterly ritual at a retail, logistics, services, or technology group; the department leads who translate company objectives into team objectives; and the key-result owners — a deliberately thin participant class who see exactly one surface (their due check-ins) and never need the full board. The economic buyer and the daily driver are usually the same seat at single-company scale (the strategy lead); at group scale the buyer is the transformation or PMO office and daily users are the leadership layer plus KR owners across departments. The regional demand signal is directional rather than audited (01 honest gaps): Gulf-focused OKR consultancies sell the implementation as engagements (research digest sources 27–28), MENA OKR implementation guides address alignment and governance explicitly (source 27), and the 2025 retirement of a major enterprise suite's OKR module — attributed publicly to limited adoption (sources 41–42) — left a migration pool with no first-party successor named (sources 41, 45). Seat economics follow the cadence, not the headcount: leadership seats carry the price; KR-owner participation is included, because taxing the check-in would tax the ritual the product exists to keep alive (12 Q-3). No mobile app at v1; the KR-owner check-in surface is responsive web reachable from a digest link.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary: cycles home, the cascade board, objective detail, the check-in desk, the cadence dashboard, the grading console), responsive to 360px in participant modes (fill one's own due check-ins, read one objective's progress). Zones: **cycles home** (cycle list with state and rollup, the first-run sample cycle offer, onboarding checklist P22); **the cascade board** — the core surface — company tier and team lanes with alignment links, per-objective progress and confidence rollups, alignment warnings (unstaffed ambition / orphaned effort) rendered in place; **objective detail** (the bilingual pair editor with the writing lint and the drafting-assist panel, key results with metric bars and owners, alignment links, the check-in history per key result); **check-in desk** (my due check-ins this period, the structured form); **cadence dashboard** (the KR × period coverage grid, overdue and at-risk zones, blocker digest); **grading console** (close gate, deterministic grade sheet, cycle export); jobs & activity console (T3-consolidated); settings (cadence, capability consents, usage meter, teams & members). ⌘K command palette (P05 — list surfaces exceed three). No performance-review surfaces of any kind (LOOP-10); no mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

Bilingual objective writing assistant with check-in cadence — defensible on three grounds (evidence in 01): (1) **the bilingual pair is a schema, not a locale setting** — EN/AR objective and key-result text is authored side-by-side per the documented side-by-side convention of Gulf business communication (academic: source 38, cited by 75; institutional guidance: source 40), while every mainstream OKR tool treats Arabic as a localization afterthought and none ships a bilingual *authoring* surface for the objective text itself; (2) **the cadence is the failure-mode answer** — the practice literature locates OKR failure in rhythm and transparency (sources 2, 4, 5, 9) and the canonical guidance demands regular check-ins (source 23), but the tools' check-in features are per-user dashboards, not a leadership-visible coverage surface with derived at-risk zones and a named overdue trail; (3) **deterministic grading and derived warnings make the board inspectable** — progress is computed from submitted check-in values by published formula (07c), at-risk is a rule with two named triggers, alignment warnings name the exact structural hole, and the cycle package exports the whole trail — the audit posture the enterprise tier bundles into strategy-execution suites, here as the product's default grammar (and its deliberate scope limit: no strategy mapping, no reviews, no feedback — LOOP-10).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No performance reviews** — the product never evaluates people: no review cycles, no ratings of persons, no calibration, no performance summaries, no HR-appraisal vocabulary in any locale (the Arabic copy discipline extends to refusing تقييم الموظفين-shaped terms, 11). Progress percentages belong to key results; confidence belongs to check-ins; nobody is graded but the objective.
- **No strategy mapping** — no balanced-scorecard perspectives, no causal strategy maps, no initiative portfolios, no roadmap canvases, no cross-cycle strategy diagrams. Alignment links connect objectives *within one cycle's cascade* — that is OKR alignment practice, not strategy mapping; anything wider is out.
- **No 360 feedback** — no peer feedback, no upward feedback, no praise/recognition features, no person profiles, no feedback requests. Blocker notes and check-in text are the only free-form communication, attached to key results, never to people.
- No task or project management — no task lists, no boards of work items, no execution tracking (meeting-notes-action-desk owns meeting-born actions; this product owns the outcome layer they serve).
- No KPI/analytics platform — the rollup is cycle-scoped objective arithmetic, not a metrics warehouse; historical metric series beyond the cycle's own check-in values are out of scope.
- No AI generation beyond the declared drafting assist; no auto-translation of bilingual pairs (either side empty is an honest state, never machine-filled); no AI-written check-ins, no AI confidence, no AI grading — grading is arithmetic.
- No individual (personal) objectives at v1 — the cascade is company and team level; personal objectives drift toward the performance boundary and are recorded as an open question with a recommended default of not shipped (12 Q-11), never a silent scope grab.

## Success signals (Mock-Adapter Definition-of-Done)

A strategy lead can, in one first session on the Mock Adapter with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI: create a cycle with a bilingual name and a weekly cadence; author a company objective as a bilingual pair and watch the deterministic lint flag a task-shaped key result and a soft verb, then fix both; request drafting proposals from the assist, accept one key-result proposal into the editor, edit one, dismiss one (P15 gate exercised); add a team objective, link it to two company objectives, and watch the cascade render with alignment; assign a key result to an owner with baseline, target, and unit; as that owner, open the check-in desk, save a draft, submit the check-in with a value, a confidence of 3, and a named blocker, and watch the key result's progress bar, the objective rollup, and the company-tier ring update with the Cascade Settle moment; watch the seeded overdue check-in and the low-confidence key result render as derived at-risk zones with their trigger named; open the cadence dashboard and read the coverage grid's missing zone; start the close gate on the seeded completed cycle, read the deterministic grade sheet with the aspirational 0.70 threshold explained, and export the cycle package before artifact expiry — and the entire flow must complete with the assist unbound and every abstract capability off: bilingual pairs typed by hand, lint rules firing from fixed tables, check-ins submitting, and direct-download exports (LOOP-5 bar).
