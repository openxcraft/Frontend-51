## What it is

A bilingual (ar/en) endowment registry for waqf administrators: one authoritative register of the endowments (أوقاف) a mutawlli or foundation administers — each waqf a record carrying its deed summary (donor, purpose, deed reference in both scripts), its asset register (the income-bearing corpus: buildings, cash placements, corporate shares), its beneficiary register (who the deed says benefits, with shares), and its distribution policy (administration cap, reserve, remainder rule) — plus the product's spine, the **beneficiary distribution ledger**: an append-only, sequence-numbered book of entries (inflows per asset, expenses by kind, distributions per beneficiary, reversals) in which every dirham of income has exactly one traceable path from the asset that earned it to the beneficiary who received it. A distribution is never typed in as an amount: the administrator records the period's inflows and expenses, runs the **allocation engine** — deterministic arithmetic over the deed's own policy (distributable = inflows − capped administration expenses − policy reserve; each beneficiary receives distributable × share; the rounding remainder lands in the deed's designated residual destination) — reviews the computed plan line by line (holds and reason-coded adjustments recorded, never silent), then records it, which commits the plan to the ledger as immutable entries with an attestation. Delivery of each beneficiary's amount is *recorded* against an abstract disbursement channel — the product never moves money.

The second face is the one no competitor ships at this shape: **transparency reports**. From the ledger's own state the product deterministically generates a periodic report — opening balance, inflows by asset, expenses by kind, distributions by beneficiary class, administration and distribution ratios, the unallocated remainder and its declared destination, variance against the prior period — as a bilingual artifact that is *sealed*: a sealed report is an immutable snapshot pinned to the ledger state it was computed from, with its generation and sealing logged, and exportable as a bilingual document, a data file, or a print bundle. The beneficiary statement — the same arithmetic pointed at one beneficiary's ledger history — is a second report kind, the artifact that answers "where is my share?" without a meeting. The AI stance is **declared non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1, 07a): allocation is arithmetic over recorded entries and policy shares, ratios are division, the seal is a state machine, and the same ledger state always yields the same report — which is exactly what a trust-facing artifact requires. No AI capability is bound anywhere, no workflow degrades when AI is absent, and no sparkle glyph appears in any surface (05).

The buyer's reality (01 evidence): a family waqf is administered by a retired professional in spreadsheets and messaging threads, answering to relatives once a year with a hand-built table; a foundation's operations desk runs dozens of endowments whose income, expenses, and distributions live in an accounting tool built for businesses, not for deed-bound purposes — and the quarterly transparency report is assembled by copy-paste under deadline. The category that exists is either enterprise waqf suites priced and shaped for awqaf authorities (01), government portals that serve the regulator's registry rather than the administrator's ledger, or generic accounting software with no concept of a deed, a share, or a sealed report. This product is the administrator's instrument: the register first, the ledger as the single source of truth, the report as the receipt the deed's beneficiaries and the board can actually read.

## Buyer

Waqf administrators in MENA and waqf-holding communities — two shapes. (1) The **family-waqf mutawlli**: an individual administrator (often the founder's descendant, occasionally a professional trustee) running 1–5 endowments with a handful to a few dozen beneficiaries, Arabic-first, answering to the family and to the deed's charitable end-beneficiary. (2) The **waqf foundation operations desk**: 2–6 administrative seats at a private awqf foundation or endowment-holding institution managing 20–100+ endowments, bilingual, producing periodic reports for a supervisory board, external auditors, and (where applicable) the jurisdiction's awqaf authority (market context, 01). The economic buyer is the mutawlli or the foundation's operations/finance lead who answers when a beneficiary asks for an accounting, when the board asks why the administration ratio moved, or when an auditor asks for the distribution trail; daily users are administrators (record entries, run allocations, record deliveries), the board treasurer (reads and seals-facing reports, viewer seat), and external auditors (read-only export access, 02 Yasmin). Beneficiary-facing delivery is export-artifact-only at v1 — no beneficiary self-service portal (12 Q-8). Per-seat for administrator seats; viewer seats free (12 Q-3).

## Unique feature

**Beneficiary distribution ledger with transparency reports**, defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the ledger is append-only with per-entry sequence numbers, reason-coded reversals instead of edits, and evidence references — the accounting convention auditors already trust, productized for waqf (an endowment's records must outlive its administrator); (2) the distribution is **computed, not typed** — deterministic allocation over the deed's recorded policy, so the same ledger state always produces the same plan and the same report, and every figure in the transparency report traces to ledger entries a reader can walk (the derivation is disclosed in the worksheet, never hidden in a formula bar); (3) the transparency report is a **sealed bilingual artifact** — immutable, snapshot-pinned, ratio-bearing, variance-bearing — the artifact the category's dashboards and exports are structurally unable to produce because they never modeled the deed's own terms as data; (4) the beneficiary statement is the trust instrument — one beneficiary's full ledger history in one artifact, which turns the annual "where is my share?" conversation into a document. No retrieved competitor binds beneficiary shares, deed policy, an append-only distribution ledger, and sealed reports into one product at administrator price points (01 positioning gaps).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- **No payment processing** — the product never moves money, holds no funds, and touches no payment rails. Distributions are *recorded* as ledger entries; delivery of each beneficiary's amount is recorded against an abstract disbursement channel (08 N2) whose manual alternative — record the delivery by hand with an evidence note and date — is the primary path. The money path belongs to banks, cashier tools, and the administrator's own hands; the ledger records what happened, it never executes it.
- **No investment management** — assets are registered with their income profile as descriptive metadata (rental property, cash placement, corporate share); there is no portfolio view, no return tracking, no performance attribution, no investment decision support. Endowment asset management belongs to investment desks and asset managers; the registry records the income an asset produced, not a strategy for producing more.
- **No sharia board tooling** — no fatwa management, no sharia-compliance review workflows, no fiqh adjudication of any kind. The deed's terms are recorded as data (shares, caps, remainder rule) exactly as the deed states them; interpreting, contesting, or validating those terms against fiqh is a scholar's act performed outside this product. The allocation engine applies the recorded policy; it never rules on it (the honesty note renders on the policy editor, 11).
- No general accounting / ERP: no double-entry books of the institution, no payroll, no inter-account reconciliation — the ledger is the endowment distribution ledger only (inflows, expenses, distributions, reversals per endowment).
- No donor/CRM fundraising: no campaigns, no donor profiles, no zakat or sadaqah collection (zakat workflows are zakat-calculation-suite's family — LOOP-3 mirror in 12).
- No beneficiary self-service portal at v1 — statements and reports reach beneficiaries as export artifacts through the delivery channel or by hand; a portal is a LOOP-4 owner decision, never a silent default (12 Q-8).
- No property/facility management: no lease management, no maintenance workflows, no tenant portals — an asset's income arrives as a recorded inflow with evidence; the renting of it happens elsewhere.
- No AI anywhere (declared non-AI): no anomaly detection, no forecasting, no "smart" suggestions — the engine is arithmetic, and it says so (the derivation disclosure is the product's honesty surface, 03 CP-3).
