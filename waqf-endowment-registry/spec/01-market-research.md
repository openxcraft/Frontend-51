## Category overview

Three shapes of product currently touch the waqf administrator's desk, and none of them is the administrator's instrument. (1) **Enterprise waqf management suites** (Fiduciary International's class [2]) automate the complete lifecycle — registration, Sharia review of the hujja, investment management, revenue collection, distribution to Massarif, disbursement workflows — shaped and priced for awqaf organizations and authorities running portfolios at institutional scale. (2) **Donor-side digital waqf platforms** (Awqef's class [4]) are fintech products that create and grow new endowments through investment portfolios, serving the donor, not the administrator of an existing deed. (3) **Government registry portals** (UMEED/WAMSI in India [6][7]; the Saudi General Authority for Awqaf's own services [8]) serve the regulator's registration and supervision mandate — the certificate, the national number, the board's oversight — not the mutawlli's monthly ledger. Around them sits the reality the vendors themselves market against: "much of it still operates on spreadsheets, disconnected accounting systems, and paper records" [9], with institutions running "three or four disconnected platforms simultaneously" because "no global property management product is built around UAE waqf governance structures" and generic accounting systems "fail to produce AMAF-format reporting" [9].

The gap is not digitization — digitization is in active procurement (Sharjah's Nazer Al Waqf platform, January 2025; the Saudi authority's digital partnership initiative, April 2025 [9]). The gap is the **deed-bound distribution ledger at administrator scale**: every retrieved waqf-aware product either binds the deed's conditions into an enterprise machine (Fiduciary's Khareeta Al-Massarif distribution map [2]; FEKRA's condition-driven auto-distribution with Nazir pre-permission [3]) or leaves the deed out entirely (donor platforms [4]; generic accounting [9]). None of them documents, on any retrieved page, the three things this product binds as one: **canonical beneficiary shares as deed fractions**, an **append-only, sequence-numbered distribution ledger** whose arithmetic is disclosed line by line, and a **sealed, immutable transparency report** — the artifact accountability literature names as the governance driver [10] and the convention regulators already publish at authority scale [8].

## Demand evidence (with sources)

- **Deed conditions are the binding constraint buyers cannot currently honor cleanly**: "Every distribution must comply with the conditions written into the original waqf deed, and those conditions differ across assets, donor types, and beneficiary categories" — which is precisely why "generic property management platforms fail" and accounting exports get reconciled by hand [9]. FEKRA and Fiduciary both market deed-condition-driven distribution as their differentiator [3][2] — the demand is the category's own marketing.
- **Transparency reporting is the named pain, not a nice-to-have**: institutions compile regulator/board reports "manually from accounting software, tenancy records, and spreadsheet-based beneficiary databases" — "a monthly administrative project" [9]; the smart-waqf governance literature names transparency and accountability as the drivers of waqf digitalization [10]; the Saudi authority itself publishes disbursement-channel totals as its public accountability shape [8].
- **The audit-trail convention is already established in-category**: "every transaction, document modification, approval action, and beneficiary update must generate a permanent audit log" [9]; Fiduciary markets "document management with full audit trail" [2]. This product's append-only ledger with per-entry sequence numbers is that convention, made the spine instead of a module.
- **The buyer universe is quantified at the regulator layer (source-asserted)**: more than 11,000 endowments registered with the Saudi authority, 30,000+ registration services processed [8] — every registration a deed with terms an administrator somewhere must execute; India's WAMSI portal spans every state waqf board [7]. No administrator-seat count exists in any retrieved source (honest gap — see below).
- **GCC waqf digitization is in active procurement**: Sharjah Awqaf's Nazer Al Waqf (Jan 2025), the Saudi authority's digital partnership initiative (Apr 2025) [9] — budget lines exist, which is exactly when administrator-side tools get bought.
- **Beneficiary accountability has civil-society teeth**: India's Waqf Liaison Forum (judges, ex-waqf-board CEOs, civil servants) exists to pressure administrators' record-keeping within the Waqf Act 1995/2013 frame [7]; a beneficiary statement that answers "where is my share?" is the artifact that pressure demands.
- **Bilingual + dual-calendar is table stakes in the direct field** [2][3] — not a differentiator here, a floor this product meets by construction (05).

## Pricing landscape

The direct field publishes **no prices at all**: Awqaf.online is freemium with a free-start workspace and a 404 where its pricing page should be [1]; FEKRA sells through a buy-now catalog with no listed price on the product page [3]; Fiduciary and the enterprise class are contact-only [2]; Awqef's "1000 SAR" is a minimum waqf principal, not a seat price [4]. Adjacent grant/foundation SaaS is custom-quote at enterprise posture (Submittable, Fluxx, Blackbaud [12][16][15]). What the landscape supports: (a) a **free floor exists** (Open Waqf at zero [5]; Awqaf.online's free workspace [1]) — so the paid product must be paid for what free cannot do: sealed artifacts, the append-only ledger, deed-share arithmetic; (b) per-seat SaaS for administrative teams is the shape foundation/grant tools already sell [12][16]. Our recommended posture (12 Q-3): **per-seat for administrator seats, viewer seats free, everything unmetered except export/import windows** — sized (unverified estimate, no source anchors this band) in the two-digit-to-low-three-digit USD-per-administrator-seat/month range typical of specialized vertical SaaS at this depth; the registry's recording surface is never metered by design (a ledger that rations recording is a rejected spec, 06).

## Positioning statement

For waqf administrators — the family mutawlli and the foundation operations desk — waqf-endowment-registry is the endowment registry that turns the deed's own terms into data and the period's income into a defensible accounting: canonical beneficiary shares as fractions, an append-only distribution ledger where every entry traces from the asset that earned it to the beneficiary who received it, and sealed bilingual transparency reports pinned to the ledger state they were computed from. Unlike enterprise waqf suites, it is the administrator's instrument, not the authority's machine — no investment desks, no Sharia review workflows, priced at seats an individual mutawlli can carry. Unlike donor platforms, it administers the endowments that already exist. Unlike government portals, its register serves the deed's beneficiaries, not the regulator's filing. And unlike the spreadsheet status quo, the same ledger state always yields the same report — the receipt a beneficiary, a board, and an auditor can all walk without a meeting.

## Positioning gaps (why the wedge is open)

1. **Share canonicality**: no retrieved direct competitor documents beneficiary shares as canonical deed fractions with a sum-gate against the deed's whole; the closest is FEKRA's condition-driven auto-distribution [3] — automatic, not reviewable line-by-line with holds and reason-coded adjustments.
2. **The sealed artifact**: dashboards and "interactive real-time reporting" [2] are live views; a sealed report is an immutable snapshot with a ledger pin — the trust shape regulators use and dashboards structurally cannot produce (a dashboard recomputes; a seal does not).
3. **Determinism as a feature**: no retrieved competitor markets same-state-same-report as the product's honesty surface; the field's innovation energy goes to blockchain immutability talk [9][10] — this product delivers ledger-immutability semantics (append-only, sequence-numbered, reversal-not-edit) without the ledger-reimplementation detour.
4. **The beneficiary statement**: nothing retrieved produces one beneficiary's full ledger history as a standalone bilingual artifact — the instrument that de-escalates the annual "where is my share?" conversation (02).

## Complaints mined (paraphrased patterns — no verbatim quotes exist in the corpus)

- Administrator-side (paraphrased from vendor status-quo marketing [3][9]): reports assembled by copy-paste under deadline; income reconciled across an accounting tool, property records, and a beneficiary spreadsheet; every regulator/board request restarts the manual compilation.
- Governance-side (paraphrased from the smart-waqf literature [10]): opacity and outdated administration erode beneficiary and public trust; digitization is demanded specifically for accountability, not just efficiency.
- Civil-society-side (paraphrased from WLF's positioning [7]): administrators' records are the object of external pressure and litigation; the demand is for defensible, inspectable history.

## Market size (honest)

The buyer universe is administrator seats: individual mutawllis (1–5 endowments each) plus foundation operations desks (2–6 seats across dozens of endowments) in MENA and waqf-holding communities. The only retrieved quantifications are regulator-layer and sector-layer figures — 11,000+ registered endowments in one jurisdiction (source-asserted [8]), a "$1 trillion" sector-wide asset claim without method [9] — neither converts to an auditable administrator-seat TAM, so none is offered. Envelope: per-seat vertical SaaS with free viewer seats and unmetered recording; the demand evidence above (deed-binding failures, manual reporting, active GCC procurement) is qualitative and sufficient for a T3 positioning decision.
