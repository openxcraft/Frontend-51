# 00 — Brief: attendance-shift-planner

Family: business_ops · Tier: 2 · Roster status: researched proposal, pending owner confirmation · Seed: none supplied.

## What it is

A grid shift planner for frontline operations teams: the ops manager authors the working week — workers down the rows, days across the columns, shift blocks in the cells — and the product keeps that week inside the law. The planning surface is a desktop-first grid (workers × days) with a week navigator (prev/next/next-unplanned, dual Hijri+Gregorian dates, Ramadan-aware), a roster rail listing each worker's role, qualifications, and contract caps, and a violation inspector that lists every rule the current draft breaks. The grid is the product: shift blocks are placed, moved, stretched, and removed directly in cells; every mutation re-runs the labor-rule engine and the inspector names what changed. Mobile and narrow viewports get honest *review* surfaces — the published week per worker, the day's coverage, violation summaries — not a degraded authoring experience.

The unique feature is **labor-rule guardrails per jurisdiction with swap-aware scheduling**, and it is deliberately a rules engine, not an oracle: the product is declared **non-AI** (ai-native-patterns §1) — the engine is deterministic arithmetic over a configured, versioned **JurisdictionRuleset** (working-time caps, minimum rest between shifts, maximum consecutive days, overtime thresholds, night-work windows, Ramadan reduced-hours windows). Two stock rulesets ship at v1 — **KSA** and **UAE** — captured from public labor-law sources (01: HRSD official text; Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 guides) with capture dates and a "verify against current law" honesty note, plus a fully editable **custom** ruleset. Every violation **names the rule it broke, the measured value, and the required value** ("Rest period: 11h required by KSA v1 — 7h 30m provided between closing Sun 23:00 and opening Mon 06:30"), because a guardrail that says only "scheduling conflict" is a guardrail nobody trusts. Blocking violations refuse publication through a gate that lists its blockers; warnings (overtime thresholds, night-window marks) travel with the plan into the publication record. Waivers exist, are recorded with actor, reason, and timestamp, and are exported — the audit posture the category's compliance tier sells as an enterprise feature, here as the default grammar of the tool.

Swap-awareness is the second half of the feature and the boundary is exact: this product owns **plan creation and the authoritative schedule**; the co-batched T1 flagship **shift-swap-marketplace** owns swap *negotiation* and fairness-ranked matching (LOOP-3, mirrored in both products' 12-open-questions). This planner publishes the week through a schedule export channel (downloadable artifact, marketplace-compatible format — abstract capability, LOOP-5), and when an already-agreed swap comes back — pasted from the marketplace, imported from a change channel when bound, or recorded manually by the supervisor who approved it over the phone — the planner applies it to the published plan, re-runs every guardrail for **both** affected workers (a swap that fixes coverage can break a rest rule for the receiving worker), and refuses the amendment until new blockers are cleared or waived. Every swap-in change carries provenance (from-worker, to-worker, agreed-at, source) in the change log. The adjacency boundary is the portfolio's cleanest: plan creation vs plan renegotiation.

The category gap is structural. Global scheduling platforms bundle the grid with time clocks, payroll, team chat, and AI demand forecasting (01: Deputy, When I Work, Connecteam, Quinyx, WorkJam, Skedulo, 7shifts, Humanity) — per-user or per-location pricing aimed at full-suite adoption, with compliance pitched as an enterprise afterthought ("compliance-focused scheduling" with "no free plan and unclear pricing", 01). The regional failure modes are precisely what the bundle does not model honestly: Ramadan's statutory reduced working hours (a dated, jurisdiction-specific cap change that reshapes every frontline week in the Gulf twice over — reduction *and* the demand surge), rest-period failures like clopening, overtime thresholds that differ by jurisdiction (150% KSA vs 125%/150%-night UAE classes), and qualification-gated shifts. A bilingual ar/en, jurisdiction-first planner whose differentiator is *inspectable rules with named violations* — priced for the ops-manager seat, not the enterprise roll-out — is unoccupied in the MENA set (01 positioning gaps).

## Buyer

Frontline operations managers in MENA retail chains, F&B groups, logistics hubs, and facilities-services companies — the person who builds next week's roster for a 20–60 worker team (often several teams/branches), owns coverage when someone doesn't show, and answers for labor-rule breaches when labor inspection or HR asks. Economic buyer and daily user are usually the same seat at single-site; at multi-branch groups the buyer is the ops lead and the daily users are branch managers plus a shift-supervisor layer that records agreed swaps and reads the published plan. Secondary: HR/compliance officers who audit violations, waivers, and publication records, and need the export artifacts. The worker side is deliberately thin at v1: published-plan *review* views only (worker's week, day coverage) — no worker self-service, no open-shift claiming (marketplace/communication-suite territory, LOOP-10). Full personas in 02-user-research.md.

## Surface

Web application, desktop-first (≥1280px primary authoring; responsive to 360px in **review** modes — grid authoring is desktop/tablet only). Zones: **week navigator + ruleset indicator** (team selector, week stepper, Hijri/Gregorian dates, active ruleset badge, Ramadan banner when the week intersects the configured window); **roster rail** on the inline-start edge (virtualized worker list with role, qualification chips, contract caps, day-off marks); **the planning grid** center (workers × 7 days, virtualized rows, one-to-many shift blocks per cell, drag with keyboard equivalent, inline shift editing); **violation inspector** on the inline-end edge (blocking first then warnings, grouped per worker and per rule, jump-to-cell links, waive-with-reason from the row). Supporting screens: teams & roster management, qualification library, ruleset library (inspectable rules with source notes and version history), publication & export (gate, change log, artifacts), swap log (change provenance), jobs center, settings. ⌘K command palette with bilingual normalized search (P05, T2 mandate). No mobile app; no browser extension; no public API at v1.

## Unique feature

**Jurisdiction rulesets as inspectable guardrails with swap-aware scheduling** — defensible on four grounds (evidence in 01): (1) the rules are *knowable arithmetic* — KSA 8h/day / 48h/week with a 150% overtime class and Ramadan reduction to 6h/36h for fasting Muslim workers; UAE 8h/day / 48h/week with 125% overtime, a 150% night window (21:00–04:00 class), a 2h/day overtime cap, and a 2-hour Ramadan reduction — all captured with sources and dates, none hallucinated, none requiring a model; (2) every violation names its rule with measured-vs-required values and the ruleset version it was computed against — the same inspectability that made QA-checker loyalists trust term severity (category evidence, 01) applied to labor rules; (3) the publication gate refuses blocking violations and exports the resolution ledger (waivers with reasons) — the fair-workweek/predictive-scheduling compliance posture (US DOL Fact Sheet #56B class, 01) rendered as product grammar rather than an enterprise module; (4) swap-aware re-validation closes the loop the suites leave open: the schedule stays guarded *after* publication, when real-world swaps threaten it most, without ever owning the negotiation (shift-swap-marketplace's boundary, LOOP-3).

## Exclusions (LOOP-10)

- No payroll processing — the engine flags overtime and rule breaches; it never computes pay, deductions, or end-of-service amounts. Overtime thresholds are planning flags, not wage statements.
- No swap negotiation — no swap board, no matching, no fairness ranking, no worker-to-worker requests (shift-swap-marketplace, T1 flagship, owns negotiation; this product records *agreed* swaps and re-validates; adjacency mirrored in both 12s).
- No clock-in hardware or time-and-attendance capture — no biometric devices, no geofenced clock-ins, no punch data. Planned minutes are the product's truth; attendance truth belongs to other systems.
- No AI demand forecasting, no auto-scheduling, no labor optimization — declared non-AI (ai-native-patterns §1); the manager authors, the rules check. Competitors' forecasting posture is the explicit counter-position (01).
- No team chat, no task management, no HR suite (onboarding, leave-policy administration beyond simple absence windows), no performance tracking.
- No marketplace of temp workers, no vendor routing, no payroll-provider connectors at v1 (abstract capabilities only, 08).

## Success signals (definition of done vs Mock Adapter)

An ops manager can: create a team and roster (workers with bilingual names, roles, qualifications, contract caps); select the KSA ruleset and read its rules and source note; author one full week in the grid (place, move, stretch, remove shift blocks; keyboard-complete); watch the violation inspector name planted rule breaks — a clopening rest-gap, a weekly overtime threshold, a missing qualification — with measured-vs-required values; fix one by editing the grid and watch it close; waive one with a reason; hit the publication gate, be refused with named blockers, clear them, and publish; export the schedule artifact (grid + worker views + marketplace-compatible format) and retrieve it before expiry; record an external swap against the published plan, watch the re-validation surface a *new* rest violation for the receiving worker, resolve it, and publish the amendment with provenance — all in the first session, on the Mock Adapter, with zero provider-specific knowledge anywhere in the UI and the full flow completing with every abstract capability unbound.
